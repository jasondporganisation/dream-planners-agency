# Supporting a client through a claim

A claim is the moment the relationship is really tested. Clients remember how we made them feel when something went wrong far longer than anything we said when things were fine.

This guide is **generic**. It does not describe any insurer's claims process, forms or timelines. For those, always follow the insurer's current official process and tell the client to rely on it. Message templates: M31–M36 in [`messages.md`](messages.md).

---

## Five principles

1. **Person first, paperwork second.** Ask how they are before you ask what happened.
2. **Never predict the outcome.** Don't say "sure can claim", "you're covered", "no problem" or "should be fine". The insurer assesses claims under the policy terms. What you *can* promise is your own effort: "I'll help you get everything in, and I'll keep you updated every week."
3. **Respond the same day.** Call within a few hours if you can. If you can't, send M33 and give a time you'll call.
4. **Own the follow-up.** The client should never have to chase you. Weekly updates until the claim is closed, even if the update is "no news yet".
5. **Protect their data.** Medical reports, NRIC and bank details never go over WhatsApp or personal email. Use the insurer's secure channels (PDPA protection obligation, [PDPC, PDPA overview](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/overview-of-pdpa/the-legislation/personal-data-protection-act)).

## The emotional side

### Recognise where they are
People going through a claim are often dealing with illness, injury, a death or a disrupted life. Expect:
- **Shock or overwhelm:** keep instructions short. One step at a time, in writing afterwards.
- **Anxiety about money:** acknowledge it ("I can hear this is worrying you"), but don't reassure them about amounts or outcomes.
- **Anger or frustration** (at the situation, the hospital, the insurer, you): don't take it personally or get defensive. Let them finish, then ask what would help most right now.
- **Grief:** after a death, the family may not be ready to think about paperwork for days or weeks. Follow their pace.

### What to say
- "I'm so sorry this is happening. How are you holding up?"
- "You don't have to figure this out alone. I'll walk you through it."
- "Let's take it one step at a time. Today, the only thing we need is…"
- "I don't know the answer to that yet, but I'll find out and come back to you by [day]."
- "No need to reply. I'll check in again on [day]."

### What not to say
- "Don't worry, sure can claim." (a promise you can't keep)
- "At least…" (minimises their experience)
- "You should have…" (blame)
- "That's not my department." (abandonment; find out who it is and connect them)
- Anything about other products, reviews or referrals. **No selling during a claim, and no referral asks, ever.**

### After a death
- Condolences first (M32). Attend the wake if appropriate and welcome. **Don't bring up policies at the wake.**
- When the family reaches out, or after a respectful interval, offer to help: "Whenever you're ready, I can help you understand what paperwork may be involved. There's no rush."
- Speak to the person who has the legal standing to deal with the estate or the policy. Confirm who that is through the insurer's process before you discuss any details with them (PDPA: disclose personal data only where permitted).
- Point the family to official guidance on next steps after a death, CPF nominations and estates (e.g. [MoneySense, Intestacy](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/intestacy-what-if-you-don-t-have-an-estate-plan/), [CPF Board, CPF nomination](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/account-services/providing-for-your-loved-ones/making-a-cpf-nomination)). Suggest a lawyer for estate matters.

## The practical side

### Step 1 — First contact (same day)
- [ ] Call. Listen. Ask how they are.
- [ ] Understand, in their words: what happened, when, who is affected, and where they are now (hospital, home).
- [ ] Ask whether there is anything urgent: a hospital asking about payment, a deadline someone mentioned.
- [ ] Tell them what happens next and when you'll next be in touch.
- [ ] Send M33 or M34 as a written recap.
- [ ] Log it in the tracker: `claim_open = Y`, `next_action`, `next_action_date` (no medical details in the tracker).

### Step 2 — Get the official process in front of them
- [ ] Check the insurer's **current** official claims process yourself. Don't rely on memory, because processes change.
- [ ] Give the client the official instructions or link, and explain them in plain words.
- [ ] Help them put together a document checklist from the official requirements. A shared checklist helps (see below).
- [ ] Explain the secure way to submit documents.

### Step 3 — Help with the paperwork
- [ ] Offer to go through forms with them, by phone, video or in person. Forms are stressful for someone who is unwell or grieving.
- [ ] The client (or the person with legal standing) completes and signs their own forms. Don't fill in their declarations or answer health questions for them.
- [ ] Check everything is complete before submission. Missing documents often cause delays.
- [ ] Keep a record of what was submitted and when.

### Step 4 — Follow up every week until closed
- [ ] Weekly update (M35), even if there's no news.
- [ ] If more documents are requested, explain why (if known) and help them get them.
- [ ] If there's a delay, find out the reason and an expected next step. Don't speculate.
- [ ] Ask how they are, not just about the claim.

### Step 5 — When the claim closes
- [ ] Contact them personally, whatever the outcome (M36).
- [ ] **If the outcome is not what they hoped:** acknowledge their disappointment. Make sure they have the insurer's written explanation, and explain their options:
  - the insurer's internal complaint or appeal process (escalate on the same day if they want to);
  - if they remain dissatisfied, eligible disputes can go to the **Financial Industry Disputes Resolution Centre (FIDReC)** ([fidrec.com.sg](https://www.fidrec.com.sg/)).
  Never discourage a complaint. Handling complaints independently, effectively and promptly is one of MAS's fair dealing outcomes ([MAS Guidelines on Fair Dealing, PDF](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/fair-dealing-guidelines-30-may-2024.pdf), Outcome 5).
- [ ] Update the tracker: `claim_open = N`. Set a gentle check-in 4–6 weeks later (a personal message, not a template).
- [ ] **Don't** use the claim's close to start a review, sales conversation or referral ask. Let the normal cadence resume.

## Generic claim support checklist (to share with the client)

Copy into a message or document and fill it in from the insurer's **official** requirements. Don't add requirements from memory.

```
Claim support checklist — prepared by [Adviser name] on [date]

What happened (in your words): ______________________
Date of event: __________

Documents the official process asks for:
[ ] ______________________   where to get it: __________
[ ] ______________________   where to get it: __________
[ ] ______________________   where to get it: __________

How to submit securely: ______________________
(Please don't send ID, medical or bank documents over WhatsApp.)

What I'll do: ______________________  by: ________
Next update from me: ________
My number: ________
```

## Healthcare-related claims: general pointers

These are general, public pointers only, not insurer-specific:
- Before a planned hospital stay, it is worth asking the hospital for a bill estimate. MoneySense also suggests asking the insurer whether a letter of guarantee is available ([MoneySense, Health insurance terms](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/health-insurance-terms-know-what-you-re-paying-for/)).
- Holding more than one medical policy doesn't let you claim more than 100% of actual medical expenses ([MoneySense, Health insurance terms](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/health-insurance-terms-know-what-you-re-paying-for/)).
- For how national schemes interact with private cover, see CPF Board's MediShield Life pages ([cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/healthcare-financing/medishield-life)).

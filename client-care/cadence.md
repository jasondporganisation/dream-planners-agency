# Client-care cadence: 12 months per client

**Purpose:** no existing client goes cold. Every client hears from us at least six times a year, and none of those messages is a sales message.
**Owner:** Xavier (client experience). **Approver:** Jason reviews every outgoing message before it goes out. These are templates, not sends.
**Scope:** generic. No client data, insurer names or products in this folder. Real client details go only into the team's approved, access-controlled systems (see [PDPA notes](#pdpa-guardrails-for-every-touchpoint)).

Message wording is in [`messages.md`](messages.md); the template IDs (M01–M40) are listed against each touchpoint below. Log every touch in [`tracker-template.csv`](tracker-template.csv).

---

## The rule of thumb

> **Give, don't ask.** Every touchpoint should leave the client with something (warmth, a reminder that helps them, a quick answer) and should ask nothing of them. The only regular "ask" in the year is the annual-review invitation, and that is about their life, not products.

What counts as "cold": **no two-way contact in 120 days.** A message with no reply doesn't count as two-way. When a client hits 120 days, the tracker flags them and the adviser makes a personal call or voice note (not a template).

## The calendar

"Month 0" is the month the policy was issued. Festive and birthday touches fall wherever they land in the year.

| Day 7 | "Did everything arrive?" check | WhatsApp | M06 | No |

| Month 6 | **Half-year hello**: "how's life?" A personal check-in from the adviser | WhatsApp voice note or call | M23–M24 | Yes |
| Any time | **Life events**: new job, marriage, new baby, new home, child starting school, retirement, bereavement, illness | Whatever the client used to tell us | M25–M32 | **All yes** |
| Any time | **Claims support**: the client or family tells us something has happened | Call first, then message | M33–M36 | **All yes** |
| Month 10–11 | **Annual-review invitation**: about their year and their family | WhatsApp, then email confirmation | M37–M39 | M37 yes |
| Month 12 | **Annual review meeting** (30 min, see [`annual-review-agenda.md`](annual-review-agenda.md)) and thank-you afterwards | Meeting, then WhatsApp | M40 | Yes |
| 120 days without two-way contact | **Re-warm**: personal call or voice note from the adviser | Call | none (personal) | Yes |

**Minimum year:** welcome, 30-day check, birthday, at least one festive greeting, half-year hello, annual review. That is six touches. Life events and claims come on top.

**Maximum:** no more than **two unprompted messages a month** to any client, festive greetings included. If CNY and a birthday fall in the same week, combine them or drop the lighter one.

## Touchpoint guide: what's appropriate and what isn't

### 1. Welcome after the policy is issued
- **Do:** thank them for their trust. Tell them what will arrive and when, and how to reach the adviser. Suggest they tell one family member where the documents are kept.
- **Don't:** mention other products ("now that you have X, let's look at Y"), ask for referrals in the welcome message, or describe benefits in your own words. The documents are the authority; point to them.

### 2. Day 7 and 30-day check
- **Do:** ask whether everything arrived and whether anything in the documents was unclear. If the policy has a free-look period, remind them it is described in their documents. Don't restate the terms yourself.
- **Don't:** treat this as a "top-up" opportunity. If the client raises a new need, note it and handle it properly through a fact-find at a separate meeting, not over chat.

### 3. Birthday
- **Do:** send a short, personal greeting on the day, before noon. A detail from your last conversation (a trip, a hobby, a grandchild) makes it real.
- **Don't:** attach anything, add a "by the way", mention age-related premium changes, or send a mass-broadcast greeting with the name merged in. If you have nothing personal to add, keep it short and sincere (M11).
- **Note:** for clients who don't celebrate birthdays (some religious practices), skip it. Record that in the tracker.

### 4. Festive seasons
Singapore's gazetted public holidays include Chinese New Year, Hari Raya Puasa, Hari Raya Haji, Deepavali and Christmas. Check each year's dates on the [MOM public holidays page](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/public-holidays) when you set up the calendar.

| Festival | Timing of message | Appropriate | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chinese New Year** | Eve or day 1–2 (15 days in total; a greeting any time up to Chap Goh Mei is fine) | Warm wishes for health and togetherness | "Huat"/wealth puns tied to money or returns; red packets or gifts linked to any policy; **greetings to a family in mourning** (many Chinese families who lost someone in the past year don't celebrate; send a quiet "thinking of you" instead, or nothing) |
| **Hari Raya Puasa** | On the day, or "Selamat Hari Raya" in the following weeks (it is celebrated for a month) | "Selamat Hari Raya Aidilfitri, maaf zahir dan batin" if you know the client is comfortable with it | Lunch-meeting invites during Ramadan; pushing for meetings in the last ten days of the fast; assuming every Malay client is Muslim or every Muslim client is Malay |
| **Hari Raya Haji** | On the day | A simple greeting | Long messages; it is a quieter, religious occasion |
| **Deepavali** | Eve or day | "Happy Deepavali", light and warm | Assuming every Indian client celebrates (many are Christian, Muslim, Sikh and so on) |
| **Christmas** | 24–25 Dec, or a "season's greetings" from mid-December | Season's greetings; "Merry Christmas" for clients you know celebrate | Combining it with "year-end planning" or tax-relief nudges |
| **New Year (1 Jan)** | Optional; only if no other festive greeting went out in the last month | Short "wishing you a good year ahead" | Resolutions framed around money |

**General festive rules**
- Only greet clients for festivals you know they celebrate. Record each client's festivals in the tracker (`festivals` column) after asking them, or from what they told you. Don't infer them from name or race.
- Festive messages are greetings only. No "while I'm here…", no links, no product images, no company-branded e-cards unless the insurer has approved them.
- Gifts: follow your insurer's gifts-and-entertainment policy. A gift must never depend on buying, renewing or referring.

### 5. Half-year hello
- **Do:** a personal voice note or call. "How's everyone? How was [thing they mentioned]?" This is the touch that stops relationships drifting.
- **Don't:** template it word for word. M23–M24 are starting points only.

### 6. Life events
Clients tell us about life events, or we see them because they choose to share (e.g. they post publicly and we're connected). Respond to what the client tells you. **Don't monitor clients' social media to find "triggers".**

| Event | Appropriate | Not appropriate |
|---|---|---|
| Marriage, new baby, new home, new job | Congratulations, a small personal note. You can offer a review later: "when things settle, happy to look at whether your paperwork or nominations need updating, no rush." | Messaging about cover in the congratulations message itself; turning up with a proposal |
| Child starting school, graduating | Congratulations | Education-planning pitches |
| Retirement | Congratulations; ask how they'd like to celebrate | Product talk in the same message |
| Illness or hospitalisation | Concern, an offer of practical help, "no need to reply" | Asking about claims in the first message unless they raised it; switch to [`claims-support.md`](claims-support.md) when they're ready |
| Bereavement | Condolences; attend the wake if appropriate and welcome; offer to help with paperwork **when the family is ready** | Any mention of policies, payouts or reviews at the wake or in the condolence message |
| Divorce or separation | Discretion. Only respond if the client tells you directly. Offer a private conversation about nominations when they're ready | Contacting the ex-spouse; discussing one party's policies with the other |

After any major life event, write a note in the tracker suggesting a review, and raise it **at the next natural touchpoint**, not straight away.

### 7. Claims support
See [`claims-support.md`](claims-support.md). Respond the same day, call before you text, and follow up weekly until the claim is closed.

### 8. Annual review invitation and meeting
- **Do:** frame it around their life: "what's changed for you this year?", "anything on your mind for the family?" Offer times that suit them and a 30-minute cap.
- **Don't:** say "review your portfolio", "check if you're under-insured", "new plans available", or mention premiums, returns or products. See [`annual-review-agenda.md`](annual-review-agenda.md).

## PDPA guardrails for every touchpoint

The PDPA sets the rules for collecting, using and disclosing personal data ([PDPC, Personal Data Protection Act overview](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/overview-of-pdpa/the-legislation/personal-data-protection-act)). In practice:

1. **Use only what the client gave you, for the purpose they gave it.** Birthdays and festival preferences collected for client service can be used for greetings. Don't buy, scrape or share lists.
2. **Store client details only in approved, access-controlled systems.** This repository and `tracker-template.csv` hold **no real client data**. Make working copies in the team's approved system, not in personal spreadsheets or chat groups.
3. **Never send NRIC numbers, policy numbers, medical details or bank details on WhatsApp.** Use the insurer's secure channels for documents.
4. **Respect "please stop".** If a client says they don't want greetings, stop the next day and record it (`contact_pref` column). Service messages about their existing policies can continue.
5. **Greetings and service messages to existing clients are not marketing.** If a message offers or promotes a product, it can become a "specified message" under the Do Not Call provisions. See [`referral-ask.md`](referral-ask.md#do-not-call-dnc-rules) for those rules. None of the M01–M40 templates is a specified message, and they must stay that way.

# Asking for referrals: compliant and low-pressure

**Context:** proposal P86, the "referral moment at policy delivery". This file covers how to make that moment, and the annual review, an *invitation* the client can easily decline, and the PDPA and Do Not Call (DNC) rules that apply to what happens next.

**The core idea:** our referral process **puts the friend in control**. We ask the client to pass *our* contact to someone who might find it useful. We don't ask the client to hand over the friend's number. The friend then decides whether to get in touch. That keeps the client comfortable, respects the friend, and greatly reduces the PDPA and DNC risk.

> This is guidance, not legal advice. Your insurer's compliance rules apply on top of this, and they win if they're stricter.

---

## When to ask, and when never to

| Moment | OK to ask? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Policy delivery meeting** (P86) | ✅ Yes, once, at the end, if the meeting went well | Only after the client's questions are fully answered. Never before the documents are explained |
| **Annual review** | ✅ Yes, once, at the close | Only if the review felt valuable to the client. Skip it if they raised a concern |
| Client spontaneously praises you | ✅ A natural opening | "That means a lot. If anyone you know ever wants a chat, I'd be glad to help." |
| Welcome message, 30-day check, birthday, festive greetings | ❌ No | Care messages must stay care messages (see [`cadence.md`](cadence.md)) |
| During or just after a **claim** | ❌ Never | Even if the claim went well. It would feel transactional at a vulnerable time |
| Bereavement, illness, divorce | ❌ Never | |
| After the client complained, or is unhappy | ❌ No | Fix the relationship first |
| In writing, by mass WhatsApp or broadcast | ❌ No | Referral asks happen only in conversation, one-to-one |

**Ask at most once per meeting, and at most twice a year per client.** If the client says no, hesitates or changes the subject, drop it and don't raise it again for at least a year.

## How to ask: scripts

All of these are low-pressure, make it easy to say no, and put the friend in control.

**At policy delivery (P86)**
> "Thank you again for trusting me with this. I hope today's walkthrough made everything clear. One small thing, and please feel free to say no: if you ever come across someone who's going through a big life change and would like a no-obligation chat, would you be comfortable passing them my contact? Only if you think it would genuinely help them."

**At the annual review**
> "I really enjoy these catch-ups, so thank you. If a friend or family member ever mentions wanting to sort out their paperwork or talk through a life change, I'd be glad to help, and I'll look after them the same way. No pressure at all."

**When the client offers a friend's name**
> "That's very kind, thank you. Rather than me contacting [friend] out of the blue, could you pass them my number and let them decide whether to reach out? I'd rather they hear about me from you first, and they won't feel chased."

**If the client insists on giving the friend's number**
> "Could you check with [friend] first that they're okay with me contacting them, and roughly when's a good time? Once they've said yes, I'll drop them a short hello. If they'd rather not, that's completely fine."
> Then follow the [rules below](#if-you-do-receive-a-friends-contact-details) before making any contact.

**What to give the client to pass on (a "contact card" message they can forward)**
> "Hi, this is [Adviser name], [approved title] (MAS Rep No. [number]). [Client first name] mentioned you might find a chat helpful. If you'd like to talk, you can reach me at [number] / [email]. No obligation, and no follow-up unless you ask."

The client forwards this themselves. The adviser doesn't send it to the friend.

### Never
- Ask for "three names", or go through the client's phone contacts with them
- Offer or give gifts, vouchers, discounts or cash in exchange for referrals. Paid introducer arrangements are regulated by MAS: only licensed or exempt financial advisers may appoint introducers, and **representatives must not enter into any arrangement with an introducer on their own** ([MAS FAQs on the FAA, Regulations, Notices and Guidelines, 28 Nov 2024, PDF](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas/regulations-and-financial-stability/regulations-guidance-and-licensing/financial-advisers/faq/faqs-on-financial-advisers-act-financial-advisers-regulations-notices-and-guidelines-281124.pdf); MAS Notice FAA-N02, *Appointment and Use of Introducers by Financial Advisers*). Any referral-reward scheme must come from, and be approved by, the insurer
- Make the client feel their service depends on referring ("I work on referrals, so…")
- Mention products, premiums or returns in the ask, or promise the friend anything
- Ask the client to share details about the friend's health, finances or family

## PDPA rules

The Personal Data Protection Act 2012 sets out how organisations collect, use and disclose personal data ([PDPC, PDPA overview](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/overview-of-pdpa/the-legislation/personal-data-protection-act)). For referrals, the key points are:

1. **Consent.** Generally, the friend's consent is needed to collect, use or disclose their personal data. PDPC recognises that an organisation can obtain personal data from a third-party source, such as a client, where the individual has consented, or is deemed to have consented, to that source disclosing it ([PDPC, Advisory Guidelines on Key Concepts in the PDPA, rev. 29 Apr 2026, PDF (file name still says 2022)](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/ag-on-key-concepts/advisory-guidelines-on-key-concepts-in-the-pdpa-17-may-2022.pdf)). **In practice:** ask the client to confirm that the friend has agreed to be contacted, and record that confirmation (who, when, how).
2. **Purpose.** Use the friend's details only for the purpose they agreed to (e.g. "one introductory hello"). Don't add them to newsletters, broadcast lists or festive greetings.
3. **Notification.** In your first contact, tell the friend how you got their details and why you're contacting them.
4. **Withdrawal.** If the friend says "no thanks" or "please don't contact me", stop, and delete their details unless there's a legal or business need to keep a do-not-contact note.
5. **Protection and retention.** Store referral details only in the team's approved system. Never keep them in personal notes, chat groups or this repository. Delete them when they're no longer needed.
6. **Don't disclose the client to the friend, or the friend to the client.** Don't tell the client whether the friend became a client, what they discussed, or anything about their situation. Don't tell the friend anything about the client's policies.

## Do Not Call (DNC) rules

Source: [PDPC, Advisory Guidelines on the Do Not Call Provisions (rev. 1 Feb 2021, PDF)](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/Files/PDPC/PDF-Files/Advisory-Guidelines/Advisory-Guidelines-on-the-DNC-Provisions-1-Feb-2021.pdf) · [PDPC Advisory Guidelines page](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/guidelines-and-consultation/2020/02/advisory-guidelines-on-the-do-not-call-provisions) · [DNC Registry](https://www.dnc.gov.sg/)

### What's covered
The DNC provisions apply to **"specified messages"**, broadly messages that offer, advertise or promote goods or services, sent to a **Singapore telephone number** by voice or video call, text (PDPC: "any text, sound or visual message", e.g. SMS/MMS) or fax. PDPC says the DNC provisions apply equally to all means of sending a specified message to a Singapore number, naming **WhatsApp, iMessage and Viber** as examples (DNC Guidelines, para on "message" definitions). So **WhatsApp messages to a Singapore number are covered**. There are three registers: No Voice Call, No Text Message and No Fax Message.

A message to a referred friend that introduces your services, offers a meeting about financial planning, or asks whether they'd like to receive such information **is a specified message**. PDPC states that a message whose purpose is to *obtain consent* to send specified messages is itself a specified message.

### Obligations when sending a specified message
1. **Check the DNC Register first** (PDPA s.43), unless you hold the person's **clear and unambiguous consent, evidenced in written or other accessible form**. DNC check results are **valid for 21 days**.
2. **Identify yourself and give contact details** in the message (s.44).
3. **Don't hide your caller ID** on voice calls (s.45).
4. **Never use address-harvesting software, dictionary attacks** or similar automated means to send messages (s.48B).
5. If the person withdraws consent, it must take effect within **21 days**. Treat it as immediate in practice.

### Referrals specifically
PDPC's guidelines address referral programmes directly:
- If the referrer gives you **written evidence of the friend's clear and unambiguous consent** to receive your specified messages, you don't need to check the DNC Register before messaging that number.
- If the referrer gives you the friend's number **without** that evidence, you **must check the DNC Register** before sending any specified message. If the number is on the relevant register, you may not send it.
- Separately, the PDPA's data protection provisions still require consent to collect and use the friend's personal data.

### Responding when the friend contacts you first
PDPC's guidance is that a message sent **solely to respond to an individual's own request for information** is generally not a specified message. If that request comes through a third party (e.g. the client says "my friend asked you to call"), PDPC advises due diligence to confirm the friend really made the request, for example written confirmation from the friend.

**This is why the "pass on my contact" approach is the default:** when the friend reaches out first, you are responding to their request, and the friend has chosen to share their own details.

### Existing clients
PDPC's guidelines exclude from "specified message" certain messages sent in an **ongoing relationship** (an insurance policy is given as an example) where the message's sole purpose relates to the subject of that relationship. **Don't treat this as permission to market to clients.** Our care templates (M01–M40) don't offer or promote anything, so they aren't specified messages in the first place. Keep them that way. Any product-related message to clients needs the insurer's compliance sign-off.

### Pre-contact checklist for a referred friend

```
Referral ref:            [internal ID — no NRIC]
Referred by (client ref):[internal ID]
How details were received: [friend contacted us directly  |  client passed details]
If client passed details:
  [ ] Client confirmed friend agreed to be contacted — date/how: ________
  [ ] Written evidence of clear & unambiguous consent for specified messages held?  Y / N
  [ ] If N: DNC Register checked on ______ (valid to ______, i.e. 21 days)  Result: ______
First contact:
  [ ] Says who I am, my contact details, and how I got their details
  [ ] Caller ID not withheld (calls)
  [ ] No product, premium, return or promise
Outcome: [meeting agreed | declined → stop & record do-not-contact | no response → no further follow-up]
```

**One introduction, no chasing.** If the friend doesn't respond to the first message, don't follow up. If the client later says the friend is now keen, the friend can reach out.

## Thanking the client

- Thank them **whether or not** the friend becomes a client: "Thank you for thinking of me. I really appreciate the trust."
- Don't tell them the outcome or any details about the friend (PDPA).
- No gifts or rewards for referrals unless they're part of an insurer-approved scheme. A sincere thank-you is enough.

# Ep 55 — CRM hygiene and personal data

**Track:** The Agency Blueprint (AB)
**Theme:** 1 — A pipeline without pressure
**The adviser's real question:** "My contact list is a mess of old spreadsheets, phone notes and chat threads. What do I actually have to do with people's personal data?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Your contact list is other people's personal data. The PDPA sets obligations on how it's collected, used, kept accurate, protected and eventually disposed of. Your firm will also have its own rules on top: follow them, and check with your compliance team when unsure.
2. Purpose: use data only for purposes a reasonable person would think appropriate and that the person was told about. A note taken for a review is not automatically fine for marketing.
3. Accuracy: make a reasonable effort to record data correctly and completely, especially where it may be used to make decisions about the person.
4. Protection: make reasonable security arrangements. There's no one-size-fits-all fix; think about the type of data and the harm if it leaked.
5. Retention: don't keep data forever "just in case". Stop retaining it once the purpose is no longer served and there's no legal or business need. Your firm sets the retention periods.
6. Breaches have clocks. Once there are credible grounds to believe a breach occurred, an organisation must assess whether it's notifiable within 30 calendar days; if it is, PDPC must be notified within 3 calendar days. Tell your firm immediately so it can run this process.
7. Hygiene is a routine, not a project: one place for records, consent and "do not contact" flags recorded, a monthly tidy-up, and no client data in personal chat groups or unapproved apps unless your firm allows it.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who finds three versions of the same contact: an old spreadsheet, a phone note and a chat thread, each with a different number. One person asked last year not to be contacted, and that request is only in the chat. He spends one afternoon moving everything into the system his firm approves. He marks the "do not contact" request clearly, records when and how each person agreed to hear from him, and flags old records for his firm's retention process instead of keeping them "just in case". He deletes the stray spreadsheet from his personal laptop, following his firm's process. Then he sets a 30-minute monthly reminder to tidy up.

## Your action this week
Find every place you keep client or prospect details: phone, laptop, chat apps, notebooks. Write the list down. Ask your compliance team which ones are approved, and move or remove the rest under your firm's process.

## "Never say" lines (episode-specific)
- "Keep everyone's details forever, you never know."
- "It's my contact list, so it's my data."
- "A small leak doesn't need reporting."
- "Just screenshot the client's documents to your phone."
- "Share the list in the team chat, it's quicker."

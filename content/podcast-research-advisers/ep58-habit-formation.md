# Ep 58 — How habits actually form

**Track:** The Resilient Advisor (RA)
**Theme:** 2 — The working week and your energy

**The adviser's real question:** "I keep starting good routines and dropping them after a week. Is it just willpower?"

## Key points (in order)
1. A habit is something you do automatically when a familiar situation comes up. The situation triggers the action, without much thinking.
2. Habits form through repetition in the same context: same time, same place, same preceding action ("after my first coffee, I…").
3. It takes longer than the "21 days" myth. In one well-known UCL study, the time to reach near-full automaticity varied from 18 to 254 days, with a typical figure of about 66 days.
4. Missing one day did not materially set people back in that study. So one slip is not failure. Just do it again next time.
5. Goals still matter: they get you to repeat a behaviour until it sticks. But once a habit is formed, the cue does more of the work than motivation.
6. Make the cue obvious and the action small, then add to it later.
7. Be kind to yourself. Slow progress is normal; the study showed big differences between people.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who wants to update her client notes every day but keeps forgetting. Instead of relying on willpower, she ties it to a cue: "When I sit back in the car after a meeting, I open my notes and write three lines." Some weeks she misses a day. She doesn't restart from zero, she just does it at the next meeting. A couple of months in, it feels odd to drive off without writing.

## Your action this week
Choose one small work habit. Write it as "After I [existing routine], I will [tiny action]." Do it in the same place each time. Put a tick on a calendar each day you do it, and don't worry about a missed day.

## "Never say" lines
- "It takes 21 days to form a habit."
- "It takes exactly 66 days." (Say "about 66 days in one study, and it varied a lot.")
- "If you miss a day, you have to start again."
- "Successful advisers all have morning routines of X." (No source.)
- "It's all about willpower / discipline."

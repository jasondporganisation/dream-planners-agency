# Ep 62 — Comparison and the highlight reel

**Track:** The Resilient Advisor (RA)
**Theme:** 2 — The working week and your energy

**The adviser's real question:** "Everyone in my feed is posting awards, trips and team wins. Why does it make me feel like I'm failing?"

> **Not medical advice.** If low mood lasts, talk to a doctor or call **National Mindline 1771** (24/7) or **SOS 1767** (24/7).

## Key points (in order)
1. Comparing ourselves with others is a basic human habit. Festinger's 1954 theory says we compare with others when we have no objective yardstick, and we tend to pick people similar to us.
2. That's why other advisers' posts land so hard. They're the people most like us, in a job with few clear yardsticks for "doing well".
3. Feeds are a highlight reel. You compare your behind-the-scenes week with someone else's best moments.
4. How you use social media seems to matter. In one set of studies, scrolling passively (looking, not interacting) led to lower mood over time, partly through envy.
5. But be careful: the evidence is mixed. A later review of 40 survey studies found most did not support a simple "passive use is bad, active use is good" rule.
6. So the practical point is modest: notice how you feel after scrolling, and change what isn't helping.
7. Pick a better yardstick: your own past self, your own values, the clients you served well this month.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who scrolls through colleagues' posts every night in bed: award stages, overseas conferences, "another record month". She falls asleep feeling behind. She tries a small experiment. For two weeks she mutes a few accounts, keeps social media to a set time in the day, and writes down one thing each evening that went well with a client. She's still curious about others. But she is measuring herself against her own month, not someone else's best day.

## Your action this week
After each time you use social media this week, rate your mood from 1 to 5 in a note on your phone. At the end of the week, look at the pattern and change one thing: mute, unfollow, set a time limit, or post less.

## "Never say" lines
- "Social media causes depression." (Overstates the evidence.)
- "Passive scrolling is proven to make you unhappy." (Evidence is mixed; see row 4.)
- "Those top producers are faking it." (No naming, no assumptions about real people.)
- Any income or production comparison, real or made-up.
- "Just stop comparing yourself." (Say: change the yardstick.)

# Ep 51 — Activity metrics: leading vs lagging numbers

**Track:** The Agency Blueprint (AB)
**Theme:** 1 — A pipeline without pressure
**The adviser's real question:** "I only look at my results at month-end, and by then it's too late. What should I actually be tracking week to week?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Lagging numbers tell you what already happened (cases closed, reviews completed). Leading numbers are the activities you control that come before them (conversations started, first meetings held, follow-ups done).
2. You can't steer by lagging numbers alone. They arrive late and depend on things outside your control.
3. Pick three or four leading numbers, no more. Choose ones that link to good client outcomes, not just volume.
4. Write them down and look at them weekly. A large meta-analysis of experiments found that prompting people to monitor progress helped them reach goals, and effects were larger when progress was recorded physically or reported to others.
5. Set clear weekly targets for activities, not just a vague "do more". Goal-setting research has studied specific goals for decades; check the details before quoting effect sizes (see notes).
6. Watch for the trap: when a count becomes the goal, quality can slip. Keep one quality check (e.g. "did the client leave with a clear next step?").
7. Review, don't judge. A bad week of numbers is information, not a verdict on you (see Ep 52).

Notes for the script team:
- Harkin et al. pooled studies of many kinds of goals; the abstract does not describe any adviser samples. Say "research on goal pursuit in general".
- Do not quote "d = 0.40" on air without explaining it; "a modest-to-moderate average effect" is safer.
- Do not give example targets with numbers of sales or income. Activity counts in examples should stay generic.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who only ever looked at her month-end summary and spent the last week of every month in a panic. She sets up a simple card with three weekly counts: new conversations started, first meetings held, and follow-ups done when promised. She adds one quality question: "Did each client leave knowing the next step?" Every Friday she fills it in by hand and shares it with her accountability partner. After a few weeks she can see that her quiet months follow weeks where follow-ups slipped, so she fixes that, not her pitch.

## Your action this week
Choose three leading numbers you fully control and one quality check. Draw a simple weekly grid on paper or in a note. Fill it in every Friday for four weeks, and show it to one trusted colleague or mentor.

## "Never say" lines (episode-specific)
- "Hit these numbers and the income follows."
- "Top producers do X calls a day." (No income or producer claims; no unsourced benchmarks.)
- "Activity is all that matters, quality will sort itself out."
- "If your numbers are low, you're lazy."
- Any target framed as cases or sales figures to hit.

# Ep 65 — Compliance as a daily habit

**Track:** The Agency Blueprint (AB)
**Theme:** 3 — Compliance, ethics and hard conversations

**The adviser's real question:** "Compliance feels like paperwork I rush at the end. How do I make it part of how I actually work?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Compliance is not a once-a-year audit. It is the small checks you do every working day: fact-find, basis of recommendation, disclosure, records.
2. The core legal idea is simple. Under the Financial Advisers Act, a recommendation needs a reasonable basis. That means you looked into it properly, with the client's objectives, financial situation and particular needs in mind.
3. MAS's Fair Dealing Guidelines set five outcomes. They are written for boards and senior management, but every outcome shows up in what a representative does in a client meeting.
4. Outcome 3 says customers are served by competent representatives. In practice, that means a proper fact-find and a recommendation you can explain.
5. The Balanced Scorecard framework (MAS Notice FAA-N20) ties how representatives are assessed to non-sales factors as well as sales. Your daily habits feed that record.
6. Build compliance into the meeting, not after it. Use your FI's checklist in the room, write the basis of recommendation while it is fresh, and file the same day.
7. When in doubt, ask early. Your compliance team would rather hear a question before submission than fix a problem after.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who used to do all her compliance work on Friday afternoons. By Friday, she couldn't remember why she had recommended one option over another on Tuesday. She changed one thing. Before leaving each meeting room, she spends ten minutes writing down the client's stated goals and her reasons, in plain words, on her FI's form. Her Fridays got quieter. When her supervisor asked about a case, she could answer from the file, not from memory.

## Your action this week
Pick one client meeting this week. Finish the basis-of-recommendation notes on the same day, before you go home, using your FI's template. Notice how long it took and what you would have forgotten by Friday.

## "Never say" lines (episode-specific)
- "Compliance is just a box-ticking exercise."
- "As long as the client signs, you're covered."
- "This is what MAS requires", as a sign-off on any specific practice. Say: "check your FI's policy and your compliance team."
- Do not describe BSC grades, thresholds or consequences (not verified here).
- Do not read out notice titles that name a product category. Say "MAS's notice on recommendations" or give the notice number.
- Do not quote FAA section numbers as fact until they are confirmed on SSO.

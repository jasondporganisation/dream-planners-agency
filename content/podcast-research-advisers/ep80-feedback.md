# Ep 80 — Feedback That Helps

**Track:** The Resilient Advisor (RA)
**Theme:** 4 — Building and leading a team
**The adviser's real question:** "When I give feedback, sometimes it lands and sometimes it backfires. What's the difference?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Feedback is not automatically good. In the largest classic review, Kluger and DeNisi found feedback improved performance on average, but over one-third of feedback interventions made performance worse.
2. Why? Their theory suggests feedback works less well as attention moves away from the task and towards the self. "Here's what to change in this part of the meeting" tends to help more than "You're not cut out for this."
3. Hattie and Timperley's review of the evidence in education reached a similar conclusion: feedback is powerful, but its effect can be positive or negative depending on the type and how it is given.
4. Even well-run 360-degree feedback leads to modest change. A meta-analysis of 24 longitudinal studies found improvement over time was generally small. Change was more likely when people saw a need to change, reacted positively, believed change was possible, set goals and took action.
5. So make it about the task: specific, close in time, tied to a goal, with one next step.
6. Then ask, don't tell: "How did that feel from your side?" Feedback that the person helps shape is easier to act on.
7. Receive feedback too. Ask your team what you should do more of and less of. It shows feedback is about learning, not status.

## Hypothetical example
Picture a leader who sits in on a newer adviser's client review. Afterwards, his first instinct is to say, "You talk too much." Instead he asks, "What went well, and what would you change?" She says she rushed the part about the client's goals. He agrees, and adds one specific point: "When the client paused after the question about their family, you jumped in. Next time, count to three." She tries it at her next meeting and tells him it felt awkward but worked. Neither of them talked about whether she's "good at this".

## Your action this week
Before your next feedback conversation, write the feedback as one sentence about the task, one about its effect, and one next step. Strike out any words about the person's character. Then ask one team member, "What's one thing I could do differently as your leader?"

## "Never say" lines
- "Feedback always helps."
- "Science says a third of feedback backfires, so don't bother." (That is a misreading.)
- "You're just not a natural."
- Feedback given in front of the team about one person's mistakes.
- "Close more and you'll be fine."

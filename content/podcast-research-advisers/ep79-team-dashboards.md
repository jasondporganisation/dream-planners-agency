# Ep 79 — Team Dashboards Without Micromanaging

**Track:** The Agency Blueprint (AB)
**Theme:** 4 — Building and leading a team
**The adviser's real question:** "I want to see how my team is doing, but how do I track numbers without turning into a hovering boss or pushing people to game them?"

## Key points (in order)
1. A dashboard is for asking better questions, not for judging people. Use it to start conversations, and agree with the team what is on it and why.
2. Numbers bend when you lean on them. Campbell's law: the more a quantitative indicator is used for decisions, the more it is open to corruption pressures, and the more it can distort what it was meant to measure. Strathern's version of Goodhart's law: "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."
3. Goals are powerful but have side effects. Ordóñez and colleagues argue that goal setting is overprescribed and list side effects: narrow focus, distorted risk-taking, a rise in unethical behaviour, less learning, a damaged culture and lower intrinsic motivation.
4. Locke and Latham's decades of research shows specific, challenging goals can lift performance. So the answer is careful goals, not no goals.
5. In Singapore the regulator already builds quality into the scorecard. Under MAS Notice FAA-N20 (the Balanced Scorecard Framework), firms must include four non-sales KPIs: understanding a client's needs, suitability of recommendations, adequacy of information disclosure, and standards of professionalism and ethical conduct.
6. Practical design: show quality and activity signals next to each other. Look at trends, not single weeks. Let each adviser see their own data first. Never post rankings publicly.
7. Ask your compliance team how your team dashboard sits alongside the firm's official balanced scorecard process. Don't build a shadow system that contradicts it.

## Hypothetical example
Picture a leader who used to post a weekly ranking of meetings booked. People started booking short "coffee catch-ups" to move up the list, and file quality slipped. She replaces the ranking with a private dashboard for each adviser: client reviews done, files returned for fixes, follow-ups kept, and a monthly trend line. In one-on-ones she asks, "What's this telling you?" before giving her own view. The team's numbers look less dramatic, but conversations about quality become normal.

## Your action this week
List every number on your current team dashboard. Next to each, write "What behaviour could this accidentally encourage?" Remove or re-balance one that fails the test, and tell the team why.

## "Never say" lines
- "What gets measured gets done, full stop."
- "Top of the leaderboard gets [any reward or income]."
- Any public naming or ranking of individual advisers.
- "The balanced scorecard is just a box-ticking exercise."
- Explaining how the balanced scorecard affects anyone's pay or variable income.
- "Our dashboard is MAS-approved." (Nothing here is a regulatory sign-off.)

# Ep 53 — Follow-up cadence without chasing

**Track:** The Agency Blueprint (AB)
**Theme:** 1 — A pipeline without pressure
**The adviser's real question:** "How often can I follow up before I become a nuisance, and what's the line I can't cross?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Follow-up is a service when it is agreed. The simplest fix for "chasing" is to agree the next contact at the end of each conversation: when, how, and about what.
2. Have a default rhythm and write it down, e.g. a thank-you note soon after a meeting, a check-in at the agreed time, then one gentle last note that closes the loop and leaves the door open.
3. Stop when someone says stop. Record it and honour it straight away.
4. Know the legal floor. Under the PDPA's Do Not Call provisions, before sending certain marketing calls or texts to Singapore numbers you must check the DNC register (unless you have clear and unambiguous consent), identify yourself, and not hide your number.
5. Opt-out consent is not enough. PDPC says a pre-ticked box does not count as clear and unambiguous consent for messaging a DNC-registered number.
6. MAS adds its own expectations for voice calls without a prior appointment: tell people upfront why you're calling and get consent, afresh for each call.
7. Use "if-then" plans so follow-up happens without willpower: "If a meeting ends, then I log the agreed next step before I leave the building."

Notes for the script team:
- "SMS is outside MAS's telemarketing definition" does NOT mean texts are unregulated. PDPA DNC duties still apply to marketing texts. Say both together or neither.
- Do not explain the "ongoing relationship" exemption in detail on air; its scope was not researched. Say "check with your compliance team".
- The penalty figures in fact 3 are UNVERIFIED. If used, say "penalties can be significant" unless confirmed.
- Fact 5 pooled studies of many kinds of goals; none is described as being about advisers.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who used to send "just checking in!" texts every few days to anyone who'd gone quiet, and dreaded each one. Now she ends every meeting with, "When would you like to hear from me next, and is text or email better?" She notes the answer in her CRM with a date. If someone hasn't replied after the agreed check-in and one more note, she sends a final message: "I'll leave it here so I'm not crowding your inbox. If anything changes, I'm happy to pick up." For anyone new, she checks with her firm's process on DNC before any marketing message goes out.

## Your action this week
Write your default follow-up rhythm on one page: the agreed next step, the check-in, and the closing note, with your exact wording. Add one if-then plan, e.g. "If a meeting ends, then I log the next step before I leave." Ask your compliance team how your firm handles DNC checks and consent records.

## "Never say" lines (episode-specific)
- "Keep messaging until they reply."
- "Texts don't count, only calls are regulated."
- "If they didn't untick the box, they've agreed."
- "Call from a private number so they pick up."
- "Create urgency: tell them the offer ends soon."

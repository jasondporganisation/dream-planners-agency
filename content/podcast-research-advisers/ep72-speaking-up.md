# Ep 72 — Speaking up when something's off

**Track:** The Resilient Advisor (RA)
**Theme:** 3 — Compliance, ethics and hard conversations

**The adviser's real question:** "I've seen something that doesn't sit right with me. How do I raise it without wrecking my career or my relationships?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Staying quiet often feels safer in the moment. The cost usually shows up later, for clients, the team and you.
2. Teams learn and fix things faster when people feel safe to take interpersonal risks, like asking questions, admitting mistakes or flagging concerns. Researchers call this psychological safety.
3. For leaders, safety is built in small moments. How you react the first time someone brings you bad news decides whether they bring you the next one.
4. For individual advisers, start with the smallest step that works. Ask a question, check the policy, raise it with your supervisor or compliance.
5. MAS expects FIs covered by its Individual Accountability and Conduct Guidelines to have a formal whistleblowing programme. That means channels for employees, procedures to protect anonymity and protect those who raise concerns, and a process for handling reports. Know where your FI's channel is.
6. MAS also has a public form for reporting regulatory breaches or misconduct. It is an option, not usually the first step. Check your FI's policy and get advice if you're unsure.
7. Write down what you saw, when, and who you told. Stick to facts, not guesses about motives.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an agency leader who has always said "my door is open". One day, a newer adviser tells him, nervously, that a senior team member seems to be filling in client forms after meetings without the client present. The leader's first reaction is irritation. He catches it, thanks her, and asks only factual questions. That same day, he takes it to compliance through the FI's channel. He doesn't investigate it himself. Weeks later, two other people bring him smaller worries. He realises the first conversation is what opened the door.

## Your action this week
Find your FI's whistleblowing or speak-up channel: where it is, who runs it, and what it says about confidentiality. Save it in your phone. If you lead a team, tell them where it is too, and say you'd rather hear concerns early.

- "Investigate it yourself first and gather evidence."
- "If it's a top producer, let it slide."
- "Whistleblowers are troublemakers."

# Ep 75 — Recruiting Honestly

**Track:** The Agency Blueprint (AB)
**Theme:** 4 — Building and leading a team
**The adviser's real question:** "How do I invite someone into this career without overselling it, and what do they actually have to do to get started?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Honest recruiting starts with saying what the job is, including the hard parts. Leave out income talk completely. What someone could earn is not ours to promise.
2. There are formal entry rules. Under MAS Notice FAA-N26 (in force from 1 April 2024), a person must be at least 21 and hold a minimum academic qualification (for example, a polytechnic diploma or the set GCE 'A' Level passes, or an equivalent), unless an exemption applies.
3. They must also pass the required CMFAS exams: the Rules, Ethics and Skills module (RES5) plus the product-knowledge modules for the activities they will do. These are run by IBF or SCI. (Do not name the product modules on air.)
4. Once appointed, a representative's details appear on MAS's public Financial Institution Representatives Register. Show a recruit the register so they see this is a regulated profession.
5. Realistic job previews: a meta-analysis found that giving candidates a realistic picture of the job was linked to higher performance and lower drop-out during recruitment. Tell people about the rejection, the admin, the learning curve and the compliance work.
6. Let candidates opt out without awkwardness. A "no" after an honest preview is a good outcome for both sides.
7. Rules change. Check entry and exam requirements with your compliance team and the MAS/IBF/SCI pages each time.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who meets a former classmate curious about the career. Instead of a pitch, he walks her through a real, ordinary week: study time for exams, admin, client meetings that get cancelled, and the compliance checks on every file. He shows her the MAS register on his phone and explains what it means to be listed. He says plainly that he cannot and will not talk about what she might earn. She takes two weeks to think. She decides it is not for her right now, and thanks him for being straight. A year later she refers a friend to him for a chat, because she trusts him.

## Your action this week
Write a one-page "honest week" description of the job, covering study, admin, compliance, rejection and support, with no income figures. Ask your compliance team to review it before you use it with any candidate.

## "Never say" lines
- Any income, earnings, commission, "six figures", "passive income" or "financial freedom" line. None, in any form.
- "Anyone can do this" or "The exams are easy."
- "You'll be qualified in X weeks." (Timelines vary; do not promise.)
- "Bring your family and friends as your first clients."
- Naming any exam module that refers to a product type.
- "It's basically a business you own with no risk."

# Ep 50 — What "no" does to you, and what to do with it

**Track:** The Resilient Advisor (RA)
**Theme:** 1 — A pipeline without pressure
**The adviser's real question:** "Why does one 'no' ruin my whole day, and how do I stop it piling up?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Rejection stings for a reason. Brain-imaging research suggests being socially excluded engages some of the same regions involved in the distress of physical pain. Feeling hurt is normal, not a sign you are in the wrong job.
2. Be careful with the brain science. It shows overlap in some brain regions in lab tasks. It does not prove a "no" is the same as physical pain.
3. Separate the event from the story. "They said no to a meeting" is the event. "I'm bad at this" is a story you added.
4. Self-compassion is not letting yourself off. In experiments, people who treated a failure with self-compassion were more motivated to improve, not less.
5. A short reset routine helps: notice the sting, name it, take a short break, then do one small useful task.
6. Respect the "no". A clean, warm exit keeps the door open and keeps you out of pressure tactics.
7. If low mood lasts or feels heavy, talk to someone. Support lines are listed below.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who gets three "not now" replies before lunch. By the third he's drafting a message to his manager about quitting. Instead he tries a reset he practised. He writes down what happened in one plain line, then the story he's telling himself, then what he'd say to a new colleague in the same spot. He walks round the block. When he's back he sends a short, warm reply to each person: "Thanks for letting me know. If anything changes, I'm here." Then he does one admin task he's been avoiding. The day isn't great. It isn't ruined either.

## Your action this week
Write a three-line "after a no" card: (1) what happened, in facts; (2) what I'd tell a friend; (3) one small next task. Keep it on your phone and use it the next time a "no" lands hard.

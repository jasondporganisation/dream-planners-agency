# Ep 48 — The fear of becoming "that person"

**Track:** The Resilient Advisor (RA)
**Theme:** 1 — A pipeline without pressure
**The adviser's real question:** "I don't want to be the friend everyone avoids at gatherings. How do I talk about my work without feeling like a salesperson?"

## Key points (in order)
1. The fear is reasonable. It usually comes from care for the relationship, not weakness. Name it before trying to fix it.
2. We tend to overestimate how much others notice and judge us. Research on the "spotlight effect" and the "liking gap" suggests our worst guesses about how we come across are often too harsh.
3. "That person" is defined by behaviour, not job title: pushing, not hearing "no", and turning every chat into a pitch. You can avoid all three.
4. Be upfront and give an easy exit. Saying what you do and why you are asking, then making "no" easy, protects the relationship. MAS is moving towards this standard for public places too: state your intention and get consent first.
5. Separate the friendship from the business. A useful line is: "I'd love to tell you about what I do one day, but only if it's useful. Either way, nothing changes between us."
6. You don't have to start with your closest people. It can be easier to build skill with warm-but-not-close contacts first. (Host's view, not a sourced claim.)
7. If the anxiety is strong or lasting, it is worth talking to someone. That is a strength, not a failure.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who skips his cousin's birthday dinner because he's sure everyone expects a pitch. He replays old conversations and imagines eye-rolls. A mentor suggests a small test. At the next gathering he talks about anything but work unless asked. If someone asks, he gives one honest sentence about what he does and adds, "No pressure, I'm off duty tonight." Two relatives ask follow-up questions. Nobody avoids him. The fear doesn't vanish, but it shrinks to a size he can work with.

## Your action this week
Write your one-sentence answer to "So what do you do?" that is honest, short and has an easy exit built in. Say it out loud three times. Use it once, only when someone asks.

## "Never say" lines (episode-specific)

- "If they say no, they're not real friends."
- "Push through the awkwardness: it's only rejection if you stop asking."
- "Use the family group chat to share your offer."
- "Anxiety is all in your head." (Dismissive; and the show is not giving clinical advice.)

# Ep 70 — When a client complains

**Track:** The Resilient Advisor (RA)
**Theme:** 3 — Compliance, ethics and hard conversations

**The adviser's real question:** "A client has complained about me. I feel sick. What happens now, and how do I handle it well?"

## Key points (in order)
1. A complaint feels personal. It is also a process, and processes have steps. Knowing the steps lowers the fear.
2. The complaint goes through your FI first. MAS expects complaints to be handled in an independent, effective and prompt manner (Fair Dealing Outcome 5). People not connected to the complaint should review it.
3. Your job is to cooperate, be honest and hand over your records. It is not to contact the client privately to "sort it out" unless your FI's process says you may.
4. If the client is not satisfied with the FI's final reply, they can go to FIDReC. It is an independent dispute resolution body offering mediation and adjudication.
5. Be ready for big feelings: shame, anger, worry. Talk to someone you trust, outside the case details, and keep looking after sleep and routine.
6. MAS asks FIs to treat complainants with empathy, since they may be under financial or emotional distress. You can do the same, even while you disagree.
7. Afterwards, look for the lesson without beating yourself up. What would you write down, say or check differently?

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who opens an email from her FI's complaints unit and feels her stomach drop. Her first instinct is to call the client and explain. Instead, she rings her supervisor, asks what the process is, and sends the complaints unit her full file that day. That evening, she goes for her usual walk and tells her partner only that she's dealing with "a hard work thing". The review takes weeks. Whatever the outcome, she knows she handled her part properly.

## Your action this week
Find out, before you ever need it, how your FI's complaints process works. Who handles complaints, what is expected of you, and what you must not do? Write the answers on one page and keep it somewhere you'll find it.

## "Never say" lines (episode-specific)
- "Call the client and get them to withdraw it."
- "Complaints only happen to bad advisers."
- "FIDReC will side with the client / the FI." It is independent. Don't predict outcomes.
- "You'll be fine if your notes are good."
- Don't quote FIDReC fees (not verified here).
- No details of real complaints or cases.

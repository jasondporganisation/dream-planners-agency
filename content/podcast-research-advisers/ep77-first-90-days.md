# Ep 77 — The First 90 Days of a New Adviser

**Track:** The Agency Blueprint (AB)
**Theme:** 4 — Building and leading a team
**The adviser's real question:** "Someone new just joined my team. What should their first three months actually look like?"

## Key points (in order)
1. The first months are about adjustment, not output. A large meta-analysis of newcomers found three adjustment markers that linked onboarding to later outcomes: role clarity, self-efficacy (confidence they can do the job) and social acceptance.
2. Role clarity: write down what a good week looks like, who to ask about what, and what "done properly" means for a client file.
3. Self-efficacy: build confidence with small, real tasks, observed meetings and practice before solo work. Give specific feedback early.
4. Social acceptance: introduce them to people, pair them with a buddy or mentor, and include them in team rituals from week one.
5. Encourage questions. In that same meta-analysis, newcomers' own information seeking was one of the inputs linked to better adjustment. Make asking easy and safe.
6. Know the CPD rules. Under MAS Notice FAA-N26, representatives must complete minimum CPD hours each calendar year, but not for the first calendar year in which they are first appointed. Each later year includes 6 "Core" hours, plus "Supplementary" hours that depend on the type of advisory service. Plan the learning calendar early with your compliance or training team.
7. Book a 30/60/90-day check-in in advance, and make it two-way: what's clear, what's not, what support is missing.

## Hypothetical example
Picture a leader who welcomes a new adviser with a one-page "first 90 days" plan. Weeks 1–4: learn the firm's processes, sit in on meetings, and meet the team, with a buddy for daily questions. Weeks 5–8: run parts of meetings with the leader observing, then a short debrief each time. Weeks 9–12: more independence, with file reviews still in place. Training dates for the year are on the calendar from day one. At day 30 the new adviser says she's unsure who handles which admin questions. The leader adds a "who to ask" list to the plan for the next person too.

## Your action this week
Draft a one-page 90-day plan with three columns (role clarity, confidence, belonging) and put the 30-, 60- and 90-day check-ins into both calendars now.

## "Never say" lines
- "In your first 90 days you should hit [any sales number]."
- "CPD doesn't matter in year one, so skip the training." (The first-year exemption is a regulatory detail. Learning still matters, and other firm rules may apply.)
- Any number of Supplementary CPD hours linked to a product type.
- "Just shadow me and you'll pick it up."
- "This plan means you'll definitely succeed."

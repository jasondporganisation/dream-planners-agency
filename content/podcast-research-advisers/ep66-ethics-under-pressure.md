# Ep 66 — Targets and temptation: ethics under pressure

**Track:** The Resilient Advisor (RA)
**Theme:** 3 — Compliance, ethics and hard conversations

**The adviser's real question:** "It's the last week of the quarter and I'm just short. How do I stop that pressure from bending my judgement?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Good people don't usually set out to cut corners. Research suggests pressure and tiredness change the odds.
2. In a lab experiment, people with unmet goals were more likely to behave unethically than people told to "do their best". The effect was strongest when they were *just short* of the goal.
3. Researchers have warned that goals can have side effects: narrow focus, more risk-taking, more unethical behaviour and a weaker culture. Goals are not bad. They need careful use.
4. Sleep matters too. Studies have linked less sleep to lower self-control and more unethical behaviour.
5. MAS has said plainly that a representative should not skew recommendations towards what pays them more. Fair dealing comes before the target.
6. Know your own danger zones, such as end of quarter, campaign periods and late nights. Set your rules in advance, while you are calm.
7. Talk about it. Telling a leader or peer "I'm close to target and I notice the pull" takes away much of its power.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who notices a pattern in himself. In the last fortnight of every quarter, he talks faster in meetings and skips the "do you want to think about it?" line. He writes himself three rules in a calm week. In the last fortnight, he still offers every client time to decide. He doesn't book meetings after 9pm. He tells his team leader each Monday where he stands, with no spin. The pressure doesn't go away, but it no longer runs the meeting.

## Your action this week
Write down your own "pressure rules": two or three lines you will keep no matter where you are against target. Share them with one person you trust, such as a leader, peer or partner.

## "Never say" lines (episode-specific)
- "Everyone stretches the truth a bit near quarter-end."
- "Hitting target is what matters most."
- "Sleep less, close more."
- "Research proves tired advisers are dishonest." Say: "studies in other settings found a link between less sleep and lower self-control."
- No talk of what any target is worth in money.

# Ep 57 — Time-blocking your week

**Track:** The Agency Blueprint (AB)
**Theme:** 2 — The working week and your energy

**The adviser's real question:** "My week is all meetings, messages and admin in random order. How do I get the important work done?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Time-blocking means giving each type of work its own slot in the calendar: client meetings, prospecting, follow-ups, admin, learning, rest.
2. Switching between tasks has a cost. In lab experiments, people were slower when they alternated between tasks than when they repeated one, and the cost rose with harder rules.
3. Unfinished tasks follow you. One study found people still thinking about an unfinished task did worse on the next one. This is called "attention residue".
4. So group similar work together and, where you can, close a task (or park it with a note) before you move on.
5. Make each block specific: "If it is Tuesday 9am, then I make follow-up calls from my list." Research on these "if-then" plans found a medium-to-large effect on reaching goals.
6. Protect a few blocks that matter most. Leave buffer time, because client needs do not always fit the plan.
7. Keep compliance tasks in the calendar too (record notes, file updates). Check timing rules with your compliance team.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser whose day is a string of WhatsApp replies, half-written notes and calls squeezed between meetings. He blocks Monday and Thursday mornings for follow-up calls, Tuesday and Wednesday afternoons for client meetings, and a short slot at 5pm each day to finish meeting notes. He writes one if-then line for each block. When a meeting overruns, he writes a one-line "where I stopped" note before the next thing. After a few weeks, fewer tasks are left half-done.

## Your action this week
Open next week's calendar. Book three blocks: one for prospecting or follow-ups, one for admin and notes, one with nothing in it (buffer). Write an "If it is [day/time], then I will [task]" line inside each block.

## "Never say" lines
- "Time-blocking will double / triple your income or production."
- "Multitasking costs you X% of your productivity" (figure not verified).
- "Ignore clients during your blocks." (Say: decide how you'll handle urgent matters.)
- "This is the best / only way to plan a week."
- Anything implying you can delay compliance-related record-keeping to "later in the week" without checking with your compliance team.

# Ep 56 — Burnout: what it is and the early signs

**Track:** The Resilient Advisor (RA)
**Theme:** 2 — The working week and your energy

**The adviser's real question:** "I'm tired all the time and I've stopped caring about clients I used to enjoy. Is this just a bad patch, or is it burnout?"

> **Not medical advice.** This episode explains how burnout is described by WHO and by researchers. It does not diagnose anyone. If you are struggling, speak to a doctor or call **National Mindline 1771** (24/7) or **SOS 1767** (24/7). In an emergency, call **995** or go to the nearest A&E.

## Key points (in order)
1. Burnout has a formal description. WHO's ICD-11 calls it a syndrome from chronic workplace stress "that has not been successfully managed". It is listed as an occupational phenomenon, not a medical condition.
2. It has three parts: exhaustion; mental distance or cynicism about the job; and a drop in how effective you feel. Tiredness alone is not the whole picture.
3. The early signs often show up as the second and third parts: dreading certain client calls, going through the motions, feeling that nothing you do makes a difference.
4. Researchers Maslach and Leiter look at six areas of work life: workload, control, reward, community, fairness and values. A mismatch in one or more of these is where burnout tends to build.
5. So the useful question is not "what's wrong with me?" but "which of the six areas is out of line for me right now?"
6. Self-employed advisers set a lot of their own workload and hours. That gives you some control, but it also means nobody else is watching your limits.
7. Burnout is not a sign of weakness, and you do not have to sort it out alone. Talk to someone early: a doctor, a counsellor, a trusted leader, or a support line.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who used to look forward to annual reviews. Over a few months she starts rescheduling them. She still hits her activity targets, but she feels flat, and she catches herself thinking "what's the point?" after a good meeting. She goes through the six areas and sees that workload is fine, but control and values are off: she has said yes to a run of evening events she doesn't believe in. She talks it through with her leader and a counsellor, drops two commitments, and books a check-up with her GP.

## Your action this week
Take ten minutes. Write the six areas (workload, control, reward, community, fairness, values) down the side of a page. Give each one a quick tick, a question mark or a cross for how it feels this month. Pick the one cross that bothers you most and tell one person about it this week.

## "Never say" lines
- "You have burnout" or "you don't have burnout" (no diagnosing on air).
- "Burnout is a medical condition / mental illness." (WHO says it is not classified as a medical condition.)
- "Just push through it" or "take a holiday and it'll go away."
- "Burnout only happens to weak / disorganised people."
- Any statistic on how many advisers are burnt out.
- Any suggestion that listeners skip seeing a doctor or counsellor.

# Ep 74 — From Producer to Leader: The Identity Shift

**Track:** The Resilient Advisor (RA)
**Theme:** 4 — Building and leading a team
**The adviser's real question:** "I was good at doing the work myself. Why do I feel lost now that my job is helping other people do it?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Moving from producing to leading is a change in who you are at work, not only a change in tasks. Feeling unsure at first is part of the shift, not proof you chose wrong.
2. Linda Hill (Harvard Business School) calls the first assignment to manage others the earliest real test of leadership, and a "rite of passage".
3. Ibarra's study of professionals moving into more senior roles found that people adapt by trying on "provisional selves": they watch role models, try out styles, and keep what fits. You do not need the finished leader identity on day one.
4. Leadership identity is also social. DeRue and Ashford propose that it forms when people claim a leader role and others grant it, through everyday interactions. Your team's response is part of how the identity settles.
5. The stories we tell about ourselves help. Ibarra and Barbulescu propose that people revise their self-narratives during big role changes, and test them in conversation.
6. Practical upshot: pick two or three leaders you respect, try one behaviour from each, and ask for honest feedback. Then keep, adjust or drop it.
7. Be patient with the gap between your old competence and your new one. The old scoreboard (your own output) no longer measures the new job.

## Hypothetical example
Picture a leader who was, for years, the person everyone asked for help with tricky client files. Six months into leading a small team, she still jumps in and fixes every file herself. Her evenings vanish and the team stops trying. She picks two leaders she admires. One asks questions before giving answers. The other writes a short "what good looks like" note. She tries both for a month and asks two team members what changed. The answer: "You ask more and fix less." She keeps the questions, adjusts the notes, and notices she now judges her week by how her team did, not only by what she did.

## Your action this week
Write down the names of two leaders you respect. Under each, note one specific thing they do. Try one of those behaviours in your next three team conversations, then ask one person, "What did you notice?"

## "Never say" lines
- "If you were a real leader, this would feel natural."
- "Leading is just producing through other people's hands."
- "Research proves most new leaders fail." (Hill writes an argued essay, and Ibarra's study is qualitative.)
- Any line that compares a leader's team output or override income with being a producer.
- "Step up to leader and your career is set."

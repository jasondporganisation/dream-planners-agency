# Ep 73 — Protecting client data in a scam-heavy world

**Track:** The Agency Blueprint (AB)
**Theme:** 3 — Compliance, ethics and hard conversations

**The adviser's real question:** "I carry a lot of client information on my phone and laptop. What happens if it leaks, and how do I protect my clients from scammers?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Client data is part of trust. A leak can expose clients to scammers who sound convincing because they know real details.
2. Under the PDPA, organisations must assess a suspected breach and, if it is notifiable, tell the PDPC within three calendar days. That clock is short, so report anything suspicious to your FI *immediately* and let them make the call.
3. A breach is notifiable if it is likely to cause significant harm to individuals, or if it affects 500 or more people.
4. The PDPC can impose financial penalties of up to S$1 million, or 10% of annual Singapore turnover for organisations with turnover above S$10 million. This is mainly your FI's exposure, but your habits drive the risk.
5. Scams are still large in scale. Scammers impersonate officials, including MAS, and people in financial services. Some ask victims to "check" them on MAS's Representatives Register.
6. Help clients spot this. Teach them that MAS will never ask them to transfer money. Tell them how *you* will and won't contact them, and point them to the ScamShield Helpline (1799) when unsure.
7. Basic hygiene: follow your FI's device and messaging rules, lock devices, don't forward client documents through personal chat apps unless your FI allows it, and be wary of urgent requests that "come from" colleagues or clients.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who gets a message from a colleague's number: "Urgent, can you send me Mr Tan's form? My laptop died." It feels normal. She remembers that compromised chat accounts are a known scam route. She calls the colleague on a number she already has. The colleague's account had been taken over. She reports it to her FI's IT and compliance teams straight away. That week, she sends her clients a short note, approved by her FI, explaining how she will and won't contact them.

## Your action this week
Do a ten-minute "data walk" of your own setup. Where does client information live: phone, laptop, chat apps, paper in the car? Fix the single biggest gap using your FI's rules. Then save your FI's breach-reporting contact in your phone.

## "Never say" lines (episode-specific)
- "You have 3 days from when you find a leak." The clock runs from when the organisation *determines* it is notifiable. And you should tell your FI at once.
- "Small leaks don't need reporting." Let your FI assess every suspected breach.
- "Scams only happen to the elderly / careless."
- "Check the MAS register to see if a caller is from MAS." The register is not a list of MAS staff.
- Don't name any company, chat app or scam case from the SPF brief or the advisories.
- No product types when describing scams (e.g. say "scammers posing as financial services staff").

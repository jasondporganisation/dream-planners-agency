# Ep 63 — Tax, MediSave and records as a self-employed adviser

**Track:** The Agency Blueprint (AB)
**Theme:** 2 — The working week and your energy

**The adviser's real question:** "Nobody deducts anything from my commissions. What do I actually have to file, pay and keep?"

> **General information only, not tax advice.** Rules change. Check the IRAS and CPF Board pages in the week of recording, and speak to IRAS, CPF Board or a qualified tax professional about your own case.

## Key points (in order)
1. IRAS treats commission agents as self-employed. You declare your commission as self-employment income in your tax return, even if the paying organisation has also sent the figures to IRAS. Check any pre-filled numbers.
2. Your taxable business income is broadly your income less allowable business expenses. That is why records matter.
3. Keep proper records and accounts for 5 years: invoices, receipts and other supporting documents. IRAS says estimates are not acceptable.
4. If records are missing, IRAS may disallow expenses, assess extra income on its best estimate, and/or impose penalties.
5. MediSave: if your annual net trade income (NTI) is more than $6,000, you must contribute to your MediSave Account as a self-employed person. NTI is gross trade income minus allowable business expenses, capital allowances and trade losses.
6. CPF Board sends a notice of CPF contributions for self-employed persons after IRAS assesses your income. Payment is due within 30 days of the notice. The amount depends on your age and NTI; use CPF Board's calculator.
7. Build a monthly habit: file receipts, note expenses, and set aside money for tax and MediSave as income comes in (see Ep 61).

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who keeps receipts in a shoebox and pieces them together every April. One year she can't find proof for several expenses and has to leave them out. Now, every Friday in her weekly review, she photographs receipts into one folder named by month and adds a line to a simple expense sheet. When her CPF notice arrives after her tax assessment, she already has money set aside for it, and she knows the 30-day deadline.

## Your action this week
Set up one folder (paper or digital) with 12 monthly sub-folders for this year. Put every business receipt from the past month into it, and add a calendar reminder to keep records for 5 years before deleting anything.

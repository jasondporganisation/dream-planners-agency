# Ep 68 — Telling a client what they don't want to hear

**Track:** The Resilient Advisor (RA)
**Theme:** 3 — Compliance, ethics and hard conversations

**The adviser's real question:** "My client wants something that doesn't fit their situation. How do I say so without losing them?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Most of us avoid hard honesty because we expect it to go badly. Research suggests we often overestimate how badly.
2. In a set of studies, people who focused on being honest found it more pleasant and socially connecting, and less harmful to relationships, than they had predicted.
3. For advisers, honesty is not only kindness. It is part of fair dealing. Clients should get suitable recommendations and clear, relevant information (Fair Dealing Outcomes 2 and 4).
4. Lead with what you heard. Repeat back the client's goal before you say why this choice may not serve it.
5. Say the hard part plainly, once, and then stop talking. Silence gives them room to think.
6. Offer a path. "Here's what I can recommend instead, and why." Or: "Let's take time and look again."
7. If the client still wants to go ahead, follow your FI's process and write it down. Your notes should show what you advised and what the client chose.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser whose long-time client arrives excited about an idea a relative suggested. It doesn't fit the client's stated goal of keeping money available for a child's education. She feels the urge to just go along. Instead, she says: "You told me last year that this money is for your daughter's school fees. I'm worried this choice works against that. Can I show you why?" Then she waits. The client is quiet, then asks questions. They agree to meet again next week. She writes up what was discussed that afternoon.

## Your action this week
Think of one honest conversation you have been putting off with a client or a team member. Write the first two sentences you will say, starting with what they told you they want. Have the conversation this week.

## "Never say" lines (episode-specific)
- "Just give clients what they ask for. It's their money."
- "Honesty always keeps the client." Research shows people *overestimate* the harm. It does not promise any outcome.
- "Tell them it's safe so they relax."
- "If they insist, it's not your problem." Say: "follow your FI's process and document it, and check with compliance."
- No product examples in the hypothetical. Keep the clash about goals, not products.

# Ep 78 — Leading Lapsed Producers with Dignity

**Track:** The Resilient Advisor (RA)
**Theme:** 4 — Building and leading a team
**The adviser's real question:** "Someone on my team has gone quiet and stopped producing. How do I talk to them without shaming them or giving up on them?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Start with the person, not the number. A quiet period can come from health, family, confidence, burnout, or a real rethink about the career. Ask before you assume.
2. Self-determination theory (Ryan & Deci) proposes three basic psychological needs: competence, autonomy and relatedness. When these are met, people tend to be more self-motivated. When they are thwarted, motivation and well-being drop.
3. Applied to work (Gagné & Deci), the theory separates more autonomous motivation (doing it because it matters to you) from controlled motivation (doing it under pressure or for approval). Pressure tactics tend to push people towards the controlled kind.
4. So the leader's job is to rebuild need support. Competence: one small, doable next step. Autonomy: let them choose the plan. Relatedness: keep them included and spoken to with respect.
5. Have an honest conversation about direction. Staying, scaling down, changing roles or leaving can all be dignified outcomes. Help them decide; don't decide for them.
6. Keep the standards. Being kind does not mean relaxing compliance, supervision or the firm's rules. Check firm policy and compliance on any formal steps.
7. Protect privacy. Never discuss a struggling person's situation in front of the team.

## Hypothetical example
Picture a leader whose team member has had almost no client meetings for two months and has stopped coming to team sessions. Instead of putting a red mark on the dashboard, the leader asks for a private coffee and opens with, "I've noticed you've been quieter. How are you doing?" It turns out the adviser is caring for a parent and feels like a failure. They agree on one small step he chooses himself: keep in touch with existing clients for the next month, with a check-in every two weeks. The leader also mentions the firm's support options. Two months later the adviser says he wants to stay but at a slower pace, and they plan around that.

## Your action this week
Think of one team member who has gone quiet. Send a short private message that asks how they are, with no mention of numbers, and offer a time to talk that suits them.

## "Never say" lines
- "Everyone else is hitting their numbers. What's wrong with you?"
- "Produce or you're out." (Formal performance steps go through firm policy and compliance, not on-air threats.)
- Any public ranking or naming of low producers.
- "Just think about the money you're missing."
- "Motivation research proves you can fix anyone."

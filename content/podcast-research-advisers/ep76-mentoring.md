# Ep 76 — Mentoring That Works

**Track:** The Resilient Advisor (RA)
**Theme:** 4 — Building and leading a team
**The adviser's real question:** "I want to mentor the newer people on my team, but does it actually make a difference, and what makes it work?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Mentoring helps, but modestly. Two large meta-analyses found mentoring linked to better outcomes, with effects that were generally small. That is honest and still worth doing.
2. The benefits are broad. Eby and colleagues found links with behavioural, attitudinal, health-related, relational, motivational and career outcomes, across youth, academic and workplace mentoring. Workplace and academic mentoring showed larger effects than youth mentoring.
3. Feeling-based outcomes tend to be stronger than hard career outcomes. Allen and colleagues found effects on objective career outcomes were small; they also looked at subjective ones such as career satisfaction.
4. Mentoring has two sides: career help (guidance, sponsorship, stretch tasks) and psychosocial help (encouragement, acceptance, a safe person to talk to). Allen et al. found the type of mentoring related differently to different outcomes.
5. Quality matters more than the label. A poor or "marginal" relationship may not help. Agree on purpose, meeting rhythm and how either side can end it kindly.
6. Mentoring is not supervision. Keep compliance sign-off and file review with the proper supervisor and process. A mentor listens, shares experience and asks good questions.
7. Mentors gain too: it sharpens your own thinking. But protect your time and set limits.

Note: Eby 2008 and Allen 2004 are mostly studies from the US and other Western settings, across many jobs. They were not studies of Singapore advisers. Say "studies found", not "mentoring will".

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who agrees to mentor a newer colleague. At their first coffee they agree three things: meet every two weeks for 30 minutes, the newer adviser brings the agenda, and either can pause the arrangement with no hard feelings. Some sessions are about planning the week. Others are about a client meeting that went badly and how it felt. When a question about a client file comes up, the mentor says, "That's one for your supervisor and compliance. Let's make sure you raise it today." After three months they review: what helped, what didn't, keep going or stop.

## Your action this week
If you mentor someone, send them three questions before your next session: "What do you want from these meetings? How often? How will we know it's helping?" If you don't, write down one person who might value 30 minutes of your time and offer it.

## "Never say" lines
- "Mentoring guarantees faster success."
- "Studies show mentees earn more." (Allen et al. looked at objective outcomes including pay; do not turn this into an income line.)
- "Just copy what I did with my clients."
- "Don't bother compliance with that, I'll tell you what to do."
- "Find a mentor or you'll fail."

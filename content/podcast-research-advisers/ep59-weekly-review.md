# Ep 59 — The weekly review

**Track:** The Agency Blueprint (AB)
**Theme:** 2 — The working week and your energy

**The adviser's real question:** "Every Monday I start from scratch and forget what I promised people last week. How do I stop things slipping?"

## Key points (in order)
1. A weekly review is a short, fixed meeting with yourself: same day, same time, roughly 30–45 minutes.
2. Look back first. What did I do? What did I promise clients, prospects or my team? What is still open?
3. Monitoring progress works. A large meta-analysis of experiments found that prompting people to track their progress helped them reach their goals, more so when progress was written down or shared.
4. Then look ahead. For each open loop, write a specific next step and put it in the calendar.
5. Making a plan helps your head as well as your list. In one set of studies, making a specific plan for an unfinished goal reduced intrusive thoughts about it.
6. Turn the most important next steps into "if-then" plans: "If it is Wednesday 2pm, then I call the three people who asked me to follow up."
7. Include a compliance check: notes filed, follow-ups logged, anything that needs your compliance team's eyes. The review is a habit, not a sign-off.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who blocks 4pm every Friday. She opens last week's calendar and her message threads and writes down every promise she made: "send that comparison of options", "call back after the school holidays", "introduce him to my colleague". Each one gets a next step and a date. She ticks what's done and notes what slipped and why. She shares a three-line summary with her accountability partner. On Monday she starts from a list, not a blank page.

## Your action this week
Book a recurring 30-minute "weekly review" in your calendar for the same slot every week. In the first one, answer three questions on paper: What did I finish? What did I promise that is still open? What are the three next steps, and when will I do each one?

## "Never say" lines
- "A weekly review guarantees you'll hit your targets."
- "Top producers all do this." (No source.)
- "Your review counts as your compliance check." (Say: "Check with your compliance team on what's required.")
- Any production, income or case-count figures as a "result" of reviewing.

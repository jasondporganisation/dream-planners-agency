# Ep 49 — Referrals as a habit, not a favour

**Track:** The Agency Blueprint (AB)
**Theme:** 1 — A pipeline without pressure
**The adviser's real question:** "Asking for referrals feels like begging. How do I make it a normal part of my work without making clients uncomfortable?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Reframe it. A referral is a client choosing to share something that helped them. Your job is to make that easy and safe, not to extract names.
2. Build the ask into your process at natural points (for example, at the end of a review where the client said it was useful), so it isn't a one-off favour you have to work up courage for.
3. Ask for an introduction, not a list. The best route is the client asking their friend first, then the friend contacting you or agreeing to be contacted.
4. Personal data rules apply to the friend's details. Under PDPC guidance, when you receive someone's data from a third party, check that the person agreed to it being shared. When you first contact them, say who gave you their details and confirm they agreed.
5. Paid introducer arrangements are a different thing and are regulated. MAS says only financial institutions may appoint introducers; individual representatives may not set up their own introducer arrangements. Check with your compliance team before offering anyone anything for a referral.
6. Thank people without strings. A thank-you note is fine; check your firm's rules on gifts.
7. Habits take time. A small repeated routine, tied to a fixed point in your process, tends to become automatic with repetition, and missing once is not fatal.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who used to ask for referrals only when her diary was empty, and it always came out sounding desperate. She changes one thing. At the end of every annual review, if the client says it was useful, she asks one question: "Is there anyone you think would value a conversation like this? If so, would you mention me to them first, and let me know if they're happy to hear from me?" She logs who agreed and what the friend was told. When she calls, she opens with who referred her and checks the friend is comfortable. Some months nothing comes of it. The question still gets asked, every review.

## Your action this week
Choose one fixed point in your client process where a referral question fits naturally. Write the exact words you will use, including the "ask them first" step. Run it past your compliance team, then use it at every matching meeting for the next month.

## "Never say" lines (episode-specific)
- "Give me five names before I leave."
- "I'll give you a voucher / cash / a gift for every referral." (Check firm rules and MAS rules; individual reps cannot set up introducer arrangements.)
- "Just pass me their number, I'll take it from there." (Without checking the friend agreed.)
- "Don't tell them I got their number from you."
- "Referrals mean you'll never need to prospect again."

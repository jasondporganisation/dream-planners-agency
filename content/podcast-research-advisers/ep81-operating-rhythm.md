# Ep 81 — The Team Operating Rhythm: Meetings, One-on-Ones, Written Standards

**Track:** The Agency Blueprint (AB)
**Theme:** 4 — Building and leading a team
**The adviser's real question:** "How many meetings should my team actually have, and how do I stop them eating everyone's week?"

## Key points (in order)
1. A rhythm is a small set of repeating meetings with a clear purpose, plus written standards so not everything needs a meeting.
2. Meeting time is not free. Perlow, Hadley and Eun note research reporting that executives spend nearly 23 hours a week in meetings, up from under 10 in the 1960s. That figure is about executives in general, not advisers.
3. Quality matters as much as quantity. Rogelberg and colleagues found that how employees experienced meetings, and whether meetings felt effective, was strongly related to job attitudes and well-being.
4. Meetings shape how people feel about the whole job. In a later study, satisfaction with meetings predicted overall job satisfaction, even after accounting for other parts of the job.
5. Suggested rhythm: one short weekly team meeting (with an agenda and a time limit), regular one-on-ones the team member partly owns, and one longer monthly or quarterly review. Cancel anything without a purpose.
6. Write the standards down: what a complete client file looks like, response times, and how to escalate a concern. A study of a 19-item surgical safety checklist in eight hospitals found death and complication rates fell after it was introduced. That is a very different setting, but it shows how short written standards can support consistency.
7. Review the rhythm every quarter: ask the team which meeting they'd drop and which they'd keep.

## Hypothetical example
Picture a leader whose team has four standing meetings a week and still feels out of sync. She asks everyone to rate each meeting "keep, change or drop". Two meetings are merged into one 30-minute weekly huddle with a fixed agenda: wins, blockers, one learning point. One-on-ones move to every two weeks, and the team member sets the first item. She writes a one-page "how we work" note covering file standards, response times and who to call about a compliance question. After a month, people say the week feels calmer, and fewer questions come up as one-off messages.

## Your action this week
List every recurring team meeting with its purpose in one line. Ask your team to mark each one keep, change or drop, and act on at least one "drop".

## "Never say" lines
- "Advisers spend 23 hours a week in meetings." (The figure is about executives.)
- "A checklist will stop all mistakes." (The surgery study was in hospitals and a before/after design.)
- "More meetings mean more accountability."
- "This rhythm is how top producers make more."
- "Our written standards replace compliance review."

# Ep 69 — Documentation as protection

**Track:** The Agency Blueprint (AB)
**Theme:** 3 — Compliance, ethics and hard conversations

**The adviser's real question:** "What do I actually need to write down, and why does it matter so much?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Good notes protect the client first. They show the advice fit the client. They also protect you when memories differ years later.
2. The legal anchor is the reasonable basis for a recommendation. Your file is how you show you had one.
3. MAS expects the basis of recommendation to be fully documented: the client's objectives and needs, the explanation of the recommendation, and the possible risks.
4. MAS notices set specific rules. FAA-N16 covers recommendations, including documentation and record keeping. FAA-N03 covers information you must give clients, including remuneration and conflicts. Your FI's forms are built on these.
5. When a complaint is reviewed, the FI looks at the sales process and what was said, in light of the client's profile. Clear notes make that review fair to everyone.
6. Write as if a stranger will read it in five years. Use plain words, dates, what the client said, what you advised and what they decided.
7. Same-day filing beats perfect filing. Notes written days later are thinner and easier to challenge.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser who meets a client who changes her mind twice in one meeting. In the past, he would have written "client agreed to plan B". This time, he notes what she first asked for, the concern he raised, the options they discussed, and her final choice in her own words, with the date. Three years later, the client asks why the plan looks the way it does. The answer is in the file, and the conversation stays calm.

## Your action this week
Pull one of your files from six months ago. Read it as a stranger would. Could they tell what the client wanted, what you recommended and why? Note one thing you'll add to every file from now on.

## "Never say" lines (episode-specific)
- "If it's not written down, it didn't happen, so leave out anything awkward."
- "Backdate it, nobody checks."
- "Good notes mean you'll win any complaint."
- "Keep records for X years" (not verified here). Say: "follow your FI's retention policy."
- Never suggest editing or "tidying up" notes after a complaint arrives.

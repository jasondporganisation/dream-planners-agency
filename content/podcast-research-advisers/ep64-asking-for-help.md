# Ep 64 — Asking for help

**Track:** The Resilient Advisor (RA)
**Theme:** 2 — The working week and your energy

**The adviser's real question:** "I'm the one people come to for help. How do I ask for it myself without feeling like a failure?"

> **Not medical advice.** If you are struggling, you can call or message **National Mindline 1771** any time (24/7, anonymous if you wish): call 1771, WhatsApp +65 6669 1771, or webchat at mindline.sg. **SOS**: 24-hour hotline 1767, CareText (WhatsApp) 9151 1767. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call **995** or go to the nearest A&E. Check all numbers on the official pages before recording.

## Key points (in order)
1. Advisers spend their days being the steady one. That can make it harder to admit you need support.
2. You're not alone in finding it hard to seek help. In Singapore's 2016 national mental health study, most people with a mental disorder in the past year had not received treatment.
3. The same research found that people with higher education and people in work were *more* likely not to get treatment. Working professionals are not immune to this pattern.
4. Help comes in layers: a trusted friend or leader; a peer or mentor; your GP; a counsellor; a national support line.
5. Singapore now has a single, round-the-clock entry point: National Mindline 1771, launched in June 2025, by phone, WhatsApp or webchat, and you can stay anonymous.
6. Asking early is easier than asking late. You don't need to be in crisis to call.
7. Practise the ask: "I've not been myself lately. Can we talk this week?" is enough.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an agency leader who is the first person his team calls when a case goes wrong. After a hard few months at home, he's sleeping badly and snapping at people. He tells himself leaders should cope. One evening he sends a WhatsApp to Mindline 1771 without giving his name, just to talk it through. That gives him the push to see his GP and tell a peer leader what's going on. He also tells his team he's taking a Friday afternoon off for himself, which quietly gives them permission to do the same.

## Your action this week
Save National Mindline 1771 and SOS 1767 in your phone now. Then write down the names of two people you could call if you had a bad week. Send one of them a short message just to stay in touch.

## "Never say" lines
- "Call this number and you'll feel better." (No promised outcomes.)
- "Most advisers struggle with mental health." (No source about advisers.)
- "You don't need a doctor, just talk to a friend."
- Any diagnosis, or naming medications or treatments.
- "Strong people don't need help" or "real leaders handle it themselves."
- IMH's old helpline number, or any number not checked on an official page that week.

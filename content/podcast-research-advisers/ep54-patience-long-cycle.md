# Ep 54 — Patience in a long cycle

**Track:** The Resilient Advisor (RA)
**Theme:** 1 — A pipeline without pressure
**The adviser's real question:** "Some people take months to decide. How do I stay patient and keep my energy up without pushing them?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Long decisions are normal for important choices. A slow "yes" or a slow "no" is the client's right, not a problem to fix.
2. Patience is easier when your attention is on what you control: your activity, your follow-up rhythm, and your learning, not the date a client decides.
3. "Grit" is a popular idea, but be careful with it. A large meta-analysis found grit only moderately related to performance and retention, and strongly overlapping with conscientiousness. The "perseverance of effort" part looked more useful than the rest.
4. Persistence is not the same as refusing to let go. Research on if-then planning includes helping people disengage from failing courses of action. Knowing when to close a file kindly is part of patience.
5. Energy comes from recovery, not just effort. Researchers describe four recovery experiences away from work: psychological detachment, relaxation, mastery (learning something new), and control over your own time.
6. Spread your pipeline so no single person's decision carries your whole month. (Host's view.)
7. Mark small progress. Notice conversations held and lessons learned, not only decisions made.

Notes for the script team:
- None of these studies is about advisers or sales cycles. Present them as general research on persistence, planning and recovery.
- Do not say "grit predicts success". Fact 2 is the more cautious, later picture; if you mention fact 1, mention fact 2 straight after.
- Fact 4 is a measurement study. It describes kinds of recovery; it does not prove any one activity restores energy.

## Hypothetical example
Picture an adviser with a prospective client who has been "thinking about it" for four months. He feels the urge to send another nudge, and resents feeling it. He steps back. He has agreed a check-in date with the client, so he leaves it until then. Meanwhile he puts his effort into things he controls: two new conversations this week and one skill he's learning. He keeps Sunday evenings free of work messages and goes back to a hobby he'd dropped. When the check-in comes, he asks a simple question: "Is this still something you want to look at, or shall I close the file for now?" Either answer is fine.

## Your action this week
List every open conversation older than 60 days. For each, write the agreed next contact date. If there isn't one, plan one respectful question that lets the person continue or close. Then block one evening this week that is fully off work.

## "Never say" lines (episode-specific)
- "Never give up on a prospect."
- "Grit is the secret to success."
- "Every long cycle eventually closes." (Implies a guaranteed outcome.)
- "Create a deadline so they decide faster."
- "Rest is for when you've hit target."

### 84 · An 11.11 game plan

**Theme:** Budgeting · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** 11.11 is coming. Decide what you'll buy before the countdown starts.

**Post:**

Big sale days are designed to make you decide fast: countdown timers, "only 3 left", flash deals at midnight.

The way to beat that is to decide slowly, in advance.

Before the sale:
1. **Make a list** of things you actually need or have been waiting to buy.
2. **Set a total budget** for the day, and stick to it.
3. **Check normal prices** now, so you know if a "deal" really is one.
4. **Remove saved cards** from shopping apps, so every purchase needs a moment of effort.

During the sale, buy only what's on your list. If something else tempts you, add it to next month's list and see if you still want it.

A sale only saves money if you were going to buy the thing anyway.

### 85 · Year-end bonus and the CPF ceilings

**Theme:** CPF · **Flag:** ⚑ Uses 2027 ceilings (confirmed unchanged on 1 Jan 2027). Re-verify on cpf.gov.sg before posting.

**Hook:** Getting a year-end bonus? Here's how CPF ceilings apply to it.

**Post:**

CPF contributions are capped. Two ceilings matter:

**Ordinary Wage ceiling:** $8,000 a month in 2027, the same as 2026. CPF is only paid on monthly ordinary wages up to this amount.

**Annual salary ceiling:** $102,000 a year. The ceiling for Additional Wages, such as bonuses, is $102,000 minus the total ordinary wages that were subject to CPF in the year.

What this means: for higher earners, part of a bonus may not attract CPF contributions, because the annual ceiling has already been partly used up by monthly salary.

If your bonus payslip looks different from what you expected, this is often worth checking first. CPF Board's page on wages explains what counts.

### 86 · Working past 65? CPF still contributes

**Theme:** CPF · **Flag:** ⚑ Uses the 2027 rate table (rates for these bands unchanged on 1 Jan 2027). Re-verify on cpf.gov.sg before posting.

**Hook:** Still working after 65? Your CPF doesn't stop.

**Post:**

Many people don't realise that CPF contributions continue for older workers, at lower rates.

For Singapore Citizens and third-year-onwards PRs in the private sector, earning above $750 a month, the 2027 rates are:
• **Above 65 to 70:** 16.5% in total (employer 9%, employee 7.5%)
• **Above 70:** 12.5% in total (employer 7.5%, employee 5%)

The new rate applies from the first day of the month after your 65th or 70th birthday.

Since 1 Jul 2026, the re-employment age is 69 (MOM). So more people may be working into their late 60s, and their CPF keeps growing while they do.

If a parent is working past 65, they may be glad to know this.

### 87 · The three-document year-end check

**Theme:** Wills, LPA and nominations · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Before the year ends, check three documents. That's it.

**Post:**

You don't need a full estate plan overhaul every year. But a quick check of three documents goes a long way.

**1. Your will.** Do you have one? Did you marry after writing it? (Marriage revokes it.) Are the guardians and executor still right?

**2. Your LPA.** Have you made one? If so, are the people you chose still the right ones, and do they know?

**3. Your nominations.** Is your CPF nomination up to date? (Marriage revokes it too; divorce doesn't.) Do your insurance policies have nominations that match your wishes?

If all three are fine, great. You've confirmed it in fifteen minutes.

If one isn't, you've found your first task for the new year.

### 88 · What overrides a will?

**Theme:** Wills, LPA and nominations · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** You wrote a will leaving everything to one person. Some things may not follow it.

**Post:**

A will is important, but it doesn't control everything. According to MoneySense, some arrangements take precedence over a will:
• **CPF nominations.** CPF savings aren't part of your estate and can't be left by will.
• **Right of survivorship.** For jointly owned assets held this way, the surviving owner takes over.
• **Trust nominations on insurance policies.** These take priority over a will.

And for revocable insurance nominations, the insurer pays according to whichever is the latest: the nomination or the will.

So the safest approach is to look at your will, your CPF nomination, how your property is held and your policy nominations together. If they point in different directions, the result may not be what you intended.

### 89 · The value of a stay-at-home parent

**Theme:** Family protection concepts · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** "She doesn't earn an income, so we don't need to plan for her." Think again.

**Post:**

When families think about protection, they often focus on the person with the salary. But a parent who stays home is doing work the family relies on every day: childcare, school runs, cooking, managing the home, caring for grandparents.

If that parent were ill or no longer there, much of that work wouldn't disappear. Someone would need to do it, or the family would need to pay for help.

So when you think about family protection, include everyone whose contribution the family depends on, paid or not.

It's also a good reminder to recognise that work properly. It's often invisible, until it isn't being done.

To every parent holding the home together: thank you.

### 90 · Planning for long life, one habit at a time

**Theme:** Retirement habits · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Retirement planning isn't one big decision. It's a few small ones, repeated.

**Post:**

People often put off retirement planning because it feels huge. But most of it is small habits done consistently over many years.

Habits that add up:
• **Look at your CPF statement** a few times a year.
• **Save automatically** on payday.
• **Raise your savings** a little when your income rises.
• **Look after your health.** It affects both your costs and how long you can work.
• **Keep learning.** Skills help you stay employable for longer, if you want to.
• **Review once a year,** on a fixed date.

None of these needs a perfect plan. They just need starting.

Pick the one you're not doing yet, and start it this month.

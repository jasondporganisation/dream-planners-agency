### 67 · A birthday money ritual

**Theme:** Retirement habits · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Every birthday, I ask myself the same five money questions.

**Post:**

Birthdays are a natural checkpoint. Another year older, another year closer to the life you're planning for.

Here's a short ritual you can do each birthday:
1. **What did I save or pay off this year?** Celebrate it.
2. **What changed in my life?** Job, family, home, health.
3. **Are my documents current?** Will, LPA, nominations.
4. **Is my retirement picture clearer than last year?** Even a little.
5. **What's one thing I'll do before my next birthday?**

Ten minutes, once a year, on a date you'll never forget.

It sounds small. But done every year for twenty or thirty years, it becomes one of the most useful money habits you'll ever have.

When's your birthday? Put it in your calendar now.

### 68 · At 55, the Special Account closes

**Theme:** CPF · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** "My Special Account will still be there at 60." Not since 2025.

**Post:**

Since 2025, CPF closes your Special Account when you turn 55.

This is one of those changes that older articles, and older relatives, may not reflect. So if someone tells you what "used to happen" to the Special Account after 55, it's worth checking the current CPF page.

What this means for you: when you approach 55, your CPF accounts work differently from how they did in your 40s. CPF Board explains what happens to your savings at 55 on its "Reaching 55" page.

A good habit as you approach that birthday: read CPF's milestone pages a year or two before, so nothing comes as a surprise.

Know someone turning 55 soon? Share this with them.

### 69 · CPF LIFE pays out for life, and doesn't vanish at death

**Theme:** Retirement habits · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** "If I die early, CPF keeps my money." That's a common worry. It's not how CPF LIFE works.

**Post:**

Some people hesitate about CPF LIFE because they worry what happens if they pass away early.

According to CPF Board, all three CPF LIFE plans pay monthly for as long as you live. And when you pass away, any remaining premium balance goes to your loved ones.

That remaining balance is also covered by your CPF nomination. So if there's someone specific you'd want to receive it, a nomination helps make that clear.

The idea is simple: CPF LIFE is designed so you don't outlive your income, and your family isn't left out if you pass on early.

Worth sharing with a parent who's been worried about this.

### 70 · The Full Retirement Sum is a benchmark, not your number

**Theme:** CPF · **Flag:** ⚑ Uses the 2027 cohort Full Retirement Sum (effective 1 Jan 2027). Re-verify on cpf.gov.sg before posting.

**Hook:** "I need $228,200 to retire." No. That's not what the number means.

**Post:**

For members turning 55 in 2027, the CPF Full Retirement Sum is $228,200.

It's easy to read that as "the amount I need to retire". It isn't. The Full Retirement Sum is a CPF benchmark. It isn't a personal calculation of what your retirement will cost.

Your own number depends on:
• The lifestyle you want
• Whether you'll still be working part-time
• Your housing situation
• Your health, and the people you support
• Other savings you have

So treat the Full Retirement Sum as one reference point, not a target someone else set for you.

### 71 · The care conversation

**Theme:** Family protection concepts · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Who would look after you if you needed daily help? Have you asked?

**Post:**

Most families plan for hospital stays. Fewer plan for what can come after: needing help with daily life for a long time, at home or in care.

It's a hard topic, which is why it's often avoided until it's urgent. Some questions to start with:
• If I needed daily help, where would I want to be cared for?
• Who in the family could realistically help, and who couldn't?
• How would the cost be shared?
• What support schemes exist? (Check the official government pages.)
• Have I written down what I'd want?

Talking about it doesn't make it more likely. It makes it more manageable.

If your parents are getting older, this might be the conversation to have this year, gently, and with all the siblings involved.

### 72 · Spend in line with what you value

**Theme:** Budgeting · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** A budget isn't about spending less. It's about spending on purpose.

**Post:**

Two people can earn the same and spend the same, and one feels in control while the other feels stretched. Often the difference is whether their spending matches what they care about.

Try this:
1. Write down three things that genuinely matter to you. Family time, health, travel, learning, giving, whatever's true.
2. Look at last month's spending.
3. Mark each big item: does it support one of those three?

You'll likely find some spending that matches and some that doesn't. The ones that don't are where you have room to change, without feeling deprived.

Spending money on things you value isn't a failure of budgeting. That's the point of it.

What are your three?

### 73 · What I can, and can't, call myself

**Theme:** Career and adviser life · **Flag:** Note for Brand Guardian: this post quotes the restricted terms "Financial Adviser" and "independent" only to explain that Jason does not use them (MAS FAQ Q10, Q16).

**Hook:** You may notice I never call myself a "financial adviser". There's a reason.

**Post:**

Under MAS rules, representatives like me can't call ourselves "Financial Advisers" on our own. The term can be used together with "representative". Titles like "financial planner" aren't restricted.

The word "independent" is restricted too. MAS allows it only for firms with no product bias or links to product providers. A tied representative of an insurer doesn't meet that test, so I don't use it.

You can check any representative on the MAS Register of Representatives. Each has a unique number.

I think these rules are good for everyone. They help people understand who they're talking to, and what that person can and can't offer.

Before you speak to anyone about money, it's worth checking the Register.

### 75 · Learning is part of the job

**Theme:** Career and adviser life · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** In this job, what you knew last year may already be out of date.

**Post:**

Every January, CPF figures change. MediShield Life rules change from time to time. MAS updates its guidelines. Even the rules on how I can post online changed in 2026.

So learning isn't optional. It's how I make sure what I tell people is correct.

How I keep up:
• I check official sources directly: cpf.gov.sg, moh.gov.sg, mas.gov.sg, moneysense.gov.sg.
• I date-stamp the figures I use, so I know when to re-check them.
• I'm happy to say "let me confirm and come back to you" instead of guessing.
• I learn from colleagues and mentors.

If you enjoy learning and explaining, this can be a rewarding career. If you'd rather learn something once and be done, it may not suit you.

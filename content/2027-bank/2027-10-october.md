### 76 · How many Singaporeans have an LPA?

**Theme:** Wills, LPA and nominations · **Flag:** Date-sensitive. MSF figures as of 20 Feb 2026; check for newer data before posting.

**Hook:** About 1 in 7 Singapore citizens has made an LPA. Are you one of them?

**Post:**

According to MSF, 404,000 citizens had made a Lasting Power of Attorney as of 20 Feb 2026. That's about 1 in 7.

Among seniors over 65, the figure was 197,000, about 1 in 4.

So most adults haven't made one yet. Often it's not a decision. It's simply something people haven't got round to.

An LPA isn't only for older people. Accidents and sudden illness can happen at any age. It lets you choose who can make decisions for you if you lose mental capacity, instead of leaving it to a court process.

For Singapore Citizens, the Form 1 application fee is free (a Certificate Issuer fee still applies).

Could this be the month you get it done?

### 77 · Two kinds of decisions in an LPA

**Theme:** Wills, LPA and nominations · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** An LPA can cover two very different parts of your life.

**Post:**

When you make a Lasting Power of Attorney, you're appointing one or more people (your "Donees") to act for you if you lose mental capacity. You must be 21 or over to make one.

The powers fall into two areas:

**Personal welfare:** decisions about your personal life and care.

**Property and affairs:** decisions about your money and property.

You can give powers in one area or both, and to the same person or different people. Some people choose a spouse for one and a financially organised sibling for the other.

Think about who suits each type of decision, then talk to them before you apply.

### 78 · Choosing your Donee: questions to ask

**Theme:** Wills, LPA and nominations · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** The hardest part of an LPA isn't the form. It's choosing the person.

**Post:**

The person you appoint in your LPA may one day make important decisions for you. So it's worth thinking carefully.

Questions to ask yourself:
• Do I trust them completely, with money and with my wellbeing?
• Are they organised and level-headed under stress?
• Do they live nearby, or could they act from where they are?
• Would they be willing? Have I actually asked them?
• Would my other family members accept this choice?
• Should I name a replacement in case they can't act?

And one practical point: once your LPA application is submitted, MSF says there's no refund. So make your choices and check every detail before you submit.

It's a big decision. Take your time.

### 79 · What an executor actually does

**Theme:** Wills, LPA and nominations · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Being named someone's executor is an honour. It's also a lot of work.

**Post:**

An executor is the person named in a will to carry out its wishes. It sounds simple, but it's a real job, often done while grieving.

It can involve:
• Finding and gathering documents
• Dealing with banks and other institutions
• Settling bills and debts
• Distributing what's left according to the will
• Keeping the family informed

So when choosing an executor, think about who is organised, patient, trusted by the family, and willing. Ask them first. Nobody should find out at the funeral.

And if someone asks you to be their executor, it's fine to ask questions before saying yes: where things are kept, who else is involved, and what they'd want.

### 80 · ICU and ward limits under MediShield Life

**Theme:** MediShield Life and hospitalisation basics · **Flag:** Date-sensitive. Claim limits apply from 1 Jun 2026; check the MOH benefits page before posting.

**Hook:** A day in ICU can cost a lot. Here's how MediShield Life's daily limits work.

**Post:**

MediShield Life pays daily claim limits for hospital stays. From MOH's benefits page (from 1 Jun 2026):
• **Normal ward:** up to $830 a day, plus an extra $800 a day for the first 2 days
• **ICU:** up to $5,140 a day

These are the maximum claimable amounts per day. Your share then depends on the deductible and co-insurance.

Knowing the daily limits helps explain why a long stay, or a stay in a higher ward class, can leave a bigger bill than expected.

### 81 · Comparison is a budget killer

**Theme:** Budgeting · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Your feed shows other people's highlights. Your bank account pays for yours.

**Post:**

Social media is full of holidays, renovations, cars and restaurant dinners. It's easy to feel behind.

But you're comparing your full picture with someone else's brightest moments. You don't see their debts, their stress, or what they gave up.

Some ways to protect your budget:
• **Notice the feeling.** "I want that" after scrolling is a signal, not a plan.
• **Mute accounts** that consistently make you feel you need more.
• **Follow your own goals.** Write them somewhere you'll see them.
• **Celebrate your own progress,** even if nobody else sees it.

Money is personal. The only fair comparison is with where you were last year.

What's one account you could mute this week?

### 82 · Life event? Time for a review

**Theme:** Family protection concepts · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Your plan was right for the life you had. Is it right for the one you have now?

**Post:**

Family plans go out of date quietly. Not because they were wrong, but because life moved on.

Moments that should trigger a review:
• Marriage (remember: it revokes your will and CPF nomination)
• A new baby
• Buying a home or taking on a big loan
• A new job, a career break or self-employment
• A parent starting to need care
• Divorce or separation
• A serious health diagnosis in the family
• Children starting to support themselves

A review doesn't have to mean changing anything. Sometimes it just confirms you're still on track. Either way, you'll know.

Which of these has happened to you in the last two years?

### 83 · Is this career right for you? An honest checklist

**Theme:** Career and adviser life · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Thinking about becoming an adviser? Ask yourself these questions first.

**Post:**

I'm often asked whether someone should join this industry. My honest answer: it depends on you.

Questions worth asking yourself:
• Do I enjoy explaining things to people, again and again?
• Can I handle hearing "no" without taking it personally?
• Am I comfortable with income that isn't fixed?
• Will I manage my own time without a boss?
• Am I willing to keep learning every year?
• Can I follow strict rules, even when they slow me down?
• Would I put someone's needs ahead of a sale?

If most answers are yes, it could be worth a conversation. If not, that's useful to know too.

There's no shame in deciding it isn't for you. Choosing well matters more than choosing fast.

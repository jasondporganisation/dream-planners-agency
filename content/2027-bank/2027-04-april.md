### 26 · Your MediShield Life premium can come from MediSave

**Theme:** MediShield Life and hospitalisation basics · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Do you pay cash for MediShield Life? Most people don't need to.

**Post:**

MediShield Life premiums are fully payable by MediSave. That includes using a family member's MediSave, for example a parent paying for a child, or an adult child helping a parent.

On top of that, there's support for those who need it:
• Premium subsidies of up to 60% for lower- to middle-income households who qualify.
• Pioneer Generation and Merdeka Generation subsidies for those cohorts.
• Additional Premium Support, so that no one loses cover because they can't afford the premium.

The aim is that every Singapore Citizen and PR keeps this basic cover for life.

If you're not sure how your family's premiums are being paid, it's worth a quick look in your CPF account.

### 27 · Why your MediShield Life premium changes with age

**Theme:** MediShield Life and hospitalisation basics · **Flag:** Date-sensitive. Premiums are for policies starting or renewing on or after 1 Apr 2025; check MOH's table before posting.

**Hook:** Your MediShield Life premium is set by one thing you can't change: your age.

**Post:**

MediShield Life premiums go by "age next birthday", in bands. Some examples from MOH's table, before subsidies, including 9% GST:
• Ages 21–30: $295 a year
• Ages 31–40: $503 a year
• Ages 41–50: $637 a year
• Ages 51–60: $903 a year

Premiums are higher in the older bands, so your premium steps up as you move from one band to the next.

Two things to remember:
1. These are before subsidies. Many households pay less.
2. They can be paid fully by MediSave.

So when you're planning MediSave use over the long term, remember that premiums for you, and perhaps your parents or children, will come out of it every year.

### 28 · What an Integrated Shield Plan is (and isn't)

**Theme:** MediShield Life and hospitalisation basics · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** "Shield plan" and "MediShield Life" sound similar. They aren't the same thing.

**Post:**

**MediShield Life** is the national scheme. Every Singapore Citizen and PR has it automatically. It's sized for subsidised treatment in public hospitals (Class B2/C wards).

An **Integrated Shield Plan** is offered by private insurers. It's made of two parts: the MediShield Life part, plus an additional private part for higher ward classes or private hospitals.

Three facts worth knowing:
• You can be covered by only one Integrated Shield Plan at a time.
• If the private part excludes a pre-existing condition, the MediShield Life part still covers it.
• Part of the private premium can come from MediSave, up to set limits.

So an Integrated Shield Plan doesn't replace MediShield Life. It builds on top of it.

### 29 · MediSave limits and riders: what's paid in cash

**Theme:** MediShield Life and hospitalisation basics · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** "My hospital plan is all paid by MediSave." Is it, though?

**Post:**

For an Integrated Shield Plan, MediSave can pay for the private part only up to the Additional Withdrawal Limits, set by age next birthday:
• 40 and below: $300 a year
• 41–70: $600 a year
• 71 and above: $900 a year

Anything above that is paid in cash.

Riders are different. Rider premiums must be paid fully in cash. MediSave can't be used for them at all.

Why it matters: cash premiums come out of your monthly budget, and they can change over time. It's worth knowing how much of your family's hospital cover is cash today, and how that may change over the years.

Check your annual statements and add the cash part to your budget.

### 30 · New rider rules, one year on

**Theme:** MediShield Life and hospitalisation basics · **Flag:** Date-sensitive (rules from 1 Apr 2026). No 1 Jan 2027 dependency.

**Hook:** Bought a new hospital plan rider since April 2026? The rules changed.

**Post:**

MOH introduced new requirements for Integrated Shield Plan riders sold from 1 Apr 2026.

For new riders:
• They may not cover the minimum deductible. That's between $1,500 and $3,500 a year, depending on ward class.
• The cap on co-payments was raised from at least $3,000 to at least $6,000 a year.
• The 5% minimum co-payment, in place for new riders since 1 Apr 2019, is unchanged.

What it means: with a newer rider, you should expect to pay some of every hospital bill yourself. That's by design, to help keep private health insurance sustainable as healthcare costs rise.

Riders sold between 27 Nov 2025 and 31 Mar 2026 must move to compliant riders by the first renewal after 1 Apr 2028.

### 31 · Track one month, honestly

**Theme:** Budgeting · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** You can't fix a budget you've never seen.

**Post:**

Most people have a rough idea of what they spend. Most rough ideas are wrong.

This month, try tracking everything for one month. Every coffee, every ride, every top-up.

Some tips that make it stick:
• Use whatever is easiest: a notes app, a spreadsheet, or your banking app's categories.
• Don't judge while tracking. Just record.
• Sort it into a few groups at the end: essentials, family, lifestyle, goals.
• Look for one surprise. There's almost always one.

At the end of the month, you'll know where your money actually goes. That's far more useful than any budget template, because it starts from your real life.

Then decide one change. Just one.

### 32 · Three "what if" questions for every family

**Theme:** Family protection concepts · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Three questions most families have never said out loud.

**Post:**

You don't need a spreadsheet to start thinking about family protection. You need three honest questions.

**1. What if one of us were in hospital for a while?**
Who pays the bills? Who looks after the kids? How much would come out of pocket?

**2. What if one of us couldn't work for a long time?**
How long could the household manage on one income, or none?

**3. What if one of us passed away?**
Would the family stay in the same home? Who would make decisions? Do we know where everything is?

You don't need all the answers today. Just talking about it puts you ahead of most households.

Pick one question and bring it up this week, gently.

### 33 · What nobody told me about my first year in this job

**Theme:** Career and adviser life · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** If you're starting out as an adviser, here's what I wish I'd known.

**Post:**

My first year in this industry taught me things no training slide did.

• **Learning never stops.** The rules change every year. You have to keep up so what you tell people is correct.
• **Rejection isn't personal.** Most people aren't ready to talk about planning. That's normal.
• **Your reputation is built slowly.** By keeping small promises, like calling back when you said you would.
• **Compliance protects you.** It feels slow at first. Later you realise it protects both you and the people you serve.
• **You need support.** A good mentor and team make a huge difference.

It's a demanding career, and it isn't right for everyone. But if you enjoy helping people and you're patient, it can be meaningful.

New advisers: what's surprised you most so far?

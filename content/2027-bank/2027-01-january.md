### 01 · CPF rates for workers above 55 to 60 change today

**Theme:** CPF · **Flag:** ⚑ Depends on 1 Jan 2027 rule change. Re-verify on cpf.gov.sg before posting.

**Hook:** Aged above 55 to 60 and still working? Check your January payslip.

**Post:**

From 1 Jan 2027, total CPF contributions for workers above 55 to 60 rise from 34% to 35.5% of wages.

Here's the split:
• Employer: 16% → 16.5%
• Employee: 18% → 19%

These rates are for Singapore Citizens and third-year-onwards PRs in the private sector, earning more than $750 a month.

What it means in practice: a little more of your pay goes into CPF each month, and your take-home pay may be slightly lower. The extra goes towards your retirement savings.

If you're in this age group, look at your first 2027 payslip and compare it with December's. If the numbers don't match what you expect, ask your HR team.

Save this for a parent or colleague who's in this age band.

### 02 · Above 60 to 65: a smaller change, but still a change

**Theme:** CPF · **Flag:** ⚑ Depends on 1 Jan 2027 rule change. Re-verify on cpf.gov.sg before posting.

**Hook:** Working in your early 60s? Your CPF rate moved on 1 January.

**Post:**

From 1 Jan 2027, total CPF contributions for workers above 60 to 65 go from 25% to 26% of wages.

• Employer: 12.5% → 13%
• Employee: 12.5% → 13%

Not every age group changes. For 55 and below, above 65 to 70, and above 70, the rates stay the same in 2027.

One detail people miss: a new age band starts from the first day of the month after your 55th, 60th, 65th or 70th birthday. So if you have a birthday milestone this year, your rate may change partway through the year, not just in January.

A simple habit: when your rate changes, check one payslip to see the new split.

### 03 · Where does the extra CPF money go?

**Theme:** CPF · **Flag:** ⚑ Depends on 1 Jan 2027 rule change. Re-verify on cpf.gov.sg before posting.

**Hook:** Higher CPF rates for older workers in 2027. So where does the extra go?

**Post:**

From 1 Jan 2027, CPF contribution rates rise for workers above 55 to 65.

The extra contributions don't land just anywhere. CPF Board says they go to your Retirement Account first, up to the Full Retirement Sum. After that, they go to your Ordinary Account.

Why does this matter? For many people in this age group, the change adds to savings set aside for retirement, not to money you can spend today.

If you're 55 or older, it helps to know roughly where your Retirement Account stands against the Full Retirement Sum for your cohort. You can see this when you log in to your CPF account.

### 04 · Turning 55 in 2027? Your retirement sums are new

**Theme:** CPF · **Flag:** ⚑ Depends on 1 Jan 2027 figures (2027 cohort retirement sums). Re-verify on cpf.gov.sg before posting.

**Hook:** If you turn 55 this year, last year's CPF numbers don't apply to you.

**Post:**

CPF retirement sums are set for each cohort, by the year you turn 55.

For members turning 55 in 2027:
• Basic Retirement Sum: $114,100
• Full Retirement Sum: $228,200
• Enhanced Retirement Sum: $456,400

For comparison, the 2026 cohort's figures were $110,200, $220,400 and $440,800. Since 2025, the Enhanced sum is 4 times the Basic sum.

Two things to keep in mind:
1. These are CPF benchmarks. They are not a personal figure for how much you need to retire.
2. They are set by birth year. A friend who turned 55 last year has different numbers from you.

If your 55th birthday is this year, it's a good year to look at your CPF statement properly.

### 05 · Myth: "Everyone's CPF goes up in 2027"

**Theme:** CPF · **Flag:** ⚑ Depends on 1 Jan 2027 rule change. Re-verify on cpf.gov.sg before posting.

**Hook:** "My CPF deduction is going up this year." Maybe. Maybe not.

**Post:**

You may have heard that CPF rates changed on 1 Jan 2027. That's true, but only for some workers.

What changed: total rates for workers above 55 to 60 (34% → 35.5%) and above 60 to 65 (25% → 26%).

What didn't change in 2027:
• Rates for those 55 and below stay at 37%.
• Rates for those above 65 stay the same.
• The Ordinary Wage ceiling stays at $8,000 a month, the same as 2026.

So if you're 35 and your take-home pay looks different this month, CPF rates probably aren't the reason. A bonus, an increment or a change in allowances is more likely. Your payslip breakdown will tell you.

Before repeating a money headline, check which group it actually covers.

### 06 · The one-page "where everything is" list

**Theme:** Wills, LPA and nominations · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** If something happened to you tomorrow, would your family know where to look?

**Post:**

Most families don't struggle because there's no plan. They struggle because nobody knows where the plan is.

A New Year task that takes under an hour: write one page that lists where your important things are. Not the details, just the locations.

• Your will, if you have one, and who has a copy
• Your Lasting Power of Attorney, if you've made one
• Whether you've made a CPF nomination
• Your insurance policies and who to contact
• Bank accounts (bank names only, never passwords)
• Who your key contacts are: a lawyer, an executor, an adviser

Keep it somewhere safe and tell one trusted person it exists.

It's not a legal document. It's a map. And a map saves your family a lot of searching on the worst week of their lives.

### 07 · The January subscription audit

**Theme:** Budgeting · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** How many things are you paying for every month that you forgot about?

**Post:**

January is a good time for a small, boring, useful task: the subscription audit.

Here's how:
1. Open your last three months of bank and card statements.
2. Highlight every charge that repeats: streaming, apps, cloud storage, gym, delivery memberships.
3. For each one, ask: "Did I use this last month? Would I sign up again today?"
4. Cancel the ones that get two "no"s.
5. Put the renewal dates of the rest in your calendar.

The aim isn't to cut everything you enjoy. It's to make sure every recurring payment is one you actually chose, not one you're paying out of habit.

Whatever you free up, give it a job straight away, so it doesn't quietly disappear into everyday spending.

### 08 · What an adviser's first week of January looks like

**Theme:** Career and adviser life · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** People think January is our busiest selling month. For me, it's a planning month.

**Post:**

The first week of January in my diary looks quieter than people expect.

I block out time to:
• Re-read the rule updates that took effect on 1 January, so what I share is current.
• Look at last year honestly: what went well, where I wasn't as responsive as I'd like to be.
• Plan when I'll check in with the families I look after, so reviews don't depend on memory.
• Set a learning goal for the year.

This job is mostly about consistency. Being reachable, explaining things clearly, and following up when you said you would.

If you're curious about what advisory work is really like day to day, I'll keep sharing the honest version here this year. The good parts, and the hard parts too.

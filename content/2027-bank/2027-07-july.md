### 51 · One in five residents is 65 or older

**Theme:** Family protection concepts · **Flag:** Date-sensitive. SingStat figures for end-June 2026; check for a 2027 update before posting.

**Hook:** Singapore is ageing faster than most of us notice.

**Post:**

According to SingStat, at end-June 2026, 19.5% of Singapore residents were aged 65 and over. That's about 825,300 people. In 2016, it was 12.4%.

Among citizens, the share was 21.4%.

What does that mean for an ordinary family?
• More of us will be caring for ageing parents while still working.
• More of us will be the senior, sooner than it feels.
• Conversations about health, care and money in later life become everyday topics, not rare ones.

None of this is a reason to panic. It's a reason to plan with your eyes open: for your parents, and for yourself.

Is caring for an older family member already part of your life?

### 52 · Fewer workers for every senior

**Theme:** Retirement habits · **Flag:** Date-sensitive. SingStat 2026 figure; check for a 2027 update before posting.

**Hook:** 3.2. That number says a lot about retirement in Singapore.

**Post:**

SingStat's old-age support ratio for 2026 is 3.2. That means there were 3.2 residents aged 20 to 64 for every resident aged 65 and over. The year before, it was 3.3.

In plain terms: there are fewer working-age people for each older person than before.

What it means for your own planning:
• Expect to rely more on your own savings and CPF in retirement.
• Family support may be stretched across more older relatives, so it helps not to count on it.
• Building good habits early matters, because time is the one thing you can't add later.

It isn't a scary statistic. It's an honest one, and honest numbers are a good starting point for a plan.

### 53 · Don't budget against the median

**Theme:** Budgeting · **Flag:** Date-sensitive. SingStat 2025 (preliminary) figure; check for the 2026 report (usually February) before posting.

**Hook:** Saw the "median household income" headline and felt behind? Read this first.

**Post:**

SingStat reported median monthly household market income at $12,446 in 2025 (preliminary), up from $11,558.

Headlines like that can make people feel they're doing badly. But a median tells you about the whole country, not about you. It doesn't know your household size, your stage of life, whether there's one income or two, or what you support.

A better way to use statistics like this:
• Compare yourself with yourself last year.
• Look at what's left after your essentials, not the top-line number.
• Ask whether your money is going where you want it to.

Your budget should start from your own life, not from a national number.

### 54 · Know your spending triggers

**Theme:** Budgeting · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Most overspending isn't about money. It's about mood.

**Post:**

Think back to the last thing you bought and regretted. How were you feeling at the time?

Common spending triggers:
• **Stress.** A rough day turns into a "treat".
• **Boredom.** Scrolling becomes shopping.
• **Celebration.** A good day justifies a big spend.
• **Comparison.** Someone else's holiday makes you book one.
• **Tiredness.** Late-night purchases you don't remember making.

Knowing your triggers won't stop you feeling them. But it lets you notice the moment and choose.

Try this: for the next two weeks, every time you buy something unplanned, jot down how you felt. Patterns show up quickly.

Which trigger is yours? Mine is tiredness.

### 55 · The sandwich generation squeeze

**Theme:** Family protection concepts · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Supporting your kids and your parents at the same time? You're not alone.

**Post:**

The "sandwich generation" are people caring for children and ageing parents at once. It's a lot: time, money and emotional energy, often all in the same week.

Some things that help:
• **Talk to siblings early.** Agree who contributes what, in money or time, before a crisis forces it.
• **Know your parents' situation.** Their savings, their cover, their wishes, and where their documents are.
• **Protect your own future too.** Helping family is important. But if your own retirement savings stop completely, the squeeze may simply move to your children later.
• **Ask for help.** There's government support for caregivers. Check the official pages.

You can't do everything. But a plan makes it much easier to do the important things well.

### 56 · Who can be your CPF nominee?

**Theme:** Wills, LPA and nominations · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Can you nominate someone who doesn't have a CPF account? Yes.

**Post:**

A few facts about CPF nominations that people often get wrong:

• **Who can make one:** anyone aged 16 or over.
• **Who can be a nominee:** anyone, including people who don't have a CPF account. A friend, a relative overseas, a partner.
• **What's included:** your Ordinary, Special, MediSave and Retirement Account savings, and your CPF LIFE premium balance.
• **Witnesses:** if you do it online with Singpass, you need two witnesses. They can't be your nominees, and they can't be organisations.
• **Cost:** free.

This matters most for people whose wishes don't follow the default family order. If there's someone specific you'd want to receive your CPF savings, a nomination is how you make that clear.

### 57 · How I plan my week

**Theme:** Career and adviser life · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** In a job with no fixed hours, the calendar is everything.

**Post:**

Advisory work doesn't come with a boss telling you what to do each hour. That's freedom, and it's also a trap.

Here's roughly how I block my week:
• **Mornings:** learning and admin. Reading updates, preparing for meetings, writing notes.
• **Afternoons and some evenings:** meetings, because that's when families are free.
• **One block a week:** reviews and follow-ups for the families I already look after.
• **One block a week:** team time, coaching and support.
• **Protected time:** family dinners and rest. Non-negotiable.

The blocks move around, but they always exist. Without them, urgent things push out important ones.

If you're thinking of a career with flexible hours, ask yourself honestly: will I manage my own time well?

### 58 · Retirement isn't a date. It's a long chapter.

**Theme:** Retirement habits · **Flag:** Date-sensitive. SingStat 2025 life tables; check for the 2026 tables (usually June) before posting.

**Hook:** If you reach 65, how many more years might you have? More than many people think.

**Post:**

SingStat's latest life tables show that a Singapore resident aged 65 can expect to live about 21.6 more years on average (2025). For women it's 23.1 years; for men, 19.9.

That's an average, not a prediction. Some people will live much longer, some shorter.

What it means for planning: retirement isn't a finish line. It may be a chapter of 20 years or more, with very different phases. Active early years, slower middle years, and later years when health and care needs often change.

A useful habit: when you think about retirement, think in phases, not just one big number.

What would you want your first five years after retiring to look like?

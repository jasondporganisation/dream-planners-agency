### 17 · LPA Form 1 or Form 2?

**Theme:** Wills, LPA and nominations · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Making a Lasting Power of Attorney? There are two forms. Most people use one of them.

**Post:**

An LPA lets you choose who makes decisions for you if you lose mental capacity. When you apply, you pick between two forms.

**Form 1:** general powers with basic restrictions. According to MSF, 98% of citizens who made an LPA used Form 1.

**Form 2:** customised powers. The clauses must be drafted by a Singapore lawyer.

Fees differ too. For Singapore Citizens, the Form 1 application fee is free, with no end date (MSF, announced 5 Mar 2026). Form 2 costs $30 for citizens and PRs.

Which suits you depends on how specific your wishes are. If you're unsure, start by reading the Office of the Public Guardian's pages on msf.gov.sg, and ask your Certificate Issuer questions before you sign.

### 18 · "Free" LPA? There's still one fee

**Theme:** Wills, LPA and nominations · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** The LPA Form 1 application is free for citizens. But it isn't completely free.

**Post:**

Every LPA has to be certified by a Certificate Issuer: an accredited doctor, a practising lawyer or a registered psychiatrist.

That certification fee is separate. It isn't set by the Office of the Public Guardian, and every applicant pays it.

For a sense of scale, MSF reported the median Form 1 certification fee as at June 2025:
• Doctors: $47.50
• Lawyers: $104.50
• Psychiatrists: $350
(excluding consultation and GST)

One more thing: once an LPA application is submitted, there's no refund. So check names, details and choices carefully before you submit.

Ask about fees upfront when you book a Certificate Issuer.

### 19 · Myth: "My spouse can decide for me anyway"

**Theme:** Wills, LPA and nominations · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** "If I can't make decisions, my husband will." Not automatically.

**Post:**

Many people assume a spouse or adult child can step in automatically if they lose mental capacity. In Singapore, that isn't how it works.

Without a Lasting Power of Attorney, your family would need to apply to court for a Deputy to be appointed under the Mental Capacity Act. That takes time and effort, at a moment when the family is already under stress.

An LPA lets you choose, in advance, who can act for you on personal welfare, property and affairs, or both.

It isn't about expecting the worst. It's about making sure the people you trust can help you without extra hurdles.

If this is a conversation your family keeps postponing, maybe this is the month.

### 20 · Sinking funds for the bills you know are coming

**Theme:** Budgeting · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** That "unexpected" yearly bill? It comes every year.

**Post:**

Car servicing, annual insurance premiums, festive spending, school fees, a parent's birthday dinner. They aren't emergencies. They're just not monthly.

A sinking fund is a small pot you fill bit by bit for a cost you already know about.

How to set one up:
1. List every cost you pay once or a few times a year.
2. Note the amount and the month it's due.
3. Add them up and divide by 12.
4. Move that amount each month into a separate account or pot.

When the bill arrives, the money's already there. No stress, no card debt, no raiding your emergency savings.

It's one of the least exciting money habits, and one of the most calming.

### 21 · The "wait a day" rule

**Theme:** Budgeting · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Add to cart. Close the app. Come back tomorrow.

**Post:**

A lot of spending happens in a moment: a flash sale, a late-night scroll, a bad day at work.

One simple habit helps: for anything that isn't a planned purchase, wait a day before buying.

If you still want it tomorrow, and it fits your budget, go ahead. You've made a choice, not a reaction.

If you've forgotten about it by tomorrow, you've learnt something useful about that purchase.

Some people make it a week for bigger items. Some keep a "want list" on their phone and review it once a month.

The point isn't to never treat yourself. It's to put a little space between the urge and the payment.

Try it this week and tell me what you didn't buy.

### 22 · Who depends on you?

**Theme:** Family protection concepts · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Before talking about protection, answer one question: who relies on you?

**Post:**

Family protection starts with people, not policies.

Take a piece of paper and list everyone who would be affected, financially, if you couldn't work or weren't around. For example:
• A spouse or partner who shares the mortgage
• Children, and how many years until they can support themselves
• Parents you give an allowance to
• A sibling you help out
• A helper or caregiver your family relies on

Next to each name, note what they rely on you for: income, care, time, or a mix.

This list changes as life changes. Marriage, a new baby, a parent's health, a new home. That's why it's worth redoing every year or two.

It's the starting point for any honest conversation about protecting your family.

### 23 · MediShield Life's limits, explained

**Theme:** MediShield Life and hospitalisation basics · **Flag:** Date-sensitive. Claim limits apply to treatments from 1 Jun 2026; check the MOH benefits page before posting.

**Hook:** "No lifetime limit" doesn't mean "no limits".

**Post:**

MediShield Life has no lifetime claim limit. That's a real strength: you're covered for life, regardless of age or health.

But there are still limits:
• **Per policy year:** up to $200,000.
• **Per treatment:** claim limits apply, for example a normal ward at $830 a day (plus an extra $800 a day for the first 2 days), and ICU at $5,140 a day.

These limits are why a bill can be larger than what MediShield Life pays, even before you count the deductible and co-insurance.

None of this makes it "bad" cover. It's designed as a base layer for large bills in subsidised wards. Knowing the limits simply helps you understand what it does and doesn't do.

### 24 · Why every post of mine has a disclaimer

**Theme:** Career and adviser life · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Ever wondered why my posts end with the same paragraph?

**Post:**

That disclaimer at the bottom isn't decoration.

Under MAS's guidelines on digital advertising, in force since 25 Mar 2026, representatives like me count as "digital marketers". One principle stood out to me: each post must stand on its own. A disclaimer in my bio, or in another post, doesn't fix a misleading one.

So every post I share is general education. It can't take your situation into account, and it isn't a recommendation.

Some people see rules like these as a burden. I see them as part of the job. If I can't explain something accurately and fairly in a public post, I shouldn't be posting it.

If you're thinking about joining this industry, get comfortable with compliance early. It's part of doing the work properly.

### 25 · A quarter-end CPF check-in

**Theme:** Retirement habits · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** When did you last log in to your CPF account? Not to do anything. Just to look.

**Post:**

Many of us only look at CPF when we need it: buying a flat, a hospital bill, turning 55. By then, there's no time to get familiar with it.

Try a ten-minute check-in at the end of each quarter:
• Look at the balance in each account.
• Check that your employer's contributions came in every month.
• Look at how much has gone out, and on what.
• Check whether you've made a nomination.
• Note one question you'd like answered.

You don't need to change anything. The habit is simply knowing where you stand, so that bigger decisions later don't come as a surprise.

Set a calendar reminder for the last week of March, June, September and December.

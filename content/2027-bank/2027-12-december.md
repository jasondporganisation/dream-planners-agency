### 92 · Five questions for your year-end money review

**Theme:** Retirement habits · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Before you plan next year, look back at this one. Honestly.

**Post:**

A year-end money review doesn't need spreadsheets. It needs a quiet hour and five questions.

1. **What went well?** Name at least one thing, however small.
2. **What surprised me?** An expense, a windfall, a change in plans.
3. **What did I keep putting off?** A will, an LPA, a nomination, a conversation.
4. **What changed in my life?** And does my plan reflect that?
5. **What's my one priority for next year?** Just one.

Write the answers down and keep them. Next December, you'll read them again and see how far you've come.

Doing this every year matters more than doing it perfectly.

Save this for the last week of December.

### 93 · A December spending plan

**Theme:** Budgeting · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** December is the month budgets go on holiday. Yours doesn't have to.

**Post:**

Gifts, parties, year-end sales, travel, school holidays. December can quietly become the most expensive month of the year.

A simple plan:
• **List the big items:** gifts, gatherings, travel, holiday activities for the kids.
• **Set a total** for the month, and split it across those items.
• **Agree gift limits** with family or friends. Many people are relieved when someone suggests it.
• **Pay with what you have,** not with next year's money on a card.
• **Leave a small buffer** for things you'll forget.

Enjoying the season is part of the point. Planning simply means you can enjoy it without a January hangover on your statement.

### 94 · One money goal for next year

**Theme:** Budgeting · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Ten money resolutions rarely work. One usually does.

**Post:**

Every January, people set long lists of money goals. By March, most are forgotten.

This year, try choosing just one. Make it:
• **Specific.** "Make my will" rather than "sort out my affairs".
• **Small enough to finish.** You can always add another once it's done.
• **Scheduled.** Put the first step in your calendar now, with a date.
• **Shared.** Tell one person, so someone will ask how it's going.

Examples: start an automatic transfer on payday, make an LPA, update your CPF nomination, track one month of spending, or have the money conversation with your partner.

Finish one meaningful thing next year and you'll be ahead of most resolutions.

What's yours? Write it below.

### 95 · Date-stamp your money numbers

**Theme:** Retirement habits · **Flag:** Points to January 2028 changes; no 2028 figures quoted.

**Hook:** Many CPF numbers you know will change in a few weeks.

**Post:**

Government figures in Singapore are often updated every January. CPF retirement sums are set for each new cohort turning 55. Contribution rates for some age groups have changed in recent years too.

That's why a figure you read online, even a correct one, can go out of date quickly.

A simple habit: whenever you note down a money figure, write the date and the source next to it. For example, "FRS for those turning 55 in 2027: $228,200 (CPF Board)".

Then, every January, check the official pages again: cpf.gov.sg, moh.gov.sg, moneysense.gov.sg.

I do the same with everything I post. If a number has no date, treat it with caution.

### 96 · Know your policy year

**Theme:** MediShield Life and hospitalisation basics · **Flag:** Date-sensitive. Check MOH for current deductibles before posting (changed 1 Apr 2027).

**Hook:** Your MediShield Life deductible doesn't reset in January. It resets with your policy year.

**Post:**

MediShield Life works on a policy year, and the deductible applies per policy year. You pay the full deductible only once in each policy year.

Your age for the deductible is also set by your age next birthday at the start of the policy year.

Why does this matter?
• If you've had a big claim recently, a second admission in the same policy year won't need a second full deductible.
• Your premiums and deductible bands are based on age next birthday, so knowing your renewal date helps you understand any change.

Find your policy year dates in your CPF account or your MediShield Life documents.

It's a small detail, but it helps you read a hospital bill with less confusion.

### 97 · Before the holiday trip: a family admin check

**Theme:** Family protection concepts · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Packing for a family holiday? Pack the paperwork plan too.

**Post:**

Year-end travel is exciting. It's also when emergencies are hardest to handle, because you're far from home.

A quick check before you leave:
• **Does someone at home know your itinerary** and how to reach you?
• **Do you know what cover you have** while overseas? Check your documents, not your memory.
• **Are important contacts saved offline?** Family, doctor, your adviser, your embassy.
• **Does a trusted person know where your documents are** if something happens?
• **Are the kids' arrangements clear** if you can't get home?

None of this will spoil the holiday. It just means a problem is a problem, not a crisis.

Safe travels to everyone heading off this month.

### 98 · My year in this job, honestly

**Theme:** Career and adviser life · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Another year as an adviser. Here's what it taught me.

**Post:**

Looking back on the year, a few things stand out.

• **The rules kept changing.** CPF rates, hospital rider rules, digital advertising guidelines. Keeping up was a big part of the work.
• **The meaningful moments were quiet.** Helping a family make sense of a hospital bill. A family who finally sorted their documents.
• **Mistakes happened.** I missed a follow-up or two. I apologised, fixed them and improved my system.
• **The team mattered more than ever.** We helped each other through hard weeks.
• **Rest isn't optional.** The months I protected my evenings were the months I worked most clearly.

It's not a glamorous job. But it's a good one, when you do it properly.

### 99 · Thinking about a career switch next year?

**Theme:** Career and adviser life · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** If a career change is on your mind for the new year, start with these questions.

**Post:**

The end of the year is when many people start wondering if it's time for something new. Whether or not it's advisory work, a few honest questions help.

• **What am I moving away from?** And what am I moving towards?
• **What do I want my days to look like?** Not just the title.
• **Can I manage the money side of the change?** A buffer makes any switch less stressful.
• **Who has done this before?** Talk to them about the reality, not just the highlights.
• **What would I need to learn,** and am I excited about learning it?

If advisory work is on your list, I'm always happy to share what the job is really like, the hard parts included. No pressure either way.

### 100 · Small habits, long timelines

**Theme:** Retirement habits · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** If you remember only one idea from this year, make it this one.

**Post:**

Most of what I've shared this year comes down to one idea: small, steady habits beat big, occasional efforts.

• Check your CPF statement a few times a year.
• Keep your will, LPA and nominations up to date.
• Understand how your hospital cover works before you need it.
• Give your money a job before it arrives.
• Talk to your family about the hard things, gently and early.
• Review once a year, on a date you won't forget.

None of these is dramatic. Done consistently, they bring something more valuable than any single decision: peace of mind for you and the people you love.

Thank you for reading, sharing and asking questions this year. See you in the new one.

National Day is about home, and the people who make it home. So here's a family admin checklist to go with the fireworks:

☐ Do the adults in the family each know where the important documents are?

### 60 · Life changed? Your will may need to as well

**Theme:** Wills, LPA and nominations · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** A will written five years ago may not fit the life you have today.

**Post:**

A will isn't something you write once and forget. Life changes, and the will needs to keep up.

Moments that are worth a review:
• **Marriage or remarriage.** In Singapore, this revokes your existing will.
• **A new child.** Have you named a guardian? Is the child included?
• **Divorce or separation.** Do your wishes still reflect your situation?
• **Buying property.** How is it held, and does your will fit with that?
• **An executor or guardian you named can no longer act.** Time to choose someone else.
• **A big change in what you own.**

A good habit is to reread your will every few years, and after any big life event.

When did you last look at yours?

### 61 · Pre-existing conditions and MediShield Life

**Theme:** MediShield Life and hospitalisation basics · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Have a pre-existing condition? MediShield Life still covers you.

**Post:**

People with health conditions sometimes worry they can't get hospital cover. For the basic national scheme, that isn't the case.

MediShield Life covers all Singapore Citizens and PRs for life, regardless of age or health, including pre-existing conditions.

One thing to know: for serious pre-existing conditions, MOH says there are 30% Additional Premiums for the first 10 years.

And if you have an Integrated Shield Plan whose private part excludes a condition, the MediShield Life part still covers it.

So if a family member has been told they "can't get covered" for a condition, it's worth checking what MediShield Life already provides before assuming they have nothing.

### 62 · MediShield Life isn't only for hospital stays

**Theme:** MediShield Life and hospitalisation basics · **Flag:** Date-sensitive. Claim limits apply from 1 Jun 2026; check the MOH benefits page before posting.

**Hook:** Think MediShield Life only helps when you're warded? Not quite.

**Post:**

MediShield Life also helps with selected costly outpatient treatments, such as kidney dialysis and chemotherapy.

Some of the claim limits MOH lists (from 1 Jun 2026):
• **Kidney dialysis:** up to $1,750 a month
• **Cancer drug treatment:** $200 to $12,800 a month, depending on the drug

There's also an outpatient deductible of $500 per policy year for outpatient treatment from 1 Jun 2026.

These are long-running treatments, and the bills can build up month after month. Knowing that the national scheme helps with them can make a hard diagnosis a little less overwhelming financially.

If someone in your family is going through treatment like this, the hospital's medical social workers or financial counsellors can explain what applies.

### 63 · Ward class and your deductible

**Theme:** MediShield Life and hospitalisation basics · **Flag:** Date-sensitive. Amounts removed by Brand Guardian (5 Nov 2026) because 2026 deductibles are out of date from 1 Apr 2027 and the new amounts were not published. Do not add figures unless read from MOH's current table and the fact pack is refreshed.

**Hook:** Choosing a ward class changes more than the room.

**Post:**

MediShield Life is sized for subsidised treatment in Class B2 and C wards in public hospitals. It pays a smaller share of bills in Class A or B1 wards, or private hospitals.

Your ward choice also changes the deductible, the part you pay first each policy year. In MOH's table, the deductible has been lowest for Class C, higher for Class B2, B2+ and B1, and highest for Class A and private hospitals. It also depends on your age next birthday.

The amounts were updated from 1 Apr 2027, so check MOH's or CPF Board's current table for today's figures.

Ward choice is personal. Some value privacy, some value cost. The point is to make that choice knowing what it means for the bill, not to discover it at discharge.

### 64 · Surgery and implants have their own limits

**Theme:** MediShield Life and hospitalisation basics · **Flag:** Date-sensitive. Claim limits apply from 1 Jun 2026; check the MOH benefits page before posting.

**Hook:** Your hospital bill is split into parts. MediShield Life has a limit for each one.

**Post:**

When you look at a hospital bill, it's tempting to see one total. MediShield Life doesn't see it that way. It looks at each part of the treatment and applies a claim limit to it.

Some examples MOH lists (from 1 Jun 2026):
• **Surgery:** $240 to $3,900, depending on how the procedure is classified (tables 1 to 7)
• **Implants:** up to $7,000 per treatment
• **Cell, tissue and gene therapy products:** up to $141,000 per treatment, outside the policy-year limit

Then the yearly limit of $200,000 applies overall.

This is why two people with the same total bill can get different amounts from MediShield Life. What matters is what the bill is made of.

If you're facing surgery, ask the hospital for an estimate that shows what MediShield Life is expected to cover.

### 65 · Pay yourself first, automatically

**Theme:** Budgeting · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** Saving what's left at the end of the month rarely works. Try the other way round.

**Post:**

The usual approach: spend through the month, save what's left. Most months, not much is left.

"Pay yourself first" flips it. On payday, before you spend anything, move an amount into savings. Then live on the rest.

Tips that make it work:
• **Automate it.** Set a standing transfer for payday so you don't have to decide each month.
• **Start small.** An amount you won't miss is better than an ambitious one you'll undo.
• **Use a separate account.** Out of sight helps.
• **Raise it with every pay rise,** even a little.

It works because it removes willpower from the equation. The saving happens first, and your spending adjusts around it.

### 66 · On hearing "no"

**Theme:** Career and adviser life · **Flag:** None

**Hook:** In this job, you hear "no" a lot. Here's how I think about it now.

**Post:**

Early on, every "no" felt personal. I'd replay conversations and wonder what I'd done wrong.

Over time, I learnt a few things:
• **"No" usually means "not now".** People have their own timing, and that's their right.
• **It's not a verdict on you.** It's a decision about their priorities this month.
• **Respect it fully.** No pressure, no "just one more try". People remember how you handled their no.
• **Look after yourself.** Talk to your team. Keep a routine. Rest.

Some of the families I'm closest to today said no the first time. They came back when they were ready, because they felt respected.

If you're considering this career, resilience matters as much as knowledge.

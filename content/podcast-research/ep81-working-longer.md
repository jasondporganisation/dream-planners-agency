# Ep 81 — Working Longer, on Your Terms

**Theme:** Retirement and CPF in depth
**Listener's real question:** "What are the rules on retirement age now, and does working into my 60s change my CPF?"

## Key points (in order)
1. The minimum retirement age is 64 and the re-employment age is 69, both from 1 July 2026.
2. Re-employment means eligible employees can be offered work by their employer beyond 64, up to 69.
3. CPF contributions continue after 55 at stepped rates. Senior worker rates are being raised in stages (next on 1 Jan 2027).
4. Workfare Income Supplement (WIS) supports lower-wage workers aged 30 and above, and is paid partly in cash and partly into CPF.
5. Working longer can mean more contributions, the option to defer CPF LIFE, and a shorter period to fund.
6. Health and caregiving can cut working years short, so plan for both outcomes.
7. Choosing to work is personal. Purpose and pace matter as much as money.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers only)
"Siew Lan", 62, works part-time on an illustrative $2,000 a month.
- At the current 25% total rate for 60–65, about $500 a month goes into her CPF: an illustrative 12.5% employee share ($250) plus 12.5% employer share ($250).
- Working three more years at that pace adds roughly $18,000 in contributions, before interest.
*Say on air: "Illustrative arithmetic only. Actual contributions depend on wages and the rates at the time."*

## Common myths to correct
- "At 64 I'll be forced out." Employers must offer re-employment to eligible employees up to 69.
- "CPF stops after 55 / 65." Contributions continue at age-based rates.
- "Workfare is welfare cash only." Most of it goes into CPF.
- "Retirement age = CPF payout age." They are different (64 vs payouts from 65).

## "Never say" lines
- "Everyone should work till 69."
- "Your employer must keep you no matter what." Eligibility conditions apply.
- "Workfare will pay you $X this year" (WY2026 maximum not verified).

# Ep 69 — The Lasting Power of Attorney

**Theme:** Protecting what you build
**Listener's real question:** "If I lose mental capacity one day, who gets to make decisions for me, and how do I choose?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Mental capacity can be lost suddenly (stroke, accident) or gradually (dementia), at any adult age.
2. A Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) lets you choose, while you still have capacity, who will decide for you. It covers two areas: personal welfare, and property and affairs.
3. Without an LPA, family members may have to apply to court to be appointed a Deputy. That is slower and more stressful.
4. Form 1 (general powers) is free for Singapore Citizens. MSF has said this continues after the earlier waiver ends. Form 2 (customised powers) is drafted by a lawyer.
5. You must be 21 or older. A certificate issuer (an accredited doctor, a practising Singapore lawyer, or a registered psychiatrist) confirms you understand what you are signing.
6. Choose donees for trust and judgment, not just closeness. Talk to them first.
7. An LPA is about decisions while you are alive. A will is about assets after death. They do different jobs.

## Worked example (illustrative scenario, no figures)
"Grace", 45, makes a Form 1 LPA naming her husband as donee for both areas, and her sister as replacement donee. Years later she has a stroke and cannot manage her affairs. Her husband can use the registered LPA to deal with her bank matters and care decisions, with no court application.
Without the LPA, her family might need to apply to court for deputyship first.
*Say on air: "A made-up example to show how it works, not legal advice."*

## Common myths to correct
- "My spouse can automatically access my bank account if I can't." Not without legal authority such as an LPA or deputyship.
- "LPAs are only for old people." Capacity can be lost at any age.
- "An LPA is the same as a will." An LPA works while you are alive. A will works after death.
- "It's expensive and complicated." Form 1 is free for citizens, and certificate issuer fees vary.

## "Never say" lines
- "Everyone must make an LPA now."
- Naming specific clinics or law firms.
- "Your family will be stuck forever without one."

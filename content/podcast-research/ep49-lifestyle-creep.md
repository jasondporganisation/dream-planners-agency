# Ep 49 — When the Pay Rise Disappears

**Theme:** Money habits and behaviour
**Listener's real question:** "I earn more than five years ago, but I don't feel any richer. Where does it go?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Lifestyle creep: spending rises quietly to meet income. It comes through small upgrades, not one big splurge.
2. It is normal and partly healthy. Some upgrades improve life. The problem is when none of the raise is kept.
3. Household spending does rise over time. SingStat's average monthly household expenditure went from $5,163 (2017/18) to $5,931 (2023).
4. A simple rule: decide in advance what share of any raise goes to future you (e.g. half), before it hits your account.
5. Watch the recurring costs: subscriptions, car, bigger home, dining. They are harder to undo than one-off treats.
6. Separate "this makes my life better" from "everyone around me has this".
7. Review once a year (Ep 54). Compare the growth in income with the growth in spending.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers only)
"Wen" gets an illustrative $500-a-month raise in take-home pay.
- Without a plan: a new phone plan (+$40), more food delivery (+$150), a gym upgrade (+$80) and weekend outings (+$230) absorb all $500 within months.
- With a "half for future me" rule: $250 goes to savings by standing instruction, and $250 is free to enjoy.
- Over a year that is $3,000 kept, and she still feels the raise.
*Say on air: "Illustrative. The habit is deciding before the money arrives."*

## Common myths to correct
- "Lifestyle creep only happens to big spenders." It is mostly small, recurring changes.
- "I deserve it, so I shouldn't save any of it." You can do both.
- "Higher income automatically means more CPF." Contributions are capped at the OW ceiling.

## "Never say" lines
- Judging specific purchases as "wasteful".
- "Never upgrade your lifestyle."
- Product or brand names for apps and tools.

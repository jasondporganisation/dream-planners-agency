# Ep 53 — The Bonus Month Decision

**Theme:** Money habits and behaviour
**Listener's real question:** "My year-end bonus is coming. How do I decide what to do with it so it doesn't just vanish?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Decide before it arrives. A plan made in November beats impulse in December.
2. A calm order of priorities: high-interest debt first, then the emergency fund, then known upcoming costs, then longer-term goals, with a planned share for enjoyment.
3. Remember tax: a bonus raises chargeable income, and next year's tax bill may be higher (Ep 55).
4. CPF also applies to bonuses (Additional Wages), subject to the annual ceiling. Check your payslip.
5. Voluntary CPF top-ups can attract tax relief, up to $8,000 for yourself and $8,000 for family, but they lock the money for retirement.
6. Enjoyment is legitimate. A planned treat stops the "I deserve it" spiral.
7. Write the split down before payday.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers only)
"Nadia" expects an illustrative $6,000 bonus after CPF.
- $1,500 clears the rest of a card balance.
- $2,000 takes her emergency fund from 3 to about 4 months.
- $1,000 is set aside for next year's tax bill and festive costs.
- $1,000 goes to a long-term goal of her choosing.
- $500 is a planned treat.
*Say on air: "Illustrative split, not a formula. Your order depends on your situation."*

## Common myths to correct
- "Bonus money is free money." It is income, taxed and counted for CPF.
- "Topping up CPF is always the best use." It brings tax relief but locks the money away.
- "I should invest all of it right away." Debt and buffers usually come first.

## "Never say" lines
- "Put your bonus into [product]."
- "Top up the max: it's the best tax hack."
- Any return or tax-saving figure presented as certain for the listener.

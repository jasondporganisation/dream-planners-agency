# Ep 54 — Your Once-a-Year Money Check-Up

**Theme:** Money habits and behaviour
**Listener's real question:** "I never look at the big picture of my money. What should I check, and how often?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Once a year is enough for most people. Pick a fixed date, such as your birthday or after tax filing.
2. Net worth snapshot: what you own (cash, CPF balances, investments, home equity) minus what you owe.
3. Cash flow: did savings keep up with income this year? (Ep 49.)
4. Buffers: is the emergency fund still 3–6 months of current expenses?
5. Protection and paperwork: review coverage against needs with a licensed professional, and check nominations, will and LPA after life changes.
6. CPF check: read your annual statement, housing usage and accrued interest, and nominations.
7. Choose one or two actions for the next year. Don't try to fix everything.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers only)
"Jason and Mel" (illustrative couple) do a one-page review:
- Own: cash $40,000; CPF $210,000; home equity (estimated) $250,000. Owe: housing loan $300,000 (already counted in equity), car loan $20,000.
- Net worth (rough): $40,000 + $210,000 + $250,000 − $20,000 = $480,000.
- Emergency fund: $40,000 against $6,500 monthly expenses, about 6 months. OK.
- Actions for next year: update CPF nominations after their wedding, and make LPAs.
*Say on air: "Made-up figures. Home value is an estimate, not cash."*

## Common myths to correct
- "Net worth is only for rich people." It is just a scorecard.
- "My home value is money I can spend." It is illiquid.
- "Coverage multiples are rules I must hit." They are general guidelines. Personal needs differ.

## "Never say" lines
- "You need 9× your income in insurance." Say "MoneySense's rule of thumb is…".
- Naming insurers, policies or apps.
- "Your net worth should be X by age Y."

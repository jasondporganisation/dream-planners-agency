# Ep 55 — Reading Your Tax Bill

**Theme:** Money habits and behaviour
**Listener's real question:** "How is my income tax worked out, and what are these reliefs everyone talks about?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Singapore resident income tax is progressive. Each slice of chargeable income is taxed at its own rate.
2. The first $20,000 of chargeable income is taxed at 0%. The top rate is 24% on income above $1 million (from YA2024).
3. Reliefs reduce chargeable income. Common ones relate to family (parents, children, spouse) and to CPF/SRS top-ups.
4. All personal reliefs together are capped at $80,000 per Year of Assessment.
5. Relief is not a refund. It lowers the income that tax is calculated on.
6. Keep records and check your pre-filled return. Many reliefs are automatic, but not all.
7. Plan for the bill. Set aside a little each month, or use instalments.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers only)
"Aaron" has an illustrative chargeable income of $20,000 after reliefs.
- The first $20,000 is at 0%, so his tax is $0 at this level (before any other factors).
- If a relief had brought his chargeable income down from $29,000 to $20,000, that $9,000 relief would remove tax on the slice above $20,000, at the rate for that band (see IRAS table).

## Common myths to correct
- "A pay rise can make me take home less because I move into a higher bracket." Only the slice above the threshold is taxed at the higher rate.
- "Relief means the government pays me." It lowers taxable income.
- "I can claim unlimited reliefs." There is an $80,000 cap.
- "SRS withdrawals are tax-free." 50% is taxable at or after the statutory retirement age.

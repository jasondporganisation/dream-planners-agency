# Ep 80 — Your Home and Your Retirement

**Theme:** Retirement and CPF in depth
**Listener's real question:** "If I use my CPF to pay for my flat, what happens to my retirement savings?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Most Singaporeans use their OA to pay for their home. It is a normal and allowed use.
2. There are limits. Generally OA use is capped at the lower of the purchase price or valuation. Going beyond that (up to 120%) needs the Basic Retirement Sum set aside.
3. CPF used for housing plus accrued interest is refunded to your CPF when you sell. That is "putting back" what retirement would have had.
4. If you sell at market value, you do not have to top up a refund shortfall in cash.
5. Money in a home is not monthly income. A paid-off flat lowers costs but does not pay the grocery bill.
6. A useful habit is to check your CPF housing usage and accrued interest statement once a year.
7. Lease length matters for how much CPF can be used, and for older buyers.

"Accrued interest" means the interest your CPF would have earned at the OA rate had the money not been used. Describe it in those plain words.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers only)
"Jun" used an illustrative $150,000 of OA savings for a flat over the years. With accrued interest, his statement shows an illustrative $185,000 to refund.
- If he sells for enough, $185,000 goes back into his CPF (to the OA, or first to the RA if he is 55 or older).
- If the sale price at market value is not enough, he does not need to top up the gap in cash.
*Say on air: "These are made-up figures to explain the idea of a refund, not a projection."*

## Common myths to correct
- "Accrued interest is a penalty I pay to the Government." It goes back to your own CPF.
- "Using CPF for my home means I'll have no retirement." It reduces what's in CPF, but the refund goes back in when you sell.
- "My flat is my retirement plan." A home is shelter. Income needs a separate source.
- "There's no limit to how much CPF I can use." Valuation and lease rules apply.

## "Never say" lines
- "Property always goes up."
- "Use as little CPF as possible: pay cash instead" (or the reverse) as blanket advice.
- "Sell and right-size: you'll be fine."

# Ep 56 — Money Talks Before Marriage

**Theme:** Family and life stages
**Listener's real question:** "We're getting married. What money conversations should we have before the wedding, not after?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Money is one of the most common sources of tension in relationships. Talking early is cheaper than arguing later.
2. Share the basics honestly: income, debts, savings, CPF balances, and money commitments to parents.
3. Agree a system: fully joint, fully separate, or "yours, mine and ours". Any of these can work if it is explicit.
4. Wedding and housing timelines interact. Discuss the flat budget (Ep 57) alongside the wedding budget.
5. Paperwork changes at marriage: marriage revokes an existing will and an existing CPF nomination. Both need redoing.
6. Talk about future plans (children, caring for parents, career breaks) and how you would handle a drop in income.
7. Keep talking. A short monthly "money date" works for many couples.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers only)
"Hafiz and Clara" list their numbers on one page:
- Take-home: $4,200 and $3,800. Parents' allowance: $500 and $300.
- Debts: Clara has an illustrative $6,000 study loan, Hafiz none.
- They choose "yours, mine and ours": each puts $2,000 a month into a joint account for shared costs and savings, and keeps the rest.
- After the wedding: new CPF nominations, new wills, and a joint emergency fund target.
*Say on air: "A made-up couple to show the kind of conversation, not a formula."*

## Common myths to correct
- "Talking about money before marriage is unromantic." It protects the relationship.
- "My old nomination or will still applies after marriage." Marriage revokes both.
- "Joint accounts are the only way to be a real team." Any agreed system works.

## "Never say" lines
- Prescribing one "right" way to split money.
- Gender stereotypes about who manages money.
- Product names (joint accounts, cards, policies).

# Ep 70 — Wills, and What Happens Without One

**Theme:** Protecting what you build
**Listener's real question:** "Do I really need a will, and what happens to my things if I don't have one?"

## Key points (in order)
1. A will sets out who receives your estate and who manages it after you pass away.
2. Basic validity rules (Wills Act): you must be 21 or older, the will must be signed with two or more witnesses present at the same time, and a gift to a witness or their spouse is void.
3. Marriage revokes an earlier will. Life changes should prompt a review.
4. Without a will, the Intestate Succession Act decides who inherits. For example: spouse and children split 50/50; spouse and parents split 50/50; spouse alone takes everything.
5. The Intestate Succession Act does not apply to Muslims, whose estates follow Muslim law.
6. Some assets do not pass by will: CPF savings (by nomination) and certain insurance nominations. See Ep 71.
7. This is general information. Wills are legal documents, and many people get legal help.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers only)
"Mr Lim" passes away without a will. He leaves an illustrative $300,000 in non-CPF assets, a wife and two children.
- Under the intestacy rules: his wife receives 50% ($150,000) and the children share 50% ($75,000 each).
- Suppose he had wanted to leave something to his elderly mother or a charity. Without a will, that does not happen.
*Say on air: "Illustrative and simplified. Debts, joint assets and other rules affect real estates."*

## Common myths to correct
- "Without a will, everything goes to my spouse." Not if there are children or parents.
- "A will made years ago still works after I remarry." Marriage revokes it.
- "My will covers my CPF." CPF savings are distributed by nomination, not by will.
- "A witness can also be a beneficiary." The gift to that witness is void.

## "Never say" lines
- Naming will-writing services or law firms.
- "DIY wills are just as good" or "DIY wills are dangerous" as blanket claims.
- Anything on Muslim inheritance beyond "different rules apply". Refer to official sources.

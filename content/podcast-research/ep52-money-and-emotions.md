# Ep 52 — Money, Mood and the Fear of Missing Out

**Theme:** Money habits and behaviour
**Listener's real question:** "Everyone around me seems to be buying or investing in something. Why do I feel I'm falling behind?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Money decisions are emotional first and logical second. Noticing that is the skill.
2. FOMO is social comparison. You see others' wins, not their losses or debts.
3. Urgency is a warning sign. Scammers and pushy sales both rely on "act now" (see Ep 72).
4. A personal "cooling-off" rule (e.g. wait 48 hours before any large or unfamiliar money decision) mirrors safeguards built into Singapore's system.
5. Know your own goals. "Behind" only means something against your own plan.
6. Check before you act. Is the firm or person regulated? (Ep 73.)
7. Investment scams were the largest scam category by losses in 2025 (~$336.2 million). FOMO is part of why.

## Worked example (illustrative scenario)
"Marcus" sees colleagues talking about a "can't-miss" opportunity in a chat group. He feels pressure to put in an illustrative $5,000 today.
His 48-hour rule:
1. Write down why he wants it and what he would do if it fell by half.
2. Check whether the firm is on the MAS Financial Institutions Directory.
3. Ask whether this fits his written goals.
By day two the urgency has faded. He decides based on his plan, not the chat.
*Say on air: "A made-up scenario. We're not commenting on any specific investment."*

## Common myths to correct
- "If everyone's doing it, it must be safe." Popularity is not proof.
- "I'm too smart to be influenced." Everyone feels social pressure.
- "Missing out is losing money." Not acting is often a valid choice.

## "Never say" lines
- Naming any asset, coin, stock, fund or platform.
- "Now's a good / bad time to invest."
- Any return figures, including "people made X%".

# Ep 50 — The Minimum Payment Trap

**Theme:** Money habits and behaviour
**Listener's real question:** "If I just pay the minimum on my credit card each month, I'm fine, right?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Paying the minimum avoids late fees, but interest runs on the rest of the balance.
2. Credit card interest in Singapore is typically 25% to 29% a year (MoneySense).
3. The minimum payment is usually 3% to 5% of the balance or a fixed amount such as $50, whichever is higher.
4. MoneySense's example: a $5,000 balance at 25% a year, paying only the $50 minimum, takes more than 14 years to clear.
5. MAS limits borrowing. If interest-bearing unsecured debt exceeds 12× monthly income for three straight months, new credit is barred. Borrowers 60 days past due cannot get more unsecured credit.
6. You can request your own credit report from Credit Bureau Singapore (regulated by MAS) to see your position.
7. The way out: stop adding to the balance, pay more than the minimum, and seek free help early if it is overwhelming.

## Worked example
Use MoneySense's own example rather than inventing one: a $5,000 card balance at 25% a year, paying only a $50 minimum, takes more than 14 years to clear.
Illustrative contrast: interest on a $5,000 balance at 25% a year is roughly $1,250 a year, about $100 a month. A $50 payment barely touches it at first, which is why the debt lingers.
*Say on air: "The 14-year figure is MoneySense's example. The monthly interest is a rough illustration."*

## Common myths to correct
- "Paying the minimum keeps me debt-free." It keeps you in good standing, not out of debt.
- "Interest is only charged on the unpaid part." Check your card terms: interest-free periods can be lost once you carry a balance. Describe this as "check how your card works", not as a universal rule (**UNVERIFIED** detail).
- "Checking my credit report hurts my score." Not stated by MoneySense, so avoid the claim either way.
- "Debt problems are shameful." Early help is common and sensible.

## "Never say" lines
- Naming banks, cards, balance-transfer offers or debt consolidation products.
- "Take a personal loan to pay off your card."
- "You'll be blacklisted."

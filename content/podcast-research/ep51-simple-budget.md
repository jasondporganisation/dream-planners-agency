# Ep 51 — A Budget You Will Actually Keep

**Theme:** Money habits and behaviour
**Listener's real question:** "I've tried budgeting apps and spreadsheets and always give up. Is there a simpler way?"

## Key points (in order)
1. A budget is a plan for where money goes, not a punishment for spending.
2. Start with three numbers: take-home pay, fixed costs, and what you want to save (pay yourself first, Ep 48).
3. What remains is "free to spend". You do not need to track every category.
4. Look at the past two or three months of statements once to find your real fixed costs.
5. Plan for irregular costs (annual insurance, festive spending, school fees) by setting aside a monthly amount.
6. Review monthly in 15 minutes. Adjust rather than abandon.
7. MoneySense's saving guideline (at least 20% of take-home pay) is a direction to grow towards.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers only)
"Shu Fen" takes home an illustrative $5,000 a month.
- Save first: $800 (16%) moves out on payday.
- Fixed costs: $2,600 (housing loan top-up, utilities, phone, transport, insurance, parents' allowance).
- Irregular-cost pot: $300 a month (festive, annual bills, birthdays).
- Free to spend: $1,300. That is the only number she watches day to day.
*Say on air: "Illustrative split. Your fixed costs will be different."*

## Common myths to correct
- "Budgets need detailed tracking." Three or four numbers can be enough.
- "If I overspend one month, the budget's failed." Adjust next month.
- "Irregular costs are emergencies." Most are predictable and can be planned.

## "Never say" lines
- Recommending specific apps or bank tools.
- "This is the best budgeting method."
- Moralising about spending choices.

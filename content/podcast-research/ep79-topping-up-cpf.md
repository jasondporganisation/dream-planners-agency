# Ep 79 — Topping Up CPF: For Yourself and Your Family

**Theme:** Retirement and CPF in depth
**Listener's real question:** "If I put extra cash into my own or my parents' CPF, what do I get, and what do I give up?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Cash top-ups to retirement savings can raise future monthly payouts, for yourself or for loved ones.
2. Tax relief: up to $8,000 a year for topping up yourself, and up to another $8,000 for loved ones.
3. For lower-income seniors, the Matched Retirement Savings Scheme (MRSS) matches top-ups dollar for dollar, up to $2,000 a year and $20,000 over a lifetime.
4. Top-ups that get the matching grant do not also get tax relief. You get one or the other.
5. From 2026 there is also a matched MediSave scheme for eligible members aged 55–70.
6. The trade-off: topped-up money is set aside for retirement and is not cash you can easily get back.
7. Keep your own emergency fund and needs first. A top-up to parents is a gift that is hard to reverse.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers only)
"Ravi" tops up an illustrative $2,000 to his 60-year-old mother's CPF.
- If she qualifies for MRSS, the Government matches $2,000. Her retirement savings rise by $4,000, and Ravi gets no tax relief on that top-up.
- If she does not qualify, Ravi may claim tax relief on the $2,000 (within the $8,000 family cap), and her savings rise by $2,000.
*Say on air: "Illustrative only. Eligibility is checked by CPF, and tax relief depends on your own tax position."*

## Common myths to correct
- "Top-ups can be withdrawn whenever." They become retirement savings under CPF rules.
- "I get tax relief and the matching grant." Not both on the same top-up.
- "Only rich people top up." The matching scheme is designed for lower-income seniors.
- "Tax relief means free money." It reduces taxable income. It is not a refund of the top-up.

## "Never say" lines
- "Top up to the max every year: it's the best tax hack."
- "Your parents will definitely get the grant."
- "Top-ups guarantee a comfortable retirement."

# Ep 75 — How CPF Interest Grows

**Theme:** Retirement and CPF in depth
**Listener's real question:** "What interest does my CPF actually earn, and why do people say the first $60,000 matters?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Each CPF account earns interest at its own rate. The Ordinary Account (OA) rate is lower. The Special, MediSave and Retirement Account rate is higher.
2. Current rates: OA 2.5%; SA/MA/RA 4% (1 Oct–31 Dec 2026). Rates are reviewed every quarter.
3. The 4% floor on SA/MA/RA has been extended to 31 Dec 2027.
4. Extra interest goes to the first slice of your balances: an extra 1% on the first $60,000 if you are under 55, with more for those 55 and over. The OA counts for at most $20,000 of that.
5. Compounding is quiet: interest is credited and then earns interest itself. That is why time matters more than timing.
6. The trade-off: SA and RA money is set aside for retirement and is less flexible than cash. Higher interest comes with less access.
7. These are CPF interest rates set by policy. They are not investment returns, and we do not compare them with products.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers only)
Two friends each have an illustrative $10,000 more in a CPF account for one year, ignoring extra interest.
- At the OA's current 2.5%: about $250 in interest for the year.
- At the SA/RA's current 4%: about $400.
The next year, interest is earned on the interest too. That is the "quiet compounding" in the show's name.
*Say on air: "Simplified on purpose. Real crediting is monthly and the rates are reviewed each quarter."*

## Common myths to correct
- "CPF interest is fixed forever." Rates are reviewed quarterly. The floors are set for stated periods.
- "Everyone gets 5% or 6%." Extra interest applies only to the first slice of balances, with caps.
- "OA money earns the same as SA money." It does not.
- "CPF interest is a return like an investment." It is a policy-set interest rate, not a market return.

## "Never say" lines
- "Guaranteed 4% forever" or "risk-free returns."
- "CPF beats [any product or bank account]."
- "Rates will go up / down next year."
- "Move all your OA money to SA right now." Transfers are generally irreversible, so the point needs care, not a push.

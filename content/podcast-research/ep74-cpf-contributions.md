# Ep 74 — Where Your CPF Contributions Go

**Theme:** Retirement and CPF in depth
**Listener's real question:** "Every month a big chunk of my pay goes to CPF. How much is it, and why does it change as I get older?"

## Key points (in order)
1. CPF is your money, saved by you and your employer together. It is not a tax.
2. The total contribution rate for most private-sector employees aged 55 and below is 37% of wages: 17% from the employer and 20% from the employee.
3. Rates step down after 55, 60, 65 and 70. The lower rates are meant to help older workers stay employable. They are being raised in stages, and the next step is in January 2027.
4. There is a ceiling. Contributions are only due on monthly Ordinary Wages up to $8,000 (from 1 Jan 2026), and the total wage ceiling is $102,000 a year.
5. The CPF Annual Limit of $37,740 caps all mandatory and voluntary contributions in a calendar year.
6. Your payslip and CPF statement are worth reading once a year: check that what was deducted matches what arrived.
7. A bigger salary does not mean CPF grows without limit, because of the ceilings. That matters for anyone planning around high earnings.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers only)
"Mei", 32, earns an illustrative $5,000 a month in Ordinary Wages.
- Employee share, 20%: $1,000 is deducted from her pay.
- Employer share, 17%: $850 is added on top.
- Total into her CPF: $1,850 a month.
If her pay were an illustrative $10,000, contributions would be worked out on $8,000 only, the OW ceiling. Total: 37% × $8,000 = $2,960.
*Say on air: "These are made-up round numbers to show the arithmetic, not anyone's actual payslip."*

## Common myths to correct
- "CPF is a tax." It is savings in your own name, with interest.
- "If I earn more, my CPF grows without limit." The OW ceiling and Annual Limit cap it.
- "Rates drop to zero after 55." They step down. They do not stop, and senior rates are being raised.
- "The employer share comes out of my salary." It is paid on top of the employee share.

## "Never say" lines
- "CPF is the only retirement plan you need."
- "You'll definitely have enough by 65 if you work full-time."
- "Put your extra money into [any product] instead of CPF."
- Do not quote allocation percentages to each account (not verified here).

# Ep 60 — Raising Kids Who Understand Money

**Theme:** Family and life stages
**Listener's real question:** "How do I teach my kids about money without lecturing them or making them anxious?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Children learn money mostly by watching. Your calm habits teach more than talks.
2. Age 4–7: money is exchanged for things, and waiting is part of it. Use clear jars for save, spend and give.
3. Age 8–12: pocket money with small choices and small mistakes. Let them feel the result of a choice.
4. Teens: budgeting for a month, comparing prices, understanding wants vs needs, and digital payments.
5. Talk openly about trade-offs ("we're choosing this instead of that"), not about fear ("we can't afford anything").
6. Scam awareness is part of money skills today: never share OTPs or passwords, and be wary of "too good to be true".
7. Government accounts can show saving in action. The CDA (and from 2027 a CDA open to age 16) and the PSEA are visible pots of savings for the child.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers only)
"Jia Hui", 9, gets an illustrative $10 a week in pocket money, split into three jars: $6 spend, $3 save, $1 give.
- She wants a $30 toy. At $3 a week saved, she can see it will take 10 weeks.
- Halfway, she spends some savings on snacks and the wait gets longer. That is the lesson, learned without a lecture.

## Common myths to correct
- "Kids are too young to understand money." Simple ideas start early.
- "Talking about money will make them anxious." Calm, honest talk reduces anxiety.
- "Schools teach this." Home habits matter most.

## "Never say" lines
- Naming kids' bank accounts, apps or investment products.
- "Start investing for your child now in [X]."
- Shaming families about pocket money choices.

# Ep 62 — Caught in the Middle: The Sandwich Years

**Theme:** Family and life stages
**Listener's real question:** "I'm paying for my kids and my parents at the same time. How do I not lose myself in the middle?"

## Key points (in order)
1. The sandwich years are common, and Singapore's ageing population makes them more so. One in five residents is now 65 or older.
2. The oxygen-mask principle: protect your own emergency fund and retirement basics, or the pressure passes to your children later.
3. Map the commitments: kids (school, care), parents (allowance, medical, care), you (housing, savings).
4. Use the support you are entitled to: child-related reliefs, Parent Relief, Grandparent Caregiver Relief if grandparents help with childcare, and caregiver grants.
5. Share the load with siblings openly, with agreed amounts and roles.
6. Plan for one crisis, not every crisis. A health event for a parent is the most likely shock (Ep 63, 68).
7. Time and health are resources too. Burnout costs money.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers only)
"Lay Peng", 45, takes home an illustrative $7,000 a month.
- Children: $1,500. Parents: $800. Housing: $1,800. Household: $1,600.
- Herself: $1,300 saved, which she protects first by standing instruction on payday.
- When her father needs care, she and her brother apply for the Home Caregiving Grant if eligible, and share the remaining cost.
*Say on air: "Made-up numbers. The idea is to see the whole picture on one page."*

## Common myths to correct
- "Putting myself first is selfish." A stable middle protects both ends.
- "I'll sort out my retirement when the kids are grown." The years left to save shrink.
- "Only I can do this." Siblings, schemes and community support exist.

## "Never say" lines
- "You're doing it wrong."
- Product names or "you need insurance for X".
- Guilt framing on either generation.

# Ep 71 — Nominations: CPF and Insurance

**Theme:** Protecting what you build
**Listener's real question:** "I've written a will. Isn't that enough to make sure my family gets everything?"

## Key points (in order)
1. CPF savings are not part of your estate, so a will cannot distribute them. A CPF nomination does.
2. Without a CPF nomination, savings go to the Public Trustee's Office, which distributes them under intestacy laws (or a Muslim inheritance certificate) for a fee. That can take months.
3. Marriage revokes an existing CPF nomination. Divorce does not. Both moments call for a review.
4. A CPF nomination does not cover property bought with CPF, Dependants' Protection Scheme payouts, or CPF Investment Scheme investments.
5. Insurance policies can carry their own nominations under the Insurance Act: revocable (s 133) or trust/irrevocable (s 132). Policyholders must be 18 or older.
6. A simple checklist: will, CPF nomination, insurance nominations, LPA, and a note telling family where documents are.
7. Review after marriage, divorce, children, or the death of a nominee.

## Worked example (illustrative scenario)
"Priya" made a CPF nomination naming her parents when she was single. She later married and wrote a will leaving everything to her husband.
- Her marriage revoked the CPF nomination.
- Her will does not cover CPF.
- If she passed away without renewing it, her CPF would go through the Public Trustee under intestacy rules, not by her own choice.
Her fix is simple: make a new CPF nomination and check her insurance nominations.

## Common myths to correct
- "My will covers everything." CPF and some insurance pass by nomination.
- "My nomination still stands after marriage." Marriage revokes a CPF nomination.
- "Without a nomination, CPF goes to the Government." It goes to family under intestacy rules, through the Public Trustee.
- "Nominations are permanent." Most can be changed, though trust nominations have stricter rules.

## "Never say" lines
- Naming insurers or specific policies.
- "Use a trust nomination for your kids". That is a personal legal decision.
- Public Trustee fee amounts (not verified).

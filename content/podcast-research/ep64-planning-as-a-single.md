# Ep 64 — Planning Well on Your Own

**Theme:** Family and life stages
**Listener's real question:** "I'm single and may stay that way. What should I plan for differently?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Single is a valid long-term plan, not a waiting room. More people are living on their own: one-person households are 16.7% of resident households.
2. One income means one safety net. An emergency fund at the higher end of 3–6 months makes sense for many.
3. Who decides if you can't? An LPA lets you choose a trusted person, who doesn't need to be a spouse.
4. Who receives your CPF and assets? Make a CPF nomination and a will. Without them, intestacy rules decide, and that may not match your wishes.
5. Retirement on one income: CPF LIFE gives a lifelong base. Plan your own top-ups and housing.
6. Long-term care: think early about who would help, since there is no spouse or child by default.
7. Community and friendships are part of the plan.

## Worked example (illustrative scenario)
"Melissa", 38, single, lists her plan:
- Emergency fund: 6 months of her illustrative $3,000 expenses = $18,000.
- LPA: her closest friend as donee for personal welfare, and her brother for property and affairs.
- CPF nomination: split between her mother and a niece.
- Will: leaves a gift to a charity she supports. Without one, her estate would go to her parents under intestacy.
*Say on air: "A made-up example. Your choices are personal."*

## Common myths to correct
- "Estate planning is for people with families." Singles may need it more, so their wishes are followed.
- "My LPA donee has to be family." It can be someone you trust.
- "Singles don't need an emergency fund as big." One income means less backup.

## "Never say" lines
- Pity framing ("don't worry, you might still marry").
- Assumptions about why someone is single.
- Product names.

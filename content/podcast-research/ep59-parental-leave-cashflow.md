# Ep 59 — Parental Leave and the Household Budget

**Theme:** Family and life stages
**Listener's real question:** "How much paid leave do we get when the baby comes, and how do we plan our money around it?"

## Key points (in order)
1. Paid leave has grown. For children born from 1 Apr 2026, an eligible couple can have up to 30 weeks of paid leave in total.
2. Paternity leave is 4 weeks and mandatory for children born from 1 Apr 2025.
3. Shared Parental Leave is 10 weeks for children born from 1 Apr 2026 (6 weeks for births from 1 Apr 2025). By default it is split equally.
4. Government-paid leave has a cap of $2,500 per week per parent. Higher earners may see a pay gap during leave.
5. Give your employer at least 4 weeks' notice. Plan dates together early.
6. Announced at NDR 2026: the Government will fund all child-related leave, and Childcare Leave and Extended Childcare Leave will be merged. The new day count is not confirmed here.
7. Build a "leave budget": expected pay, extra costs, and a small buffer for surprises.

## Worked example (illustrative numbers only)
"Ravi" earns an illustrative $14,000 a month (about $3,230 a week). He takes 4 weeks of paternity leave.
- Government-paid leave is capped at $2,500 a week. If his employer pays only the reimbursed amount, his gap is about $730 a week, roughly $2,900 over 4 weeks.
- He and his wife plan for that gap from savings, and agree to split the shared leave.
*Say on air: "Illustrative. How pay during leave works depends on your employer. Check with HR and the official pages."*

## Common myths to correct
- "Paternity leave is optional for the employer." For births from 1 Apr 2025, 4 weeks is mandatory.
- "Shared leave must go to the mother." It is shared, with an equal default split.
- "Leave pay always matches salary." There is a $2,500 a week cap on government-paid leave.

## "Never say" lines
- Telling couples how to split leave.
- "Employers will pay full salary": that varies.
- Day counts for the merged childcare leave (not verified).

# R05 — The minimum payment trap on your credit card

**Life stage:** First job · **Target length:** ~55 seconds

## Hook (first 2 seconds)
"Paying the minimum on your credit card? Watch this first."

## Voiceover
Paying the minimum on your credit card? Watch this first.

Your first credit card feels like freedom. But here's the catch. The minimum payment is usually just three to five per cent of your unpaid balance, or a fixed amount like fifty dollars, whichever is higher. Pay only that, and the rest keeps getting charged interest.

How much interest? MoneySense says credit card interest is about twenty-five to twenty-nine per cent a year. That's why a small balance can snowball.

There's also a rule from MAS. If your interest-bearing unsecured debt is more than twelve times your monthly income for three months in a row, you won't be given new credit, and your existing credit cards and lines can be suspended.

So three simple habits. Pay your full statement balance if you can. If you can't, pay more than the minimum, and clear the debt with the highest interest first. And if things feel out of control, free help exists, like Credit Counselling Singapore.

Save this for your first card.

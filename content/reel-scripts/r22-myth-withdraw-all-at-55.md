# R22 — Myth: "At 55, I can take out all my CPF"

**Life stage:** Retirement myths · **Target length:** ~60 seconds

## Hook (first 2 seconds)
"At fifty-five, you can withdraw all your CPF. True or false?"

## Voiceover
At fifty-five, you can withdraw all your CPF. True or false?

Mostly false. Here's what actually happens.

When you turn fifty-five, a Retirement Account is created for you, and savings are set aside in it to give you monthly payouts later in life.

What can you take out? According to the CPF Board, you can withdraw five thousand dollars or more once you reach fifty-five. If you've met the Full Retirement Sum, you can withdraw your excess savings above it. For members turning fifty-five in 2026, the Full Retirement Sum is two hundred and twenty thousand four hundred dollars.

If you own a property, you may be able to use it to cover up to half of that sum, so less needs to be set aside in cash. Conditions apply.

So CPF at fifty-five isn't a lump-sum payday. It's the start of setting up your retirement income.

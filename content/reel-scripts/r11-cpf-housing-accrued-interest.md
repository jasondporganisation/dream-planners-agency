# R11 — Used CPF for your flat? Meet "accrued interest"

**Life stage:** BTO / home loan · **Target length:** ~55 seconds

## Hook (first 2 seconds)
"You sold your flat. So why does CPF want money back?"

## Voiceover
You sold your flat. So why does CPF want money back?

When you use your CPF savings to buy a home, that money stops earning CPF interest. So the CPF Board keeps track of the interest it would have earned if it had stayed in your account. That's called accrued interest.

When you sell your property, the sale money first pays off any outstanding housing loan. Then you refund to your own CPF account the CPF you used, including any CPF housing grant, plus the accrued interest.

Important: this money goes back to your own CPF account, not to the government. If you're below fifty-five, it goes into your Ordinary Account. From fifty-five, it first goes towards your Retirement Account, up to your Full Retirement Sum.

And if you sell at market value but the proceeds aren't enough, you don't need to top up the shortfall in cash.

So when you count your sale profit, factor this in first.

# R27 — Inflation: the quiet thief in your wallet

**Life stage:** All ages · **Target length:** ~55 seconds

## Hook (first 2 seconds)
"Your money can shrink without you spending a cent."

## Voiceover
Your money can shrink without you spending a cent.

It's called inflation. When prices go up over time, each dollar buys a little less. The Monetary Authority of Singapore puts it simply: each Singapore dollar won't buy you as much tomorrow as it does today.

Here's an illustration. Say prices rise two per cent a year. A basket of groceries that costs one hundred dollars today would cost about one hundred and twenty-two dollars in ten years. Same basket, more money.

For context, MAS Core Inflation was zero point seven per cent for 2025. In October 2026, MAS forecast it would average one point five to two per cent for 2026. Rates move around from year to year, so the point is not the exact number. It's the long-term effect.

So when you plan for big goals, like a home, your kids' education or retirement, remember to think in future dollars, not today's dollars.

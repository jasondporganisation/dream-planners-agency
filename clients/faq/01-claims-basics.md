# Claims process basics (Q1–Q12)

General information only. It does not consider your specific objectives, financial situation or needs.

### Q1. Something has happened. What is the first thing I should do about a claim?
Look after yourself and your family first; the claim can wait a day. Then let me know what happened, roughly when, and which policies you think may be involved. Keep every bill, receipt, medical report and letter you receive, even ones that look unimportant. Many policies have a time limit for telling the insurer about a claim, and the limit differs from policy to policy, so it helps to start early. Check your own policy documents or ask me, and I will walk you through the steps and the forms for your situation.

### Q2. Which documents will I usually need for a claim?
It depends on the type of claim, but most claims ask for some of these: a completed claim form, your identity document, original or certified bills and receipts, a medical report or doctor's memo, and proof of the event (for example a police report after an accident, or a death certificate). The insurer may also ask for more information while it assesses the claim. Keep copies of everything you send, and note the date you sent it. Check your own policy documents or ask me for the exact list for your claim.

### Q3. How long does a claim take?
There is no single answer. A simple hospital claim filed electronically by the hospital can move quickly, while a claim that needs medical reports, investigations or estate documents can take longer. The biggest cause of delay is usually missing documents, so sending a complete set the first time helps. If the insurer asks for more information, reply as soon as you can. I am happy to follow up on your behalf and let you know where things stand. I cannot promise a timeline, because the insurer decides how long its assessment takes.

### Q4. Can you submit the claim for me?
I can help you prepare the forms, check that the documents are complete, and send them in or show you how to submit them yourself. Some parts must come from you personally, such as your signature, consent for the insurer to get your medical records, and your bank details for payment. For hospital bills, the hospital often files the MediShield Life and Integrated Shield Plan claim directly once you sign the consent forms at admission. Ask me before you start, and I will tell you which parts I can handle for you.

### Q5. What is a pre-authorisation or Letter of Guarantee?
For some planned hospital stays, the hospital can ask your insurer in advance to confirm it will cover part of the bill. The insurer's confirmation, often called a Letter of Guarantee, can reduce or remove the deposit you pay on admission. It is not a promise that the whole bill will be paid. The final amount still depends on the actual treatment, your policy's terms, deductibles and co-payments. Whether this is available depends on the hospital and your policy. Check your own policy documents or ask me before a planned admission.

### Q6. Why might a claim be rejected or only partly paid?
Common reasons are: the treatment or event is not covered by the policy; it falls under an exclusion; the condition existed before the policy started and was excluded or not declared; the policy had lapsed; a waiting period had not ended; or the bill is above the policy's limits. Deductibles and co-payments also mean you pay part of many medical bills yourself. If a claim is declined or reduced, ask for the reason in writing. Check your own policy documents or ask me, and we can go through it together.

### Q7. My claim was rejected. Can I appeal?
Yes. Start by asking the insurer to explain its decision in writing, then send any new information that may help, such as a fuller medical report. I can help you put the facts in order. If you still disagree after the insurer's own review, Singapore has an independent dispute resolution centre for financial services, and MoneySense has general guidance on handling disputes ([moneysense.gov.sg](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/)). Time limits can apply, so do not leave it too long. Ask me and I will help you with the next step.

### Q8. Do I have to tell the insurer about every doctor's visit?
No, not as a routine. You make a claim when you want the policy to pay for something. What matters is that the information you gave when you applied was full and accurate. If you are unsure whether something you did not mention at application could affect a claim, tell me, and we can look at it together before it becomes a problem. Answering honestly is always the safest route. Check your own policy documents or ask me if you are unsure what your policy requires.

### Q9. I was hospitalised overseas. Can I claim?
It depends on the policy. Some health and accident policies cover emergency treatment overseas, often with limits, while MediShield Life and Integrated Shield Plans have their own rules on treatment outside Singapore. Travel insurance may also apply if you had it for that trip. Keep every original bill, receipt and medical report, and ask the overseas hospital for itemised bills in English where possible. Check your own policy documents or ask me, and I will tell you what to send and where.

### Q10. Can I claim from more than one policy for the same event?
Sometimes. Policies that pay a fixed amount when something happens, such as on diagnosis of a covered illness, may each pay according to their own terms. Policies that reimburse bills usually pay only up to the actual cost, so you cannot be paid more than you spent across all of them. Hospital bills have their own order: MediShield Life and any Integrated Shield Plan come first, then MediSave or cash for the balance. Check your own policy documents or ask me, and I will map out which policies may respond.

### Q11. How will the money be paid to me?
Most insurers pay by bank transfer to an account in the policy owner's name, or by cheque. For hospital bills, the payment usually goes straight to the hospital. For a death claim, payment goes to the nominees or to the estate, depending on whether a nomination was made (see [Q49](04-nominations-wills-lpa.md)). Make sure your contact and bank details are up to date with the insurer, because outdated details are a common cause of delay. Check your own policy documents or ask me if your details have changed.

### Q12. Is there anything I should never do when making a claim?
Never hide or change information, sign blank forms, or let someone else sign on your behalf without the proper authority. Do not throw away original bills or reports until the claim is settled. Avoid guessing at answers on a claim form; if you are not sure, say so or ask me. Also be careful of anyone who offers to "speed up" a claim for a fee. Insurers do not charge you to make a claim. If anything about a claim worries you, ask me before you sign or send it.

# CPF (Q27–Q39)

General information only. It does not consider your specific objectives, financial situation or needs. Figures are from cpf.gov.sg (and mom.gov.sg for retirement ages) as checked on 3 Nov 2026. CPF figures usually change on 1 January, so always quote the year.

### Q27. How much goes into my CPF each month?
It depends on your age and wages. For Singapore Citizens and third-year-onwards PRs in the private sector earning above $750 a month, the total rate in 2026 is 37% of wages for ages 55 and below (17% from the employer, 20% from you). The rate is lower for older age bands: 34% above 55 to 60, 25% above 60 to 65, 16.5% above 65 to 70 and 12.5% above 70. Your payslip and CPF statement show the actual amounts. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/employer/employer-obligations/how-much-cpf-contributions-to-pay).

### Q28. Are CPF contribution rates changing in 2027?
Only for some age bands. From 1 Jan 2027, the total rate for workers above 55 to 60 rises from 34% to 35.5%, and for above 60 to 65 from 25% to 26%. Rates for other age bands do not change. From 2027, the extra contributions for ages 55–65 go to the Retirement Account up to the Full Retirement Sum, then to the Ordinary Account. A new age band applies from the first day of the month after your 55th, 60th, 65th or 70th birthday. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/employer/infohub/news/cpf-related-announcements/new-contribution-rates).

### Q29. Is there a cap on CPF contributions?
Yes. CPF contributions are counted only on Ordinary Wages up to $8,000 a month, in both 2026 and 2027. There is also an annual salary ceiling of $102,000. Additional Wages such as bonuses attract CPF only up to $102,000 minus the total Ordinary Wages that were subject to CPF in the year. So if you earn more than these limits, CPF is not paid on the amount above them. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/employer/employer-obligations/what-constitutes-wages-for-cpf-contributions). Ask me if you would like help reading your CPF statement.

### Q30. What are the Basic, Full and Enhanced Retirement Sums?
They are CPF benchmarks for how much is set aside in your Retirement Account when you turn 55, which then affects your CPF LIFE payouts. For members turning 55 in 2026: Basic $110,200, Full $220,400 and Enhanced $440,800. For members turning 55 in 2027: Basic $114,100, Full $228,200 and Enhanced $456,400. The Enhanced sum is 4 times the Basic. These are CPF benchmarks, not a measure of what you personally need to retire. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/infohub/educational-resources/what-is-the-cpf-retirement-sum).

### Q31. What happens to my CPF when I turn 55?
Several things change at 55. Your retirement sum is set based on the year you turn 55 (see Q30), and since 2025 the Special Account closes at 55, so it is no longer correct to assume everyone still has one after that age. What happens to your own balances, and what you can withdraw, follows CPF's rules for your situation. CPF has a full guide at [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/retirement-income/milestones/reaching-age-55). If you are nearing 55, it is a good time for a review, so we can look at how this fits with your other plans.

### Q32. What is CPF LIFE?
CPF LIFE is a national annuity that pays you a monthly income for life, starting between 65 and 70. There are three plans: Escalating (starts lower and rises 2% a year), Standard (level payouts, not adjusted for inflation) and Basic (starts low, and payouts fall once balances drop below $60,000). Any remaining premium balance goes to your loved ones when you pass away. Singapore Citizens and PRs born in 1958 or later with at least $60,000 in retirement savings when payouts start are included automatically. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/retirement-income/monthly-payouts/cpf-life).

### Q33. How much will I get from CPF LIFE?
CPF gives estimates, not guarantees. For example, CPF estimates that someone turning 55 in 2026 on the Standard Plan, with 4% interest, could receive about $950 a month from 65 with the Basic Retirement Sum, about $1,780 with the Full Retirement Sum, and about $3,440 with the Enhanced Retirement Sum. Your own payout depends on your balance, plan choice, start age and interest. The CPF website has a planner that uses your own figures. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/infohub/educational-resources/what-is-the-cpf-retirement-sum).

### Q34. Should I start CPF LIFE at 65 or wait?
That is a personal decision. If you are included automatically, you can choose to start any time from 65 to 70, and payouts start automatically at 70. Payouts rise by up to 7% for each year you defer, up to 35% if you defer to 70. Deferring may suit someone who still has other income; starting earlier may suit someone who needs the income sooner. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/retirement-income/milestones/reaching-age-65). I am happy to go through how this fits with your other retirement income when we next meet.

### Q35. What is the retirement age now?
Since 1 Jul 2026, the minimum retirement age is 64 and the re-employment age is 69. This means an employer cannot ask you to retire before 64 because of age, and generally must offer eligible employees re-employment up to 69. Some older cohorts have a lower retirement age (62 or 63). No date has been officially published for the next increase. These rules are set by the Ministry of Manpower: [mom.gov.sg](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/retirement). Your actual retirement date is your own choice; it does not have to match these ages.

### Q36. Can I use CPF to pay for insurance?
Some insurance, yes. MediShield Life premiums can be paid fully with MediSave, and the private part of an Integrated Shield Plan can be paid with MediSave up to set yearly limits ($300, $600 or $900 depending on age next birthday), with the rest in cash. Rider premiums must be paid in cash. Other types of insurance follow their own rules. Sources: [moh.gov.sg](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/medishield-life/medishield-life-premium-and-subsidy-tables/) · [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/healthcare-financing/getting-supplementary-coverage/integrated-shield-plan). Check your own policy documents or ask me how your premiums are paid.

### Q37. Does my will cover my CPF savings?
No. CPF savings are not part of your estate and cannot be left by will. The only way to decide who receives your CPF savings is a CPF nomination. It covers your Ordinary, Special, MediSave and Retirement Account savings and your CPF LIFE premium balance. It does not cover property bought with CPF, Dependants' Protection Scheme payouts or CPF Investment Scheme investments. Making a nomination is free. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/account-services/providing-for-your-loved-ones/making-a-cpf-nomination). See also [Q40](04-nominations-wills-lpa.md).

### Q38. What happens to my CPF if I have not made a nomination?
The Public Trustee's Office distributes your CPF savings to your family under the intestacy laws, or under a Muslim Inheritance Certificate for Muslims. This can take up to 6 months, and the Public Trustee deducts an administration fee. Your family has less say in who receives what. A nomination avoids this and is free to make. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/account-services/providing-for-your-loved-ones/making-a-cpf-nomination). If you are not sure whether you have a nomination, you can check by logging in to the CPF website with Singpass.

### Q39. Do I need to check my CPF nomination after I marry?
Yes. Marriage automatically cancels an existing CPF nomination, so if you made one before marrying, you need to make a new one. Divorce does not cancel it, so after a divorce it is worth checking whether your nomination still reflects your wishes. You can make a nomination online with Singpass and two witnesses (who cannot be your nominees), or in person by appointment. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/account-services/providing-for-your-loved-ones/making-a-cpf-nomination). A will is also revoked when you marry; see [Q45](04-nominations-wills-lpa.md).

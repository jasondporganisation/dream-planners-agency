# When a family member passes away (Q91–Q100)

General information only. It does not consider your specific objectives, financial situation or needs, and it is not legal advice. These questions often come at a very hard time: answer gently, keep it short, and offer to help with the next step rather than everything at once.

### Q91. My family member has passed away. What do I need to do about their insurance?
I am very sorry for your loss. There is no need to sort everything out at once. When you are ready, let me know, and I can help find out which policies they had and what the insurers need. Usually that includes the death certificate, the policy details if you have them, and identity documents for the person making the claim. Keep any letters or statements you find among their papers. Check their policy documents if you have them, or ask me, and I will guide you one step at a time.

### Q92. How do I find out what policies they had?
Start with their papers, emails and bank statements for premium payments or insurer letters. If I or my team looked after their policies, I can tell you what we have on record. You can also contact the insurers directly with the death certificate. The Life Insurance Association has general information for families at [lia.org.sg](https://www.lia.org.sg/). Ask me, and I will help you make a list and contact each insurer.

### Q93. Who receives the payout from their insurance policies?
It depends on whether they made a nomination. If there is a trust nomination, the payout goes to the nominated spouse or children. If there is a revocable nomination, it goes to the nominees unless a later will says otherwise. If there is no nomination, the insurer may pay up to $150,000 to a "proper claimant" such as a close family member, and the rest goes to the estate, to be shared under the will or the intestacy rules. Source: [moneysense.gov.sg](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/deciding-how-to-transfer-your-estate/). Ask me and I can check what applies.

### Q94. What happens to their CPF savings?
CPF savings do not go through the will. If they made a CPF nomination, CPF pays the nominees. If there was no nomination, the Public Trustee's Office distributes the savings to family under the intestacy laws, or under a Muslim Inheritance Certificate. This can take up to 6 months, and the Public Trustee deducts an administration fee. Any remaining CPF LIFE premium balance is also paid out to loved ones. Sources: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/account-services/providing-for-your-loved-ones/making-a-cpf-nomination) · [pto.mlaw.gov.sg](https://pto.mlaw.gov.sg/deceased-cpf-estate-monies/information-for-next-of-kin-estate-monies/).

### Q95. They did not leave a will. Who inherits?
For non-Muslims domiciled in Singapore, the Intestate Succession Act decides. For example: a spouse with no children or parents gets everything; with a spouse and children, the spouse gets half and the children share half; children only share everything equally; with a spouse and parents but no children, it is half each; parents only share equally. Further relatives follow if none of these survive. Muslim estates follow the Administration of Muslim Law Act. Source: [moneysense.gov.sg](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/intestacy-what-if-you-don-t-have-an-estate-plan/). For the legal steps, a lawyer or the Public Trustee's Office can help.

### Q96. Does a nominated policy payout have to wait for the estate to be settled?
Generally, a payout under a valid nomination goes directly to the nominees and does not have to wait for the estate's legal papers, because it sits outside the estate. The insurer will still need the death certificate, claim forms and proof of identity, and may ask for more information. Without a nomination, any amount above the $150,000 proper-claimant payment goes to the estate, which usually means waiting for the legal papers. Source: [moneysense.gov.sg](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/deciding-how-to-transfer-your-estate/). Check the policy documents or ask me what the insurer needs.

### Q97. Are there time limits for making a death claim?
Policies usually ask for a claim to be made within a reasonable time, and the exact wording varies. In practice, it is better to let the insurer know early, even if you do not have every document yet, because they can tell you exactly what they need and start their process. You do not have to do this in the first few days; take the time you need for your family. Check the policy documents or ask me, and I can make the first contact with the insurer for you.

### Q98. My parent was the policy owner of a policy on my life. What happens now?
When the policy owner passes away but the insured person is still alive, the policy usually continues, but ownership has to pass to someone else. Who that is depends on the policy's terms, any nomination, and the owner's will or the intestacy rules. Until ownership is sorted out, it is important that premiums continue to be paid so the cover does not lapse. Check the policy documents or ask me, and I will find out from the insurer what is needed to transfer ownership.

### Q99. My spouse has passed away. What should I review for myself?
Please take your time; none of this is urgent in the first days. When you are ready, it is worth checking your own nominations, will and LPA, because your spouse may have been named in them. If your spouse was your CPF nominee, you will want to make a new nomination. It is also worth looking at household income, the home loan and your own cover. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/account-services/providing-for-your-loved-ones/making-a-cpf-nomination). Ask me when you are ready, and we can go through it gently.

### Q100. A family member had an LPA and has now passed away. Does it still apply?
No. An LPA only applies while the person who made it is alive and has lost mental capacity. Once they pass away, the LPA ends, and their Donees can no longer act for them. Their estate is then handled by the executor named in their will or, if there is no will, by the person appointed under the intestacy process. Source: [msf.gov.sg](https://www.msf.gov.sg/what-we-do/opg/lasting-power-of-attorney/). For the legal steps, a lawyer or the Public Trustee's Office can help, and I can help with the insurance side.

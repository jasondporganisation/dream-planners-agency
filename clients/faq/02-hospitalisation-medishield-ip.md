# Hospitalisation, MediShield Life and Integrated Shield Plans (Q13–Q26)

General information only. It does not consider your specific objectives, financial situation or needs. Figures are from the MOH and CPF pages as checked on 3 Nov 2026; MediShield Life changes usually take effect in April.

### Q13. What is MediShield Life?
MediShield Life is basic health insurance for large hospital bills and selected costly outpatient treatments, such as dialysis and chemotherapy. All Singapore Citizens and Permanent Residents are covered automatically, for life, whatever their age or health, including pre-existing conditions. It is sized for subsidised treatment in public hospitals (Class B2 and C wards), so it pays a smaller share of a Class A, B1 or private hospital bill. It is designed so that you still pay part of the bill yourself. More at [moh.gov.sg](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/medishield-life/medishield-life/).

### Q14. What is an Integrated Shield Plan?
An Integrated Shield Plan is offered by private insurers. It combines your MediShield Life cover with an extra private insurance part, which is meant for higher ward classes (such as Class A) or private hospitals. You do not lose MediShield Life by having one; it is built on top of it. You can be covered by only one Integrated Shield Plan at a time. Whether you have one, and which ward class it is for, is in your policy schedule. More at [moh.gov.sg](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/integrated-shield-plans/about-integrated-shield-plans/). Check your own policy documents or ask me.

### Q15. What is a deductible?
The deductible is the first part of the claimable bill that you pay yourself each policy year before MediShield Life starts paying. For MediShield Life, it depends on the ward type and your age next birthday. For example, for ages 80 and below it is $2,000 for Class C, $2,500 for Class B2 or B1, $3,500 for Class A or private hospitals, $1,500 for day surgery and $500 for outpatient treatment (from 1 Jun 2026). You pay the full deductible only once per policy year. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/annex/understand-medishield-life-payouts). Integrated Shield Plans have their own deductibles; check your own policy documents or ask me.

### Q16. What is co-insurance?
Co-insurance is the share of the claimable bill you pay after the deductible. Under MediShield Life, the share falls as the bill gets bigger: 10% of the first $5,000, 5% of the next $5,000 and 3% of anything above $10,000 in a policy year (the bands include the deductible). So on a large bill, your share is a smaller percentage. You can pay deductibles and co-insurance with MediSave, cash or a mix, within MediSave limits. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/annex/understand-medishield-life-payouts). Integrated Shield Plans have their own terms; check your own policy documents or ask me.

### Q17. Is there a limit to what MediShield Life pays?
Yes. MediShield Life pays up to $200,000 per policy year, with no lifetime limit. There are also limits per treatment. For example, a normal ward is covered up to $830 a day plus an extra $800 a day for the first 2 days, and ICU up to $5,140 a day. Surgery, implants, dialysis and cancer drugs have their own limits. These figures apply to admissions or treatments from 1 Jun 2026. Source: [moh.gov.sg](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/medishield-life/medishield-life-benefits/). So "no lifetime limit" does not mean "no limits".

### Q18. How much does MediShield Life cost?
It depends on your age next birthday. Before subsidies, annual premiums for policies starting or renewing on or after 1 Apr 2025 (including GST) range from $200 for ages 1–20, to $503 for ages 31–40, $903 for ages 51–60 and $1,326 for ages 66–70. Premiums can be paid fully from MediSave, including a family member's MediSave. Many households receive premium subsidies, and no one loses cover because they cannot afford premiums. The full table is at [moh.gov.sg](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/medishield-life/medishield-life-premium-and-subsidy-tables/).

### Q19. Can I pay my Integrated Shield Plan with MediSave?
Partly. The MediShield Life part can be paid fully with MediSave. For the private insurance part, MediSave can be used only up to a yearly limit based on age next birthday: $300 for ages 40 and below, $600 for ages 41–70 and $900 for ages 71 and above. Anything above that is paid in cash. So it is not accurate to say your whole plan is paid by MediSave. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/healthcare-financing/getting-supplementary-coverage/integrated-shield-plan). Your premium notice shows the split; check your own policy documents or ask me.

### Q20. What is a rider, and can I pay it with MediSave?
A rider is an optional add-on to an Integrated Shield Plan. It usually helps with part of the bill you would otherwise pay yourself, such as co-insurance and, for some older riders, the deductible. Rider premiums must be paid fully in cash; MediSave cannot be used. Riders sold since 1 Apr 2019 must still leave you with at least a 5% co-payment, so a rider does not mean you will never pay anything. Whether you have a rider, and how it works, is in your policy schedule. Check your own policy documents or ask me. More at [moh.gov.sg](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/integrated-shield-plans/about-integrated-shield-plans/).

### Q21. I heard rider rules changed in 2026. Does this affect me?
From 1 Apr 2026, new riders may not cover the minimum Integrated Shield Plan deductible ($1,500–$3,500 a year depending on ward class), and the yearly cap on co-payments was raised from at least $3,000 to at least $6,000. The 5% minimum co-payment is unchanged. Riders sold between 27 Nov 2025 and 31 Mar 2026 must move to the new rules by the first renewal after 1 Apr 2028. Whether this affects you depends on when your rider started. Check your own policy documents or ask me. Source: [moh.gov.sg](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newipriders/).

### Q22. Am I covered for conditions I had before?
MediShield Life covers pre-existing conditions. For serious pre-existing conditions, an Additional Premium of 30% applies for the first 10 years. If you have an Integrated Shield Plan, the private part may exclude a pre-existing condition, but the MediShield Life part inside the plan still covers it. Exclusions on your own plan are listed in your policy documents. Source: [moh.gov.sg](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/medishield-life/coverage-for-pre-existing-conditions/). Check your own policy documents or ask me if you are unsure what applies to you.

### Q23. If I choose a higher ward than my plan covers, what happens?
Your plan will usually pay a smaller share of the bill, because it was designed for a lower ward class or hospital type. Some plans reduce the payout using a set percentage when you stay in a higher ward. MediShield Life on its own is sized for Class B2 and C wards, so a Class A or private bill leaves a larger gap. Before a planned admission, ask the hospital for an estimate and check your plan's ward class. Check your own policy documents or ask me, and we can work out roughly what you may need to pay.

### Q24. Why did my MediShield Life or Integrated Shield Plan premium go up?
Premiums are set by age band, so they usually rise when you move into a new age band, based on your age next birthday. Premiums can also change when the scheme or the plan is revised, and subsidies can change with your household circumstances. Your renewal notice shows the new premium and what part can come from MediSave. Source: [moh.gov.sg](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/medishield-life/medishield-life-premiums-and-subsidies/). Check your own policy documents or ask me if the change looks unusual.

### Q25. Can my family use their MediSave to pay my premiums?
Yes, for MediShield Life, premiums can be paid with a family member's MediSave. For the private part of an Integrated Shield Plan, MediSave can be used only within the age-based limits; whether a family member's MediSave can be used for that part follows CPF's rules, so check with CPF or ask me. Rider premiums are always cash. Arrangements between family members are set up with CPF and the insurer. Sources: [moh.gov.sg](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/medishield-life/medishield-life-premium-and-subsidy-tables/) · [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/healthcare-financing/getting-supplementary-coverage/integrated-shield-plan). Ask me if you would like help setting this up.

### Q26. I am about to be admitted to hospital. What should I check first?
Bring your NRIC and let the hospital know if you have an Integrated Shield Plan, so it can file the claim directly. Ask for a cost estimate, and check whether your plan's ward class matches the ward you are choosing. Ask whether a Letter of Guarantee is possible, which can reduce the deposit. Read the consent forms before signing, and keep copies of everything. Afterwards, keep the final bill and discharge summary. Check your own policy documents or ask me before admission if you can; I can help you understand the likely gap before you go in.

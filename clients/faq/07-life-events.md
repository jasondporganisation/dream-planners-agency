# Life events: marriage, baby, new job, retirement (Q77–Q90)

General information only. It does not consider your specific objectives, financial situation or needs.

## Marriage

### Q77. We are getting married. What should we look at?
Congratulations! A few things change automatically at marriage. Your existing CPF nomination is cancelled, and any will you made before is revoked, so both need to be redone. It is also a good time to check who is named on your insurance policies, how you would manage if one income stopped, and how you will share household costs and any home loan. Sources: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/account-services/providing-for-your-loved-ones/making-a-cpf-nomination) · [moneysense.gov.sg](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/legacy-planning/will/). Ask me and we can set up a joint review after the wedding.

### Q78. Should I change the nominees on my policies after marriage?
It is worth reviewing them. If you named parents or siblings before marriage, you may now want to include your spouse, or you may want to keep things as they are; it is your choice. Remember that a trust nomination can only name a spouse or children, while a revocable nomination is more flexible but can be overridden by a later will. Source: [moneysense.gov.sg](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/deciding-how-to-transfer-your-estate/). Check your own policy documents or ask me, and I can bring the nomination forms to your review.

### Q79. Should my spouse and I make LPAs?
Many couples do, because a spouse cannot automatically make decisions for you if you lose mental capacity. Without an LPA, your family would need to apply to court for a Deputy. Each person makes their own LPA, and spouses often appoint each other plus a backup. For Singapore Citizens, the Form 1 application fee is free; a Certificate Issuer's fee is paid separately. Source: [msf.gov.sg](https://www.msf.gov.sg/what-we-do/opg/lasting-power-of-attorney/). See [Q50–Q52](04-nominations-wills-lpa.md) for more.

## New baby

### Q80. We are expecting a baby. What should we think about?
Congratulations! Common questions at this stage are: how the family would manage if either parent could not work or passed away; how the baby's hospital bills will be covered; who would look after the child (a guardian is named in a will); and whether nominations should now include your child. Every Singapore Citizen baby is covered by MediShield Life automatically, and the premium for ages 1–20 before subsidies is $200 a year. Source: [moh.gov.sg](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/medishield-life/medishield-life-premium-and-subsidy-tables/). Ask me and we can plan a review.

### Q81. Is my newborn covered for hospital bills?
Singapore Citizen and PR children are covered by MediShield Life automatically, for life, including for conditions present at birth. MediShield Life is sized for subsidised ward classes in public hospitals, so it pays a smaller share of a private or Class A bill. If you want cover for higher wards, an Integrated Shield Plan for the child is an option to consider, and there may be timing and health conditions for adding one. Source: [moh.gov.sg](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/medishield-life/medishield-life/). Check your own policy documents or ask me.

### Q82. Do I need a will now that I have a child?
It is a good idea. A will lets you name a guardian for children under 21 and choose an executor, which no other document does. Without a will, your estate follows the intestacy rules; for example, with a spouse and children, half goes to the spouse and half is shared among the children, which may not be how you would want things arranged for a young child. Sources: [moneysense.gov.sg (will)](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/legacy-planning/will/) · [moneysense.gov.sg (intestacy)](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/intestacy-what-if-you-don-t-have-an-estate-plan/). A lawyer can help you draft one.

## New job or change in work

### Q83. I am changing jobs. What happens to my insurance?
Your personal policies stay with you; they do not depend on your employer. What may change is any group insurance your old employer provided, such as group hospitalisation or group life cover, which usually ends when you leave. Your new employer's benefits may be different. Before you leave, find out when your group cover ends and what your new employer offers, so you know if there is a gap. Check your own policy documents or ask me, and bring your new benefits summary to a quick review.

### Q84. My employer gives me insurance. Do I still need my own?
It depends. Group cover from an employer can be very helpful, but it usually ends when you leave the job, the amounts may be modest, and the terms are set by your employer, not you. Personal policies stay with you through job changes and stay in place if you later develop a health condition. Whether you need more depends on your family, debts and goals. Check your own policy documents or ask me, and bring your employee benefits summary to your review so we can look at the whole picture.

### Q85. I got a pay rise. What changes for CPF?
CPF contributions are a percentage of your wages, so they usually go up with your pay, up to the Ordinary Wage ceiling of $8,000 a month (2026 and 2027). In 2026, the total rate for ages 55 and below is 37% (17% employer, 20% employee). A pay rise is also a natural moment to check whether your protection still matches your income and lifestyle. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/employer/employer-obligations/how-much-cpf-contributions-to-pay). Ask me if you would like a quick review.

### Q86. I am becoming self-employed. What should I think about?
Without an employer, there is usually no group insurance, no paid sick leave and no employer CPF contributions, so it is worth thinking about how you would cover hospital bills, a period when you cannot work, and your retirement savings. Self-employed persons have their own CPF and MediSave rules, which are set out on the CPF website ([cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/)). MediShield Life continues to cover you as a Singapore Citizen or PR. Check your own policy documents or ask me, and we can look at the gaps together.

## Retirement

### Q87. I am planning to retire. Where do I start?
Start with a simple picture: your expected monthly spending, your expected income (CPF LIFE, any other savings or policies, and any part-time work), and the gap between them. Remember to plan for a long time: on average, a Singapore resident who reaches 65 can expect to live about 21.6 more years (SingStat, 2025), so savings may need to last into the mid-80s or beyond. That is an average, not a prediction. Source: [singstat.gov.sg](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/find-data/explore-data-themes/population/death-and-life-expectancy/latest-news-data). Ask me and we can work through it at a review.

### Q88. Can my employer make me retire?
Since 1 Jul 2026, the minimum retirement age is 64 and the re-employment age is 69 (with lower retirement ages for some older cohorts). In general, an employer cannot make you retire before the minimum retirement age because of age, and must offer re-employment to eligible employees up to the re-employment age. No date has been officially published for the next increase. The rules and conditions are set by the Ministry of Manpower: [mom.gov.sg](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/retirement). For a specific situation, check with MOM or your HR department.

### Q89. What happens to my policies when I retire?
Your personal policies continue as long as premiums are paid and the policy term has not ended. Some policies have a premium term that ends around retirement; others need premiums for longer. Hospitalisation plan premiums usually rise with age, so it is worth planning how you will pay them, including how much can come from MediSave (see [Q19](02-hospitalisation-medishield-ip.md)). Some policies may also mature and pay out. Check your own policy documents or ask me, and I can give you a timeline of premiums and maturities.

### Q90. Will CPF LIFE be enough for me to live on?
It depends on your spending, your CPF balances and your other income. CPF LIFE gives a monthly income for life, which helps with the risk of outliving your savings. As an example, CPF estimates that someone turning 55 in 2026 who sets aside the Full Retirement Sum could receive about $1,780 a month from 65 on the Standard Plan. That is an estimate, not a guarantee, and the Standard Plan is not adjusted for inflation. Source: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/infohub/educational-resources/what-is-the-cpf-retirement-sum). Ask me and we can compare it with your own expected spending.

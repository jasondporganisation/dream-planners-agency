# Policy reviews (Q66–Q76)

General information only. It does not consider your specific objectives, financial situation or needs.

### Q66. Why should I review my policies if nothing has gone wrong?
Because life changes even when nothing goes wrong. Your income, family, home, health and goals shift over the years, and so do government schemes such as CPF and MediShield Life. A review checks that what you have still matches your life today, that nominations are up to date, and that you know what you are covered for before you ever need to claim. It is also a chance to tidy up policies you may have forgotten about. A review is not a sales meeting. You can always say "nothing to change this year".

### Q67. How often should I have a review?
Once a year is a good rhythm for most people, plus a check-in after any major life event, such as marriage, a new baby, a new job, buying a home, a serious illness in the family, or retirement. Government figures often change on 1 January (CPF) and in April (MediShield Life), so an annual review also keeps you up to date with those. If a year has been quiet, the review can be short. Just let me know a time that suits you, or reply to my reminder.

### Q68. What should I bring to a review?
Bring whatever you have handy: your policy documents or a list of policies, any new policies bought elsewhere, recent premium notices, your latest CPF statement if you would like to talk about CPF, and any letters from insurers you were unsure about. It also helps to think about what has changed in your life since we last met, and any worries or questions on your mind. If you do not have documents with you, that is fine. I can pull up what I have on file for the policies I look after.

### Q69. What will we cover in a review?
Usually: what has changed in your life; a simple summary of what your policies cover in broad terms; whether premiums are paid up and affordable; whether nominations, your will and LPA are in place and up to date; any changes to government schemes that affect you; and any questions you have. If there is a gap or something you want to change, we will note it and you can take time to think. Nothing has to be decided in the meeting. Ask me if there is something specific you would like to add.

### Q70. Can you review policies I bought from someone else?
Yes, I can help you list and understand them in general terms, so you have one clear picture of your cover. I will be careful and fair in how I describe them, and I will not suggest cancelling a policy without a proper comparison of the pros and cons. For questions about the terms of a policy I did not arrange, the issuing insurer or your adviser for that policy is the right source. Check your own policy documents or ask me, and bring them along to your review.

### Q71. How do I know how much cover I need?
There is no single right number. It depends on things like who depends on your income, your debts and home loan, your savings and CPF, the lifestyle you want for your family, and what you can comfortably afford in premiums. Broad rules of thumb you see online are only a starting point. MoneySense has free, neutral guides on planning your insurance needs ([moneysense.gov.sg](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/)). The best answer comes from looking at your own situation, which we can do together at a review.

### Q72. I have lots of policies. Do I have too much?
Possibly, possibly not. Several policies can overlap, or they can each do a different job, such as hospital bills, income if you cannot work, and support for your family if you pass away. For hospitalisation, you can have only one Integrated Shield Plan at a time ([moh.gov.sg](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/integrated-shield-plans/about-integrated-shield-plans/)). Before cancelling anything, we should look at what each policy actually does, because ending a policy can cost you value and cover you may not get back. Check your own policy documents or ask me, and we can map them out.

### Q73. Can I reduce my cover instead of cancelling?
Often, yes. Some policies let you reduce the sum assured, remove a rider, or change the premium term, which can lower your premiums while keeping some protection. Each change has trade-offs, such as lower payouts or values, and some changes cannot be undone later without new health checks. A trust nomination may also mean you need your nominees' agreement for certain changes. Check your own policy documents or ask me before deciding, and I will set out the options in writing so you can compare.

### Q74. My health has changed since I bought my policy. Does that affect my cover?
For a policy that is already in force, a change in your health after it started generally does not change the cover, as long as the information you gave when you applied was accurate. That is one reason to think carefully before replacing an older policy: a new one may exclude conditions you have developed since. If you are unsure whether something should have been mentioned when you applied, tell me and we can look at it. Check your own policy documents or ask me.

### Q75. Should I review my hospitalisation plan every year?
It is worth a quick look each year. Premiums rise as you move into higher age bands, the MediSave limits differ by age ($300, $600 or $900 a year for the private part of an Integrated Shield Plan), and rider rules changed for new riders from 1 Apr 2026. MOH also adjusts MediShield Life from time to time, with a further deductible change due on 1 Apr 2027 (the new amounts are not yet published). Sources: [moh.gov.sg](https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/integrated-shield-plans/about-integrated-shield-plans/) · [moh.gov.sg (new rider rules)](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newipriders/). Check your own policy documents or ask me, and we can check that the plan still suits your budget and ward preference.

### Q76. How do I update my address, phone number or bank details?
Let me know and I will help you with the insurer's change form, or you can update most details yourself through the insurer's official app or customer portal. Keeping these up to date matters: premium reminders, renewal notices and claim payments all depend on them. If you move overseas, let me know too, because it may affect some policies. For CPF, update your details on the CPF website with Singpass. Check your own policy documents or ask me if you are not sure which policies need updating.

### Nominations and your LPA

**CPF nomination.** Your CPF savings are not part of your estate and **cannot be left by will**. A CPF nomination decides who receives them, and making one is free. Without a nomination, the Public Trustee's Office distributes the savings under the intestacy laws (or a Muslim Inheritance Certificate). This can take up to 6 months, and an admin fee is deducted. **Marriage cancels an existing CPF nomination; divorce does not** ([CPF Board](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/account-services/providing-for-your-loved-ones/making-a-cpf-nomination)).

**Insurance nominations.** A *trust* nomination takes priority over your will, and only your spouse or children can be trust nominees. With a *revocable* nomination, the insurer follows whichever is your latest document: the nomination or your will ([MoneySense](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/deciding-how-to-transfer-your-estate/)).

**Lasting Power of Attorney.** An LPA lets you choose who makes decisions for you if you lose mental capacity. Without one, your family has to apply to court for a Deputy to be appointed. For Singapore Citizens, applying for LPA Form 1 is free. You still pay a Certificate Issuer, such as an accredited doctor or a lawyer, to certify it ([MSF, Office of the Public Guardian](https://www.msf.gov.sg/what-we-do/opg/lasting-power-of-attorney/)).

**Three practical tips**

1. **Check that your CPF nomination still reflects your wishes**, especially if you've married since you made it. Marriage cancels it.
2. **Look at your nominations and your will together.** A nomination doesn't replace a will: assets outside CPF and nominated policies still follow your will or the intestacy rules.
3. **If you don't have an LPA yet, have the conversation now.** Choose who you trust and talk to them before you apply.

# Scoring Rubric — Applications and Interviews

> Based on TAFEP's fair-selection approach: select on merit, using criteria set **before** reading applications, applied the same way to everyone ([Tripartite Guidelines on Fair Employment Practices](https://www.tal.sg/tafep/getting-started/fair/tripartite-guidelines); [TAFEP Fair Recruitment and Selection Toolkit, 2026](https://www.tal.sg/tafep/resources/tools-and-templates/2026/fair-recruitment-and-selection-toolkit)). The Workplace Fairness Act will put much of this into law; prepare as if it already applies ([TAFEP, 2026](https://www.tal.sg/tafep/resources/articles/2026/fair-recruitment-practices-for-employers-in-singapore---preparing-for-the-workplace-fairness-act)).

## How to use it
1. **Two scorers** (e.g. Jason + one other team member), scoring separately before discussing.
2. **Hide names and institution** when scoring Section C answers if the form tool allows it (export answers to a sheet with only an applicant number).
3. Score only the evidence in the answer. Don't reward polish, accent, school prestige, or being "a good fit like us".
4. If scores differ by 2+ points on any criterion, discuss and record the agreed score and the reason.
5. Record scores and short reasons in the score sheet. Keep it with the selection records (PDPA retention: see [`07-pdpa-consent.md`](07-pdpa-consent.md)).
6. **Eligibility (Section B) is pass/fail, not scored.** These are the principal programme's conditions. Before an offer, the programme lead **sights ID to confirm each shortlisted applicant is 21+ by the cut-off** and records only "age checked: yes, date, by whom". If an applicant is unsure about a condition, help them check; don't reject on "unsure" alone.

## Stage 1 — Written application (max 20 points)

Each question scored 0–4.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Not answered, or off-topic |
| 1 | Very general; little evidence |
| 2 | Some relevant evidence or a reasonable attempt |
| 3 | Clear, specific, relevant evidence |
| 4 | Clear, specific evidence plus reflection (what they learned, what they'd do differently) |

| Q | Criterion | What a 4 looks like | Not a factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 Why this internship | Motivation to learn | Specific learning goals; link to their interests or plans | Wanting to "become an adviser" is **not** scored higher than any other goal |
| 9 Explain something | Clear communication | Accurate, plain English, uses an example, a friend would get it | Technical vocabulary; English fluency beyond clarity |
| 10 Teamwork | Collaboration | Clear own role, how they handled a difficulty, outcome | Size or prestige of the project/club |
| 11 Doing the right thing | Integrity and judgement | Describes the rule, the pressure, their choice and why | — |
| 12 Making something | Creating content | Describes audience, purpose, what they made, what they'd improve | Having a link; follower counts |

**Shortlist:** top [JASON: number, e.g. 3× places] scores, plus anyone within 1 point of the cut-off. Ties broken by Q11, then Q9.

## Stage 2 — Interview (max 20 points)
Use the questions in [`05-interview-guide.md`](05-interview-guide.md). Score each of the 5 areas 0–4 with the same scale.

| Area | Question(s) | What a 4 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Learning motivation | Q1 | Specific goals; curious; asks good questions |
| Explaining clearly | Q2 (explain task) | Accurate, simple, checks understanding |
| Teamwork | Q3 | Specific example with own role and reflection |
| Integrity and rules | Q4, Q5 | Understands why interns can't advise; sensible handling of the scenario |
| Content and creativity | Q6 | A concrete, sensible capstone idea for a defined audience |

## Final decision
- **Total = Stage 1 (20) + Stage 2 (20) = 40.**
- Offer to the highest totals, up to the cohort cap (8 interns in total, and 2–4 per buddy/supervisor).
- Keep a short reserve list in case someone declines.
- **Not considered at any stage:** age (beyond the yes/no 21+ programme condition), gender, race, religion, nationality (beyond the yes/no SC/PR programme condition), marital or family status, disability, appearance, school ranking, grades, family connections, social-media following, interest in a future adviser career, or ability to bring contacts.

## Feedback to unsuccessful applicants
Offer short, factual feedback on request, based on the criteria (e.g. "Your teamwork answer was strong; your explanation answer could have used an example"). Never mention a protected characteristic.

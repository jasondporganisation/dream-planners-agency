# Six Workshop Outlines

> Each workshop: **2.5 hours** (about 60 min teaching, 60 min activity, 20 min debrief, 10 min break).
> **Facilitator rules:** general education only. No products, no insurer or firm names, no premiums or returns, no "you should buy". If an intern asks "what should I get?", say: "That's a question for a licensed adviser looking at your full situation. Today we're learning how it works."
> **Figures:** use only figures from official sites, with the date. Figures already checked are in [`compliance/sg-fact-pack-2026-11.md`](../../compliance/sg-fact-pack-2026-11.md). Re-check anything that changes on 1 Jan 2027 if you run this later.
> **Interns' own numbers stay private.** Activities use made-up personas. Nobody is asked to share their income, debts or family finances.

## Workshop 1 — Budgeting and your first paycheque

**Objectives:** read a payslip; explain gross vs take-home pay; build a simple budget; understand "pay yourself first".

**Slides outline (12 slides)**
1. Title and the one question: "Where does my money go?"
2. Gross pay, deductions, take-home pay — a sample (fictional) payslip
3. What CPF deductions are, in one line (Workshop 3 covers detail)
4. Needs, wants, savings: one simple split, and why there's no single "right" ratio
5. Fixed vs variable costs
6. "Pay yourself first": save on payday, not at month end
7. Tracking: notebook, spreadsheet, bank app — pick one you'll actually use
8. Lifestyle creep: what happens when pay goes up
9. Irregular costs: annual fees, gifts, festive seasons
10. Common mistakes (no buffer, forgetting subscriptions)
11. Where to learn more: [MoneySense — Managing your money](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/managing-your-money/)
12. Recap and activity brief

**Activity — "Persona Budget" (45 min):** Pairs get a fictional persona card (e.g. "Aisha, 23, first job, lives with parents, wants to travel in a year"). They build a one-month budget in a provided spreadsheet, then add one surprise cost and rebalance. Debrief: what trade-offs did you make?

**Takeaway:** one-page budget template.

## Workshop 2 — Emergency funds, debt and credit

**Objectives:** explain why an emergency fund matters; describe how credit-card interest builds; compare "good" and "costly" borrowing in general terms.

**Slides outline (11 slides)**
1. Title: "When life happens"
2. What counts as an emergency (and what doesn't)
3. How people size an emergency fund — common rules of thumb and why they vary
4. Where people keep it: easy to reach, low risk (no product names)
5. How credit-card interest and the minimum payment work — [MoneySense on credit card interest and charges](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/understanding-credit-card-interest-and-charges/)
6. Worked example (fictional): paying only the minimum vs paying in full
7. Buy-now-pay-later: the convenience and the risk
8. Study loans and other borrowing: questions to ask before borrowing
9. If you're struggling: talk early, and where to get free help (official/community services)
10. Where to learn more: [MoneySense — Understanding credit cards](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/understanding-credit-cards/)
11. Recap and activity brief

**Activity — "The Minimum Payment Trap" (45 min):** Using a pre-built spreadsheet with a fictional balance, groups compare three repayment plans and chart the total interest. Each group writes a 3-sentence plain-English explanation for a friend.

## Workshop 3 — CPF basics for young adults

**Objectives:** name the CPF accounts and what each is for; explain who pays contributions; know where to check facts.

**Slides outline (12 slides)**
1. Title: "That big deduction on your payslip"
2. What CPF is for: retirement, housing, healthcare
3. The accounts and what each does — [CPF Board](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member)
4. Who pays: employer and employee shares. For employees aged 55 and below earning above $750 a month, the total is 37% (17% employer, 20% employee) in 2026 ([CPF Board](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/employer/employer-obligations/how-much-cpf-contributions-to-pay)) — check the date before using
5. Why contributions differ by age and wage level (in general terms)
6. Interest on CPF savings — where to find current rates ([CPF Board](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/growing-your-savings/earning-higher-returns/earning-attractive-interest))
7. MediShield Life in one line (basic health insurance for all Singapore Citizens and PRs)
8. Using CPF for housing — the idea only; details later in life
9. Retirement: CPF LIFE in one line, and why starting early matters (no projections)
10. How to log in and read your own statement (do this at home, privately)
11. Where to learn more: CPF Board's educational resources
12. Recap and activity brief

**Activity — "CPF Myth or Fact" (40 min):** Teams get 10 statements (e.g. "CPF money can only be used at 65"). They must find the answer **on cpf.gov.sg**, paste the link, and explain it in one sentence. Score: 1 point per correct answer with a correct link.

## Workshop 4 — Insurance concepts in plain English

**Objectives:** explain what insurance is (pooling risk); describe the main types of cover in general; read the key parts of any policy summary; know what a needs analysis is and why it needs a licensed adviser.

**Slides outline (12 slides)**
1. Title: "What are we actually paying for?"
2. Risk and pooling: a simple story with 100 people
3. Main categories in general terms: health, life, disability/income, long-term care, general (travel, home) — **no products, no insurers**
4. What national schemes already provide: MediShield Life, CareShield Life — [CPF Board, CareShield Life](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member/healthcare-financing/careshield-life)
5. Key words: premium, deductible, co-insurance, exclusions, waiting period
6. How to read a policy summary or product summary — [MoneySense, interpreting your insurance documents](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/interpreting-your-insurance-documents/)
7. What a needs analysis covers, and why only licensed representatives may do one — [MoneySense, financial advisory process](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/financial-advisory-process/)
8. Questions a consumer can ask any adviser
9. Free-look periods and cooling-off — check the official source for current rules
10. Common misunderstandings (e.g. "I'm young, I don't need to think about this")
11. Where to learn more
12. Recap and activity brief

**Activity — "Read the Fine Print" (45 min):** Groups receive a **fictional, made-up** policy summary written by the facilitator (no real insurer or product). They find the premium, deductible, three exclusions and the waiting period, then write two questions they'd ask before signing. **Do not use any real product document.**

## Workshop 5 — Scams, fraud and checking who you're dealing with

**Objectives:** recognise common scam patterns; check whether a person or firm is regulated; know where to report.

**Slides outline (11 slides)**
1. Title: "Too good to be true"
2. How scams work: urgency, authority, secrecy, emotion
3. Common types: impersonation, investment, job, phishing, romance
4. Red flags in financial offers: guaranteed high returns, pressure to decide now, unregistered sellers
5. Check the person: [MAS Register of Representatives](https://eservices.mas.gov.sg/rr) and [MoneySense, who you could be dealing with](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/financial-advice-who-you-could-be-dealing-with/)
6. Check the company: [MAS Investor Alert List](https://www.mas.gov.sg/investor-alert-list)
7. Tools: [ScamShield](https://www.scamshield.gov.sg/) and the ScamShield helpline; bank features that lock money — [MoneySense on MoneyLock](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/scams/moneylock/)
8. Job scams aimed at students (e.g. "easy money" tasks, money mules)
9. What to do if it happens: stop, call your bank, report
10. Talking to parents and grandparents about scams
11. Recap and activity brief

**Activity — "Spot the Scam" (45 min):** Groups sort 12 fictional messages (texts, ads, DMs) into "scam", "legit" or "can't tell yet", and for each "can't tell", list the check they'd run (register, alert list, call the official number). Includes at least one fake "financial adviser" DM promising returns.

## Workshop 6 — Talking about money fairly: honest content and the rules behind it

**Objectives:** explain why financial content is regulated; apply a simple fairness checklist; prepare the capstone for compliance review.

**Slides outline (12 slides)**
1. Title: "Would I be comfortable if a regulator read this?"
2. Why rules exist: people make big decisions from content
3. MAS digital advertising guidelines (FSG-03), in force since 25 Mar 2026: the firm is responsible for its representatives' posts; each post must stand on its own — [MAS FSG-03](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas/regulations-and-financial-stability/regulations-guidance-and-licensing/financial-advisers/guidelines/guidelines-on-standards-of-conduct-for-digital-advertising-activities.pdf)
4. Fair and balanced: no "guaranteed" unless it really is; risks shown as clearly as benefits
5. Titles matter: who can call themselves what — [MAS FAQs on the FAA](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas/regulations-and-financial-stability/regulations-guidance-and-licensing/financial-advisers/faq/faqs-on-financial-advisers-act-financial-advisers-regulations-notices-and-guidelines.pdf)
6. Why the internship rules say interns can't advise or promote products
7. Sourcing: official site, date checked, link
8. Plain English: short sentences, define jargon, one idea per slide
9. Inclusive content: no stereotypes about age, gender, race or income
10. **The capstone checklist** (below)
11. How compliance review works here: self-check → Jason → (only if publishing later) the principal insurer's approval
12. Recap

**Capstone checklist (interns use this on their own draft):**
- [ ] Every fact has an official source link and a "checked on" date
- [ ] No products, insurers, firms, premiums or returns
- [ ] No "guaranteed", "best", "risk-free", "you will", income or earnings claims
- [ ] No "contact an adviser", phone numbers, links to sign-ups, or calls to action other than "learn more at [official site]"
- [ ] Balanced: mentions limits or "it depends" where true
- [ ] Clear to an 18-year-old reading on a phone
- [ ] No real people's personal data, photos or stories without consent
- [ ] Inclusive and respectful

**Activity — "Red Pen" (45 min):** Groups get 4 fictional social posts with deliberate problems (a "guaranteed" claim, a missing source, an income boast, a product plug). They mark each problem with the checklist and rewrite one post. Then they run the same checklist on their own capstone draft.

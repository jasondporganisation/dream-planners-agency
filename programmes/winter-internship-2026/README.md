## 1. Purpose

A short, paid, **non-advising** internship that lets university students see how a financial-advisory team works, learn practical personal-finance skills, and build a real piece of work: a personal-finance **education** piece, checked by compliance.

It is **not** a sales programme. Interns are not licensed, give no advice, sell nothing and contact no clients. After the internship, interns may choose to come to an optional career talk (see [`09-career-talk-path.md`](09-career-talk-path.md)). Nobody is asked to join anything during the programme.

What a good result looks like for the intern:
- They can explain budgeting, emergency funds, CPF basics, insurance concepts in general terms, and how to spot scams.
- They have a portfolio piece and a written reference.
- They understand what advisers do and the rules advisers work under, so they can make their own career decisions.

## 3. Eligibility

### Default: the principal's 5-day programme
The principal's programme sets the eligibility rules, and the team must not enter anyone who doesn't meet them. Per the principal's leaders' briefing (1 Oct 2026) [JASON: re-check against the current briefing and forms before the form goes live], applicants must:
1. Be a current **undergraduate** (degree course). Polytechnic and ITE students and fresh graduates do not qualify.
2. Be **21 or older** on the principal's cut-off date ([JASON: confirm; the 1 Oct briefing says 21 as of 31 Dec 2025, i.e. born in 2004 or earlier]).
3. Be a **Singapore Citizen or Permanent Resident**.
4. If liable for full-time National Service, have **completed** it.
5. Not be a current or discharged bankrupt, and have no unsecured debt other than a study loan.
6. Not be on a scholarship or bonded to a government body or company.
7. Be available for all 5 days, with no overseas exchange or trip during 7–11 Dec.

Student's Pass holders are **not** eligible for the default programme (SC/PR only). The MOM vacation-work rules below apply only to the extended version if it is ever run as a stand-alone team programme.

### Age: why it is asked, and how it is checked
- TAFEP tells employers to "remove fields on age" and notes "NRIC numbers can reveal the age of a person" ([TAFEP, Preparing job application forms](https://www.tal.sg/tafep/employment-practices/recruitment/preparing-job-application-forms)). The same page says that where information that "may be viewed as discriminatory" is needed, employers should state the reason clearly. TAFEP's Workplace Fairness Act guidance allows protected characteristics in requirements only where they are "a genuine requirement of the job" ([TAFEP, 2026](https://www.tal.sg/tafep/resources/articles/2026/fair-recruitment-practices-for-employers-in-singapore---preparing-for-the-workplace-fairness-act)).
- Here the minimum age is a **condition of the principal's programme**, aligned with MAS Notice FAA-N26, which requires representatives to "be at least 21 years old" ([MAS FAA-N26](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/regulation/notices/cmg/notice-faa-n26/faa-n26-notice-on-competency-requirements-for-representatives-of-financial-advisers-1.pdf)). So the form asks **one yes/no question** ("Will you be 21 or older on [cut-off date]?") and states that reason next to it. It does **not** ask for date of birth or NRIC.
- **Check each intern's age** before their form goes to the principal: the programme lead sights a student card or other ID showing the date of birth (in person or on video), and records only "age checked: yes, [date], [by whom]". Do not photograph, copy or write down the ID number. Many undergraduates are under 21 (most women in years 1–2, and anyone who did not do National Service); do not assume.
- Age is used only for this eligibility check. It is never scored, and no upper age limit is applied.
- **Under 21** applicants cannot join the default programme. Thank them, and tell them they may apply for a future run once eligible. They are not added to any list.

### Extended version only (if run stand-alone)
If the team ever runs the 3-week version outside the principal's programme and opens it to under-21s or to Student's Pass holders: Student's Pass holders may work during vacation without a work pass only if they are full-time students at an institution on MOM's list, hold an ICA-issued Student's Pass and are aged 14 or above; term-time work is capped at 16 hours a week ([MOM, work pass exemption for foreign students](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/work-pass-exemption-for-foreign-students)). Anyone under 18 needs a parent or guardian to countersign. All IHL students are above the MOM age bands for "children" (13 to under 15) and "young persons" (15 to under 16) ([MOM, young persons and children](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/young-persons-and-children)). Interns under 21 never receive the career-talk message.

### School (MOE/IHL) internship guidance
- MOE leaves the allowance to be "mutually agreed upon between the intern and the company" before the internship starts, and says IHLs give companies "a recommended allowance range" ([MOE parliamentary reply, 4 Feb 2025](https://www.moe.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-replies/20250204-proposal-for-guidelines-on-minimum-monthly-allowance-for-students-undergoing-full-time-internships)). IHLs "encourage companies to offer student interns a fair internship allowance, based on industry norms" ([MOE reply, 25 Sep 2025](https://www.moe.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-replies/20250925-measures-to-ensure-fair-internship-compensation-for-polytechnic-ite-and-university-students)).
- IHL employer guides ask for written terms, learning objectives and a named supervisor. NUS recommends "at least $800 per month (for other roles)" and limits student-pass holders to 16 hours a week **during term** ([NUS employer guidelines](https://fass.nus.edu.sg/cnm/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2020/06/internship-guidelines-for-employers.pdf)). NTU recommends "$1250 per month" ([NTU](https://blogs.ntu.edu.sg/employers/?p=1408)). The approved S$400 for 5 days is about S$80 a day, above these norms on a daily basis (about S$36–57 a day at 22 working days a month).
- This is **not** a for-credit school attachment. If an intern wants to count it towards their course, they must ask their school first, and the school's terms then apply. ## 4. What interns may and may not do

The line comes from MAS: only "fit and proper and adequately qualified persons" may give financial advice, and MAS has banned unappointed people who did ([MAS enforcement, 2023](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/enforcement/enforcement-actions/2023/mas-bans-four-former-representatives-and-one-other-individual-for-unlawful-sub-agency-activities)). Interns are not appointed representatives.

| Interns MAY | Interns MAY NOT |
|---|---|
| Attend **internal** team meetings, trainings and huddles (with client names and details removed or the item skipped) | Attend any client meeting, call, video call or event with clients, in person or online |
| Attend the 6 workshops and do the activities | Give, or appear to give, financial advice or recommendations to anyone, including friends and family |
| Research public information (e.g. MoneySense, CPF Board, MAS websites) | Name, describe, compare, quote or explain any insurer's or firm's products or premiums |
| Draft general financial-literacy education content for the capstone | Publish anything (including on their own social media) about the internship's financial content without written approval |
| Ask advisers about their work, training and the rules they follow | Contact, collect, view or handle any client's or prospect's personal data |
| Help with internal admin that contains no personal data | Generate leads, collect contacts, run surveys of the public, or hand out business cards |
| Present their capstone internally | Be paid, rewarded or ranked on sales, leads, sign-ups or referrals |
| Choose to attend the optional career talk after the programme | Be asked to sit CMFAS exams, sign a representative contract or "commit" during the programme |

**If unsure, the intern stops and asks the supervisor.** Supervisors use the same rule: if it might look like advice or selling, an intern doesn't do it.

## 1. Purpose

A short, paid, **non-advising** winter internship that lets students see how a financial-advisory team works, learn practical personal-finance skills, and build a real piece of work: a personal-finance **education** campaign, checked by compliance.

It is **not** a sales programme and **not** a recruitment funnel. Interns are not licensed, give no advice, sell nothing and contact no clients. Interns aged 21 or over may choose to come to an optional career talk afterwards (see [`09-career-talk-path.md`](09-career-talk-path.md)). Nobody is asked to join anything.

What a good result looks like for the intern:
- They can explain budgeting, emergency funds, CPF basics, insurance concepts in general terms, and how to spot scams.
- They have a portfolio piece (the capstone) and a written reference.
- They understand what advisers do and the rules advisers work under, so they can make their own career decisions.

## 3. Eligibility

Applicants must:
1. Be a **full-time student** at a Singapore university, polytechnic, ITE or arts institution, **or** have graduated from one in the last 12 months.
2. Have the **right to work in Singapore** for the internship dates. Singapore Citizens and Permanent Residents qualify. Student's Pass holders may work during vacation without a work pass only if they meet MOM's criteria (full-time student at an institution on MOM's list, Student's Pass issued by ICA, aged 14 or above) ([MOM, work pass exemption for foreign students](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/work-pass-exemption-for-foreign-students)). [JASON: check each Student's Pass holder's institution is on MOM's vacation list and that the dates fall inside the official vacation.]
3. Be available for the full programme dates.

### Age
- **No upper or lower age is used to select interns, and the application form does not ask for age, date of birth or NRIC.** TAFEP tells employers to "remove fields on age, gender, race, religion, marital status and family responsibilities, or disability", and notes "NRIC numbers can reveal the age of a person" ([TAFEP, Preparing job application forms](https://www.tal.sg/tafep/employment-practices/recruitment/preparing-job-application-forms)).
- **Expect many interns to be under 21**, including most first- and second-year undergraduates and almost all polytechnic students. This is fine: the programme is designed for them. It also means **no intern under 21 can be appointed as a representative**, because MAS Notice FAA-N26 requires representatives to "be at least 21 years old" ([MAS FAA-N26](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/regulation/notices/cmg/notice-faa-n26/faa-n26-notice-on-competency-requirements-for-representatives-of-financial-advisers-1.pdf)).
- **Age is collected only after an offer is accepted**, as one yes/no question on the onboarding form ("Will you be 18 or older on 7 Dec 2026?"), so the team knows whether a parent or guardian should countersign the offer letter. It is never used for selection, and it is not used to invite anyone to the career talk (interns opt in themselves).
- All IHL students are above the MOM age bands for "children" (13 to under 15) and "young persons" (15 to under 16) ([MOM, young persons and children](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/young-persons-and-children)). If an applicant would be under 16 on the start date, pause and get advice before offering.

### School (MOE/IHL) internship guidance
- MOE leaves the allowance to be "mutually agreed upon between the intern and the company prior to the commencement of the internship", and says IHLs give companies "a recommended allowance range" and encourage "a fair internship allowance, based on industry norms" ([MOE parliamentary reply, 4 Feb 2025](https://www.moe.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-replies/20250204-proposal-for-guidelines-on-minimum-monthly-allowance-for-students-undergoing-full-time-internships); [MOE reply, 25 Sep 2025](https://www.moe.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-replies/20250925-measures-to-ensure-fair-internship-compensation-for-polytechnic-ite-and-university-students)).
- IHL employer guides ask for written key terms, learning objectives and a named supervisor. NUS recommends "at least $800 per month (for other roles)" and limits student-pass holders to 16 hours a week **during term** ([NUS employer guidelines](https://fass.nus.edu.sg/cnm/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2020/06/internship-guidelines-for-employers.pdf)). NTU recommends "$1250 per month" ([NTU](https://blogs.ntu.edu.sg/employers/?p=1408)). Polytechnic employer terms ask for a structured training plan and a company supervisor ([NP internship terms](https://www.np.edu.sg/docs/default-source/connect/industry-partners/internships-careers/internships/np-graded-internship-tncs-(company)_oct_2023.pdf)).
- This winter programme is **not** a for-credit school attachment. If an intern wants to count it towards their course, they must ask their school first, and the school's terms will then apply. ## 4. What interns may and may not do

The line comes from MAS: only "fit and proper and adequately qualified persons" may give financial advice, and MAS has banned unappointed people who did ([MAS enforcement, 2023](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/enforcement/enforcement-actions/2023/mas-bans-four-former-representatives-and-one-other-individual-for-unlawful-sub-agency-activities)). Interns are not appointed representatives.

| Interns MAY | Interns MAY NOT |
|---|---|
| Attend **internal** team meetings, trainings and huddles (with client names and details removed or the item skipped) | Attend any client meeting, call, video call or event with clients, in person or online |
| Attend the 6 workshops and do the activities | Give, or appear to give, financial advice or recommendations to anyone, including friends and family |
| Research public information (e.g. MoneySense, CPF Board, MAS websites) | Name, describe, compare, quote or explain any insurer's or firm's products or premiums |
| Draft general financial-literacy education content for the capstone | Publish anything (including on their own social media) about the internship's financial content without written approval |
| Ask advisers about their work, training and the rules they follow | Contact, collect, view or handle any client's or prospect's personal data |
| Help with internal admin that contains no personal data | Generate leads, collect contacts, run surveys of the public, or hand out business cards |
| Present their capstone internally | Be paid, rewarded or ranked on sales, leads, sign-ups or referrals |
| Choose (if 21+) to attend the optional career talk | Be asked to sit CMFAS exams, sign a representative contract or "commit" during the programme |

**If unsure, the intern stops and asks the supervisor.** Supervisors use the same rule: if it might look like advice or selling, an intern doesn't do it.

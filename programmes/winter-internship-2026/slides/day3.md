**Speaker notes:** Interns observe. Buddies sit next to them. If the huddle has no suitable internal content, swap in a product-neutral skills or compliance training session.

### Slide 3 — Activity: "What did you see?" debrief quiz (30 min, 11.00)
- Teams list 3 tasks they saw and sort each: **client work / learning / admin / compliance**
- Quick quiz (answers below)
- One question each for the talk at 11.30

**Speaker notes:** Quiz answers:
1. *Can an adviser call themselves a "Financial Adviser"?* **No.** Representatives may use the term only with "representative", e.g. "Financial Services Representative" (MAS FAQs on the FAA, Q10; fact pack section 5.5).
2. *Can a representative tied to one insurer call themselves "independent"?* **No.** "Independent" is only for firms with no product bias or links to product providers (fact pack section 5.5).
3. *How can a member of the public check a representative?* On the **MAS Register of Representatives**; each representative has a unique number (fact pack section 5.5).
4. *If a representative posts on social media, who is responsible for overseeing it?* **The firm (the insurer)**, under MAS digital advertising guidelines FSG-03, in force since 25 Mar 2026 (fact pack section 5.1).

## Session 3.2 — Talk: What an adviser actually does (11.30–12.30)

> Speaker: a supervisor. **No income figures, ranges or "up to" amounts; no lifestyle images; no "join us" message.** This is information for career decisions in general, not a recruitment talk (`09-career-talk-path.md` rules).

### Slide 1 — A week in the role (10 min)
- Meeting people to understand their situation (a "needs analysis")
- Admin, record-keeping and compliance checks
- Ongoing training and exams
- Reviewing existing plans when people's lives change

**Speaker notes:** Describe a realistic week, including the less glamorous parts. Don't describe sales targets or pay.

### Slide 2 — How someone becomes a representative (10 min)
- At least **21 years old** (MAS Notice FAA-N26)
- The required education, e.g. 'A' Levels with the required passes, a diploma or a degree
- Pass the required exams (for a typical life/health representative: RES5, M9, M9A, HI)
- Assessed as fit and proper, then appointed and listed on the MAS Register
- Continuing professional development every year

**Speaker notes:** Requirements as stated in the pack (`09-career-talk-path.md`, citing FAA-N26). Don't quote exam fees, pass rates or CPD hours on this slide: they're not in the fact pack. Say: "Nobody here is asking you to sit exams this week."

### Slide 3 — Supervision and rules (10 min)
- Representatives work under a firm that supervises them
- Advice must suit the person; content must be fair and balanced
- Each social post must stand on its own; a bio disclaimer doesn't fix a misleading post (FSG-03, fact pack section 5.1)
- Misconduct reporting rules tighten from 1 Jan 2027 (MAS Notice FAA-N27, fact pack section 5.8)

**Speaker notes:** Link the rules to the "may / may not" table: "This is why interns don't advise."

### Slide 4 — Other roles in finance (5 min)
- Operations, compliance, training, marketing, analytics
- Many finance jobs involve no advising at all
- The internship skills transfer: research, plain English, checking sources

**Speaker notes:** Present advising as one option among many.

### Slide 5 — Activity: "Ask the adviser" Q&A (25 min)
- Interns ask the questions they wrote at 11.00
- The speaker answers honestly, including "it depends" and the downsides
- Off-limits: income figures, any product, any client

**Speaker notes:** If asked "how much do advisers earn?", say: "We don't give income figures. Pay is mostly commission-based, varies and can be low or irregular at first. If you're ever considering it, ask for the written terms." (`09-career-talk-path.md`, "How pay works — without figures"). If asked "how do I join?", say the written handout on Friday explains what happens next, and nobody signs up this week.

## Session 3.3 — Case study: Needs, not products (1.30–3.00)

### Slide 1 — The rule for this session (5 min)
- Fictional persona only; no real client
- Describe **needs** in general terms
- Never name, compare or recommend a product, insurer or premium
- Questions a licensed adviser would ask, not answers they'd give

**Speaker notes:** Repeat the standard facilitator line from the slides README.

### Slide 2 — What a needs analysis is (10 min)
- A structured conversation about goals, income, spending, existing cover and risks
- It must be done by a licensed representative who knows the person's full situation
- Consumers can read how the process works: [MoneySense, financial advisory process](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/financial-advisory-process/)

**Speaker notes:** Interns learn the shape of the conversation, not how to do it.

### Slide 3 — Activity: Persona "Daniel and Mei" (50 min)
- **Daniel, 28, and Mei, 27, both SC, married last month, renting, both employed, no children, a parent who relies on Daniel a little**
- Groups list: (a) 5 life events or risks this couple faces; (b) which national schemes already apply; (c) 3 admin "to-dos" they could check for themselves; (d) 3 questions they could ask any licensed adviser
- No products, no amounts beyond facts from the fact pack

**Speaker notes:** Model answer (facts from the fact pack):

**(a) Life events and risks:** a large hospital bill; losing income through illness or job loss; caring for a parent; planning for a home; future children; one partner dying or losing mental capacity.

**(b) National schemes that already apply:**
- **CPF** for retirement, housing and healthcare; 37% total contribution for ages 55 and below on monthly wages above $750 in 2026 (section 1.1).
- **MediShield Life:** basic lifelong cover for large hospital bills, for all SC/PR automatically, sized for B2/C wards in public hospitals; it is designed to leave co-payments (deductible and co-insurance) (sections 2.1, 2.3). At ages 21–30 (age next birthday) the premium before subsidies is $295 a year, payable by MediSave (section 2.2).

**(c) Admin to-dos (strong answers spot these):**
- **CPF nomination:** marriage **revokes** an existing nomination, so it needs to be made again; it's free and can be done online with Singpass and two witnesses (section 1.6).
- **Will:** a will is revoked on marriage; it must be made at 21 or over, with two witnesses over 21 who aren't beneficiaries (section 3.2). Without a will, a non-Muslim spouse with no children shares the estate with parents, half each (section 3.2).
- **LPA:** anyone 21 or over can make one; Form 1 is free for Singapore Citizens, but the Certificate Issuer fee is separate (section 3.1). Without one, family must apply to court for a Deputy.

**(d) Questions for any licensed adviser:** "What does my existing cover, including MediShield Life, already do?" "What are the exclusions and waiting periods?" "What happens if I can't keep paying?" "Are you tied to one insurer?" "Can I see your number on the MAS Register?"

**Red flags to correct during the activity:** any group writing a product type with an amount ("get $X of cover"), an insurer name, "the best plan", or "they should buy". Rewrite it as a question.

### Slide 4 — Debrief (25 min)
- Which to-dos surprised you? (Most groups don't know that marriage revokes a will and a CPF nomination)
- Where did you want to give an answer, and why couldn't you?
- What makes a good question for an adviser?

**Speaker notes:** Close with: "Spotting needs and asking good questions is something any consumer can do. Recommending a solution needs a licence."

## Session 3.4 — Marketing plan as education content (3.15–5.00)

> The principal asks for a marketing plan (submission Day 4, 12pm). In this agency, the plan is a **general financial-literacy education campaign** for students and first-jobbers: no products, insurer names, prices, income claims, contact details or calls to "speak to an adviser". Nothing is published (`01-week-by-week-plan.md`, capstone rules). **[JASON: confirm with the principal that an education-content plan meets its brief.]**

### Slide 1 — What your plan is (and isn't) (10 min)
- **Is:** an education campaign on one topic (budgeting, scams, CPF basics, emergency funds, fair money content)
- **Is:** aimed at students or first-jobbers, in a format like 3–5 carousels, a one-page guide or a short talk script
- **Isn't:** a plan to find clients, generate leads or promote any product
- Nothing is published this week

**Speaker notes:** If an intern's draft drifts towards "how to get clients", redirect to "how to help someone understand X".

### Slide 2 — Plan structure (10 min)
- Audience and the one thing they should learn
- Key messages (3 max), each with an official source and date checked
- Format and channel (as a proposal only)
- How you'd know it helped (e.g. a 3-question quiz for readers)
- Compliance self-check (tomorrow's Workshop 6 checklist)

**Speaker notes:** "Channel" means where it could appear **if** it were ever approved; it is not a posting plan.

### Slide 3 — Activity: "Source or scrap" quiz (15 min)
- For each claim, decide: **keep (sourced)**, **fix**, or **scrap**

**Speaker notes:** Answers:

| Claim | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "MediShield Life covers your whole hospital bill." | Scrap | False: designed to leave co-payments (fact pack 2.1, 2.3) |
| "CPF contributions are capped at monthly Ordinary Wages of $8,000 (2026 and 2027, CPF Board)." | Keep | Matches fact pack 1.2, dated and sourced |
| "Everyone's CPF contributions go up in 2027." | Fix | Only the above-55 to 65 bands change (fact pack 1.1) |
| "Just put your CPF in your will." | Scrap | CPF savings can't be left by will; you need a nomination (fact pack 1.6) |
| "An LPA is completely free." | Fix | Form 1 is free for citizens; the certification fee is separate (fact pack 3.1) |
| "This plan gives guaranteed returns." | Scrap | No "guaranteed" unless it really is, and no products in any case (fact pack 5.2) |

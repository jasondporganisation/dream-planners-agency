## Workshop 1 — Budgeting and your first paycheque (9.45–12.15)

### Slide 1 — Where does my money go? (5 min, 9.45)
- Today: payslips, budgets, "pay yourself first"
- All examples are fictional; your own numbers stay private
- By 12.15 you'll have built a one-month budget for a made-up persona

**Speaker notes:** Set the privacy rule first. If someone volunteers their own pay, thank them and move on without writing it down.

### Slide 2 — Gross pay, deductions, take-home pay (10 min)
- **Gross pay:** what the employer agrees to pay
- **Deductions:** mainly the employee's CPF share
- **Take-home pay:** what lands in your bank account
- Fictional payslip: gross $3,500 → employee CPF 20% ($700) → take-home $2,800

**Speaker notes:** The 20% employee rate is for ages 55 and below, monthly wages above $750, in 2026 (CPF Board, via the fact pack section 1.1). Say: "The $3,500 is made up for the exercise, not a salary guide." Don't discuss what any job pays.

### Slide 3 — CPF in one line (5 min)
- Employer adds 17% on top, so 37% in total goes into your CPF (ages 55 and below, 2026)
- CPF is for retirement, housing and healthcare
- Contributions are capped at monthly Ordinary Wages of $8,000 (2026 and 2027)

**Speaker notes:** Fact pack sections 1.1 and 1.2. Workshop 3 covers CPF in depth; if it isn't run this week, point to cpf.gov.sg. Don't project balances.

### Slide 4 — Needs, wants, savings (10 min)
- **Needs:** transport, phone, food, contributions at home
- **Wants:** eating out, travel, hobbies
- **Savings:** buffer first, then goals
- One simple split is a starting point; there's no single "right" ratio

**Speaker notes:** Avoid presenting any ratio as a rule. Ask: "What's a need for one person that's a want for another?" (e.g. a car).

### Slide 5 — Fixed, variable and irregular costs (8 min)
- **Fixed:** same each month (phone plan, subscriptions)
- **Variable:** changes (food, transport)
- **Irregular:** annual fees, gifts, festive seasons, birthdays
- Divide irregular costs by 12 and set that aside monthly

**Speaker notes:** December is a good example: festive spending and year-end gifts.

### Slide 6 — Pay yourself first, and track (10 min)
- Move savings out on payday, not at month end
- Track with a notebook, spreadsheet or bank app: pick one you'll actually use
- Watch for lifestyle creep when pay goes up
- Common mistakes: no buffer, forgotten subscriptions

**Speaker notes:** Don't name or recommend any bank, app or account. "A tool you'll actually use beats the perfect tool."

### Slide 7 — Learn more (2 min)
- MoneySense: [Managing your money](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/managing-your-money/)
- CPF Board: [cpf.gov.sg](https://www.cpf.gov.sg/member)

**Speaker notes:** Official sources only. Move to the activity.

### Slide 8 — Activity: "Persona Budget" case study (45 min, about 10.40)
- Pairs, one persona card: **Aisha, 23, first job, lives with parents, wants to travel in a year**
- Fictional take-home pay: $2,800 a month
- Build a one-month budget in the template; **then** add the surprise card and rebalance
- Surprise card: "Phone screen cracked: $250 repair this month"

**Speaker notes:** Model answer (one of many; reward reasoning, not matching numbers):

| Line | Before surprise | After surprise |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution at home | $400 | $400 |
| Transport | $150 | $150 |
| Phone plan | $30 | $30 |
| Food and drinks | $450 | $400 |
| Subscriptions | $30 | $15 (pause one) |
| Fun, eating out | $300 | $165 |
| Irregular-costs pot (gifts, fees ÷ 12) | $90 | $90 |
| Emergency buffer | $650 | $650 |
| Travel goal | $700 | $650 |
| Phone repair | — | $250 |
| **Total** | **$2,800** | **$2,800** |

Good answers: keep the buffer contribution going, or use the buffer for the repair and say how it will be refilled; cut wants before needs; trim the goal a little rather than stopping it. Flag answers that leave no buffer or that add borrowing.

### Slide 9 — Debrief (20 min, about 11.25)
- What did you cut first, and why?
- Did you use the buffer or cut spending? Both can be right
- What would change if Aisha moved out?

**Speaker notes:** Two or three pairs share. Link back to "pay yourself first".

### Break (10 min) and recap (10 min, to 12.15)
- Recap slide: take-home pay; needs, wants, savings; irregular costs; pay yourself first
- Takeaway: one-page budget template (blank)

**Speaker notes:** Hand out the blank template to use privately at home, or not at all.

## Workshop 5 — Scams, fraud and checking who you're dealing with (1.30–4.00)

### Slide 1 — Too good to be true (5 min, 1.30)
- Today: how scams work, how to check, what to do
- Scammers target students too: jobs, "easy money", investments
- By 4pm you'll have sorted 12 real-looking (fictional) messages

**Speaker notes:** No scam-loss statistics: none are in the fact pack. Keep it practical.

### Slide 2 — How scams work (8 min)
- **Urgency:** "act now", "your account will be locked"
- **Authority:** pretending to be a bank, government agency or police
- **Secrecy:** "don't tell anyone"
- **Emotion:** fear, greed, love, guilt

**Speaker notes:** Ask for examples interns or their families have seen, without names.

### Slide 3 — Common types (8 min)
- Impersonation (bank, government, courier)
- Investment ("guaranteed high returns")
- Job scams and money-mule requests aimed at students
- Phishing links; romance scams

**Speaker notes:** Stress money mules: "Letting someone use your bank account for a fee can be a crime." Point to official sources, not personal stories.

### Slide 4 — Red flags in financial offers (8 min)
- "Guaranteed" high returns or "can't lose"
- Pressure to decide now; "spots are limited"
- Seller not on the MAS Register, or a firm on the Investor Alert List
- Payment to a personal account

**Speaker notes:** Link to Workshop 6: "guaranteed" is a red flag in ads too (fact pack section 5.2).

### Slide 5 — Check the person, check the firm (10 min)
- Person: [MAS Register of Representatives](https://eservices.mas.gov.sg/rr); [MoneySense: who you could be dealing with](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/financial-advice-who-you-could-be-dealing-with/)
- Firm: [MAS Investor Alert List](https://www.mas.gov.sg/investor-alert-list)
- Every representative has a unique MAS representative number (fact pack section 5.5)

**Speaker notes:** Demo a search on the Register using a buddy's own name **with their consent**. Don't search for a client or a member of the public.

### Slide 6 — Tools and what to do if it happens (10 min)
- [ScamShield](https://www.scamshield.gov.sg/) app and helpline
- Bank features that lock money: [MoneySense on MoneyLock](https://www.moneysense.gov.sg/scams/moneylock/)
- **Stop** → call your bank on the number on its official site or card → **report**
- Talk to parents and grandparents: agree a family "check first" rule

**Speaker notes:** Don't name or recommend any specific bank.

### Slide 7 — Activity: "Spot the Scam" quiz (45 min, about 2.20)
- Groups get 12 fictional messages
- Sort each into **Scam**, **Legit** or **Can't tell yet**
- For each "can't tell yet", write the check you'd run

**Speaker notes:** Answer key:

| # | Message (fictional) | Answer | Key clue / check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | SMS: "Your bank account is suspended. Click bit.ly/xx to verify." | Scam | Urgency + link; banks don't ask you to log in via SMS links |
| 2 | DM from "Financial Adviser Ken": "Guaranteed 15% a month, slots closing tonight." | Scam | Guaranteed return + pressure; check the Register and the Alert List |
| 3 | Job ad: "Earn $500/day liking videos, pay $50 to start." | Scam | Pay-to-work; too good to be true |
| 4 | Call: "This is the police. Transfer your savings to a safe account." | Scam | Authority + secrecy; officials don't ask for transfers |
| 5 | Email from your university career office about a careers fair, link to the university's own domain | Legit (likely) | Check the domain; go to the site yourself rather than clicking |
| 6 | "Hi Mum, new number, phone broken, need $300 now" | Scam | Impersonation; call the old number |
| 7 | Telegram: "Use your bank account to receive payments, keep 10%." | Scam | Money-mule request; can be a crime |
| 8 | Courier SMS: "Parcel held, pay $1.99 customs fee" with a link | Can't tell yet → likely scam | Check the courier's official app/site directly |
| 9 | Your telco app notification about your monthly bill, inside the official app | Legit | Inside the official app, no link |
| 10 | Instagram ad: celebrity "reveals" crypto platform | Scam | Fake endorsement; check the Alert List |
| 11 | Person says they're a representative and gives a name and rep number | Can't tell yet | Look up the name and number on the MAS Register |
| 12 | Landlord abroad asks for a deposit before a viewing | Scam | Payment before seeing; no in-person check |

Score 1 point for a correct sort and 1 for a correct check. Watch #11: the right answer is "check", not "legit".

### Slide 8 — Debrief (20 min, about 3.05)
- Which message nearly fooled your group?
- What's the one check you'd teach a grandparent?
- Which clues appeared most often?

**Speaker notes:** Collect "one check" answers on a flipchart.

### Break (10 min) and recap (15 min, to 4.00)
- Recap: urgency, authority, secrecy, emotion; check the person and the firm; stop, call, report
- Remind interns: if anyone contacts them during the week offering money-related "opportunities", tell the welfare contact

## Session 4.1 — Workshop 6: Would I be comfortable if a regulator read this? (9.30–11.10)

### Slide 1 — Title and today's goal (3 min, 9.30)
- "Would I be comfortable if a regulator read this?"
- Today: why money content is regulated, and a checklist you'll use on your own plan
- Submission is at 12pm; you'll leave this room with a checked draft

### Slide 2 — Why rules exist (7 min)
- People make big decisions from what they read and watch
- Advertising by financial advisers must be fair and balanced and not misleading (MAS FAQs on Fair and Balanced Advertising, fact pack 5.2)
- Advertising must be clear, easy to understand and not misleading (LIA Code, fact pack 5.3)

**Speaker notes:** Keep to the principle. Statute wording is marked UNVERIFIED in the fact pack, so don't quote section text.

### Slide 3 — Digital advertising guidelines (FSG-03) (10 min)
- In force since **25 Mar 2026**
- The firm must oversee its representatives' posts
- **Each post must stand on its own:** a disclaimer in the bio or a link doesn't fix a misleading post
- Key risk disclosures must be clear and not cut off; on reels they must stay on screen long enough to read
- Re-sharing content (including testimonials) counts as advertising

**Speaker notes:** Fact pack section 5.1. Say: "These rules apply to licensed representatives. As interns you go further: no products at all."

### Slide 4 — Fair and balanced, and titles (10 min)
- No "guaranteed" unless it really is; risks as clear as benefits
- Past or illustrative returns need a "not indicative of future performance" statement (you won't use any)
- Representatives can't call themselves "Financial Advisers" or "independent" if tied to an insurer (fact pack 5.5)
- Estimates stay estimates: e.g. CPF LIFE payouts are CPF's estimates, never "guaranteed" (fact pack 1.4)

### Slide 5 — Sourcing and plain English (10 min)
- Official site, link and "checked on" date for every fact
- Government figures change every January; date-stamp them ("for 2026")
- Short sentences, define jargon, one idea per slide or frame
- Inclusive: no stereotypes about age, gender, race or income

**Speaker notes:** Example: CPF rates for ages above 55 to 65 change on 1 Jan 2027 (fact pack, "Changes on 1 Jan 2027"). A post that says "CPF rates are…" with no date goes stale.

### Slide 6 — The capstone checklist (5 min)
- [ ] Every fact has an official source link and a "checked on" date
- [ ] No products, insurers, firms, premiums or returns
- [ ] No "guaranteed", "best", "risk-free", "you will", income or earnings claims
- [ ] No "contact an adviser", phone numbers, sign-up links or calls to action other than "learn more at [official site]"
- [ ] Balanced: says "it depends" or names limits where true
- [ ] Clear to an 18-year-old reading on a phone
- [ ] No real people's personal data, photos or stories without consent
- [ ] Inclusive and respectful

**Speaker notes:** Hand out printed copies (from `02-workshops.md`).

### Slide 8 — Activity: "Red Pen" (40 min, about 10.20)
- Groups get 4 fictional posts with deliberate problems
- Mark each problem against the checklist; rewrite one post
- Last 10 minutes: run the checklist on your **own** plan

**Speaker notes:** The 4 posts and answer key:

| Post (fictional) | Problems | Model rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| A. "MediShield Life covers everything — you'll never pay a cent in hospital! 🏥" | False claim ("never pay"); no source or date | "MediShield Life is basic lifelong cover for large hospital bills, sized for B2/C wards. You still pay a deductible and co-insurance. Learn more at moh.gov.sg (checked 3 Nov 2026)." |
| B. "I made $10k last month helping friends with their finances. DM me to find out how!" | Income boast; recruitment call to action; implies advising | Scrap. No income claims, no "DM me" |
| C. "Save on your hospital bills with Plan X: best value in Singapore, guaranteed." | Product plug; "best"; "guaranteed"; no source | Scrap the product. Education version: "Read the key words in any hospital plan: deductible, co-insurance, exclusions. MoneySense explains them." |
| D. "The CPF wage ceiling is going up again next year, so check your payslip." | Wrong (OW ceiling stays at $8,000 in 2026 and 2027); no date or source | "CPF contributions are capped at monthly Ordinary Wages of $8,000 in 2026 and 2027 (CPF Board, checked 3 Nov 2026)." |

## Session 4.2 — Self-check and buddy review (11.10–11.40)

### Slide 1 — Buddy review (30 min)
- Swap plans with another team first: 10 min checking each fact has a source and date
- Then your buddy goes through the checklist with you: 15 min
- Last 5 min: list the fixes you'll make before 12pm

**Speaker notes:** Buddies must not add product ideas or "how to get clients" suggestions. If a plan relies on a figure that isn't in the fact pack or an official page, it comes out.

### Slide 2 — Quiz: "Last check" (in pairs, during the review)
1. *Your plan says "CPF LIFE pays you $1,780 a month."* → **Fix:** it's CPF's estimate, for someone turning 55 in 2026 with the FRS on the Standard Plan, from 65 (fact pack 1.4). Better still, leave it out: it isn't relevant to first-jobbers.
2. *Your plan ends "Talk to a financial adviser today!"* → **Fix:** use "Learn more at [official site]" instead.
3. *Your plan quotes a scam-loss figure from a news article.* → **Scrap:** not in the fact pack, not an official source.
4. *Your plan uses a photo of a real friend.* → **Fix:** only with their consent, or use none.

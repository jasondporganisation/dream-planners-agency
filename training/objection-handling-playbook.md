# Objection Handling Playbook for New Representatives

**For:** Dream Planners new representatives (January 2027 intake onwards). Winter interns may read it for learning only. They must **not** use it with real clients.
**Builds on:** [Module C: The 10 Most Common Objections](new-adviser-90-days/module-c-common-objections.md) and the LACE method (Listen, Acknowledge, Clarify, Explain or Exit).
**Figures:** Taken only from VERIFIED items in the [Singapore Fact Pack, Nov 2026](../compliance/sg-fact-pack-2026-11.md) (checked 3 Nov 2026). Source tags such as [MAS-1] point to [`new-adviser-90-days/sources.md`](new-adviser-90-days/sources.md). **Open the live official page before quoting any figure to a client.** Several figures change on 1 Jan 2027 and 1 Apr 2027.
**Status:** DRAFT for review by the Sales Director (Spectre), People Director (Heron) and compliance (Brand Guardian). Not for use until a person signs it off. Your principal's compliance manual, approved scripts and forms always come first. Where they are stricter, follow them.

> **The point of this playbook:** An objection is the client telling you something important. We don't "overcome" objections. We **listen, find the real concern, answer honestly with sources, and respect the decision**, including "no". A client who understands their choice and says no is a good outcome. A client who says yes under pressure is not.

## 1. Ground rules (read first)

These come from the MAS Fair Dealing Guidelines [MAS-1], the Financial Advisers Act framework and notices [MAS-2] [MAS-4], MoneySense guidance on what clients should expect [MS-1], and the LIA Code of Life Insurance Practice [LIA-1].

1. **Be on the register before you advise.** Until your name appears on the MAS Register of Representatives, you role-play only. [MAS-5]
2. **Needs first, product last.** No recommendation without a completed fact-find and a reasonable basis that you document. [MAS-2] [MS-1]
3. **No guarantees** unless the benefit really is guaranteed in the policy documents. Illustrations are projections. Non-guaranteed means non-guaranteed. [MS-1]
4. **No product names and no insurer names** in this playbook or in role-plays. In real meetings, use only the principal's approved material.
5. **Never disparage** another adviser, insurer, agency or channel (buying direct, a robo-adviser, a friend who is an agent). Compare facts and needs, never people.
6. **You are not "independent".** A tied representative may not call themselves independent or claim to compare the whole market. [Fact pack 5.5]
7. **No twisting.** Never suggest a client cancel, surrender or replace an existing policy to buy a new one. If the client raises switching, a full written comparison of costs, lost benefits and disadvantages is required, and your coach reviews it before any recommendation. [MAS-2] [MS-1]
8. **No pressure.** No false deadlines, no "everyone is buying this", no fear tactics. Clients are never obliged to buy on the spot. [MS-1]
9. **Never talk about your commission or targets** as a reason to buy. If a client asks how you are paid, answer honestly and as your principal's disclosure rules require.
10. **Write it down.** If it isn't documented, it didn't happen. See [Section 6](#6-documenting-the-conversation).

## 2. How to use each card

Every objection card has five parts:

| Part | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **What they're really worried about** | The concern underneath the words. Check your guess with a question; never assume. |
| **Response A / Response B** | Two compliant ways in. Both start by **listening**. Pick the one that fits the client. Don't recite word for word. |
| **Never say** | Lines that break Fair Dealing, FAA or LIA rules, or simply break trust. They are patterns: avoid anything that makes the same kind of claim in other words. |
| **Role-play line** | What the "client" says in practice to start the round. |

**First move for every objection (LACE):**
- **Listen:** let them finish. Count two seconds before you reply.
- **Acknowledge:** "That makes sense" / "Thank you for telling me."
- **Clarify:** "Can I ask what's behind that?"
- **Explain** honestly with a source, **or Exit** warmly.

## 3. First-meeting objections (1–21)

### 1. "I already have insurance through work."

- **Really worried about:** Paying twice for the same cover. Or they don't actually know what the work plan covers.
- **Response A (understand first):** "That's a great benefit to have. Do you know roughly what it covers, for example hospital, life or critical illness, and for how much? If you're comfortable sharing the benefits summary from HR, we can look at it together against what you told me matters to you."
- **Response B (portability, as a question):** "One thing worth checking with HR is what happens to that cover if you change jobs, take a break or retire. Some people are happy with that; others want cover that doesn't depend on their employer. Which matters more to you?"
- **Never say:** "Company insurance is useless." / "Your employer plan will definitely end when you leave." (You haven't read their policy terms.)
- **Role-play line:** "My company already covers me, so I think I'm fine."

### 2. "I'll think about it."

- **Really worried about:** Not understanding something, cost, not trusting you yet, or a polite no.
- **Response A:** "Of course. This is an important decision and you shouldn't feel rushed. Is there a part that isn't clear yet? I'd rather explain it now than leave you with questions."
- **Response B:** "Take all the time you need. I'll leave the documents with you. Would it help if I called on [day] to answer questions, or would you prefer to contact me when you're ready?" (Then respect their answer.)
- **Never say:** "What is there to think about?" / "This price is only valid today." / "If something happens to you tonight, you'll regret it."
- **Role-play line:** "Let me think about it and get back to you."

### 3. "It's too expensive."

- **Really worried about:** Cash flow, or not yet seeing the value.
- **Response A (budget first):** "Thank you for being upfront. What monthly amount would you be comfortable keeping up for the long term, even in a tight year? A plan you can't sustain doesn't help you."
- **Response B (prioritise):** "Let's go back to your priorities. If we had to pick just one need to cover first, which would it be? We can start smaller, or simply wait."
- **Never say:** "It's only the price of a cup of coffee a day." / "You can't afford not to." / "Just stretch a bit; your salary will go up."
- **Role-play line:** "Wah, this is too much for me lah."

### 4. "My friend / relative is an agent."

- **Really worried about:** Loyalty, awkwardness, or feeling pressured by two people.
- **Response A:** "That's good. Someone who knows you well can look after you. I'm not here to replace them."
- **Response B:** "If it helps, I can be a sounding board on general questions, like how MediShield Life works, with no obligation. Or I can step back completely. What would you prefer?"
- **Never say:** "Your friend might not be very experienced." / "I can do a better job than them." / "Show me what they sold you and I'll tell you what's wrong with it."
- **Role-play line:** "My cousin is an agent, so I'll probably just buy from her."

### 5. "Insurance is a scam."

- **Really worried about:** Claims being rejected, hidden terms, or a bad story they've heard.
- **Response A (listen):** "That's a strong feeling, and I'd like to understand it. Did something happen to you or someone you know?"
- **Response B (transparency):** "You shouldn't buy anything you don't trust. Whatever we look at, I'll walk you through the exclusions and what isn't covered, not just the benefits. And you can check my registration on the MAS register." [MAS-5] [MS-2]
- **Never say:** "Our claims are never rejected." / "That was a different company; we're not like them." / "You're wrong."
- **Role-play line:** "Insurance is all a scam. They never pay when you claim."

### 6. "Buy term and invest the rest."

- **Really worried about:** Fees, value for money, or keeping control of their money.
- **Response A (agree on principles):** "That's a well-known approach and it suits many people. It works best for people who actually invest the difference consistently, for the long term. How do you see yourself doing that?"
- **Response B (needs, not ideology):** "Let's set aside product types for a moment. What do you need protected, for how long, and what are your other goals? Then we can see which structure fits, and that may well be term cover."
- **Never say:** "Term insurance is a waste of money." / "Investing yourself is too risky; let us handle it." / "Whole life gives you guaranteed returns." (Non-guaranteed bonuses are not guaranteed.)
- **Role-play line:** "I read online you should just buy term and invest the rest."

### 7. "I'm young and healthy."

- **Really worried about:** Spending on something they don't think they'll need.
- **Response A:** "That's a great position to be in. Can I ask who would be affected financially if you couldn't work for a while: parents, a partner, loans?"
- **Response B:** "If nothing is needed right now, that's a fine answer. Shall we note a review date for when things change, like marriage, a home or kids?"
- **Never say:** "Premiums only go up, so lock in now before it's too late." (Fear-based urgency.) / "You never know when you'll get cancer."
- **Role-play line:** "I'm 25 and I've never been sick. Why do I need this?"

### 8. "Let me ask my spouse."

- **Really worried about:** Making a family decision alone. This is healthy.
- **Response A:** "That's very sensible. This affects both of you. Would it help to meet together, so your spouse can ask questions directly?"
- **Response B:** "Of course. I'll leave the summary and the documents so you can go through them together. No rush at all."
- **Never say:** "You're the one paying, so it's your decision." / "Your spouse doesn't need to know."
- **Role-play line:** "I need to discuss this with my wife / husband first."

### 9. "CPF / MediShield Life is enough."

- **Really worried about:** Paying for duplicate cover.
- **Response A (respect the foundation):** "MediShield Life is an important foundation, and everyone who is a Singapore Citizen or PR has it automatically, for life. Shall I show you exactly how it works on the official page, so you can decide for yourself?" [MOH-1]
- **Response B (facts, not fear):** "MediShield Life is sized for subsidised treatment in public hospitals, Class B2 and C wards. It has a deductible and co-insurance, so there's a co-payment by design. If you're comfortable with that, it may be enough. If you'd want a higher ward class or a private hospital, that's where other options come in." [Fact pack 2.1, 2.3] [MOH-3]
- **Never say:** "MediShield Life is useless." / "MediShield Life covers your whole bill." / Any 2027 deductible amount (not yet published).
- **Role-play line:** "I've got MediShield Life and CPF. That's enough, right?"

### 10. "Can you send me the details first?"

- **Really worried about:** Time, feeling cornered, or a polite exit.
- **Response A:** "Happy to. So I send what's actually relevant, can I ask two quick questions about what you're hoping to sort out?"
- **Response B:** "Sure. I'll send general information only (we can't recommend anything until I understand your situation). Would you like a short call afterwards to go through questions?"
- **Never say:** "I can't send anything unless we meet." / (Sending a product brochure or quote **before** any fact-find, or anything not approved by the principal.)
- **Role-play line:** "Just WhatsApp me the brochure first."

### 11. "I don't have time."

- **Really worried about:** A long sales pitch.
- **Response A:** "Totally understand. Would 20 minutes at a time and place that suits you work better? If not, no problem."
- **Response B:** "No worries at all. Shall I check back in a few months, or would you rather reach out when it suits you?"
- **Never say:** "It'll only take five minutes" (if it won't). / "You'll make time when you're in hospital."
- **Role-play line:** "Sorry, very busy lately. Maybe another time."

### 12. "You just want my money / your commission."

- **Really worried about:** Conflict of interest.
- **Response A (honesty):** "That's a fair question. Yes, representatives are paid when a policy is bought. That's exactly why you should expect me to explain my reasons in writing and show you the costs and disadvantages, not just the benefits." [MS-1]
- **Response B (control):** "You're in control here. You can check my registration on the MAS register, take the documents home, and decide in your own time, including deciding not to buy." [MAS-5]
- **Never say:** "I don't earn anything from this." (Untrue.) / "I'm independent, so I have no bias." (A tied rep may not claim this.) [Fact pack 5.5]
- **Role-play line:** "You agents only care about your commission."

### 13. "I already have enough insurance."

- **Really worried about:** Being upsold.
- **Response A:** "That's good to hear. Would a review against your current goals be useful? If what you have already covers them, I'll tell you so, and there's nothing to add." [MS-1]
- **Response B:** "Fair enough. A lot of people find it helpful to list what they have in one place, including nominations. Would that be useful, with no recommendation unless there's a real gap?"
- **Never say:** "Nobody ever has enough insurance." / "Your old policy is outdated."
- **Role-play line:** "I already bought a few policies years ago. I'm covered."

### 14. "I'll buy online / direct myself."

- **Really worried about:** Cost, or wanting control.
- **Response A:** "That's a valid choice. MoneySense has a guide on buying life insurance online; I'm happy to send you the link." [MS-3]
- **Response B:** "If you ever want someone to help you think through your needs first, or to help at claim time, I'm here."
- **Never say:** "Buying online is dangerous." / "You'll get no help when you claim."
- **Role-play line:** "I can just buy it online myself, cheaper right?"

### 15. "Can you guarantee the returns?"

- **Really worried about:** Losing money.
- **Response A:** "No, and I'd be wrong to. The documents show which parts are guaranteed and which are not. Let me take you through both columns." [MS-1]
- **Response B:** "Projected figures are illustrations based on assumptions, not promises. For investment-linked policies, the value can go down as well as up." [MS-2] [Fact pack 5.2, 5.8]
- **Never say:** "Guaranteed returns." / "You can't lose money." / "Expected yield of X%." / "Historically it always goes up."
- **Role-play line:** "So how much will I confirm get back?"

### 16. "I'm not ready / maybe next year."

- **Really worried about:** Timing, other priorities.
- **Response A:** "That's completely fine. What would need to happen for it to feel like the right time?"
- **Response B:** "Shall I note down to check in at a time you choose? Or you can contact me whenever you like."
- **Never say:** "Next year your premium will be much higher." (Fear-based urgency.) / "You might not be insurable next year."
- **Role-play line:** "Maybe after my bonus next year."

### 17. "My parents already bought insurance for me."

- **Really worried about:** Not knowing what they have; not wanting to upset parents.
- **Response A:** "That's thoughtful of them. Do you know what the policies cover and who owns them, you or your parents?"
- **Response B:** "A good first step could be simply finding out what you have. If it already meets your needs, that's great."
- **Never say:** "Old policies your parents bought are probably bad." / "You should cancel those and start fresh."
- **Role-play line:** "My mum bought a policy for me when I was a baby."

### 18. "I had a bad experience before (claim rejected / pushy agent)."

- **Really worried about:** Being hurt again.
- **Response A:** "I'm sorry that happened. Would you like to tell me about it? It helps me understand what matters to you."
- **Response B:** "If a dispute is still open, you have proper channels: the insurer's complaints process, and then the Financial Industry Disputes Resolution Centre. I can help you find the right contact." See [fidrec.com.sg](https://www.fidrec.com.sg). Do not give legal advice.
- **Never say:** "That agent was terrible." / "That company is known for rejecting claims." / "I'll make sure your claims are approved."
- **Role-play line:** "My last agent disappeared after I signed."

### 19. "I'm single, I don't have dependants."

- **Really worried about:** Paying for others' benefit.
- **Response A:** "That's a fair point. Death cover may matter less for you right now. What would happen to your own finances if you couldn't work for six months?"
- **Response B:** "Some people in your position focus on hospital costs and income, not death cover. Let's look at your situation before deciding anything."
- **Never say:** "Everyone needs life insurance." / "Who will pay for your funeral?"
- **Role-play line:** "No wife, no kids. Why do I need insurance?"

### 20. "I have a health condition; nobody will cover me."

- **Really worried about:** Rejection, embarrassment.
- **Response A (factual reassurance):** "Thank you for trusting me with that. MediShield Life covers all Singapore Citizens and PRs for life, including pre-existing conditions. For serious pre-existing conditions, MediShield Life charges 30% additional premiums for the first 10 years." [Fact pack 2.1] [MOH-1]
- **Response B (honest about private cover):** "For private plans, the insurer decides after underwriting. I can't promise an outcome. What I can do is make sure the application is complete and honest; you must disclose everything asked, because non-disclosure can affect claims later."
- **Never say:** "Don't mention it on the form." / "You'll definitely be accepted." / "They'll never find out."
- **Role-play line:** "I have diabetes, so insurance won't take me, right?"

### 21. "You're new. How do I know you'll still be around?"

(Listed here because new reps hear it often in first meetings.)

- **Really worried about:** Being left without service.
- **Response A:** "That's a fair concern. I'm supported by a team leader and our team, and the policy is with the insurer, not with me personally. If I'm ever unavailable, you can always contact the insurer directly, and it will arrange servicing under its own procedures." (Check your principal's actual orphan-policy servicing practice before saying more.)
- **Response B:** "You can check my registration on the MAS register. I'd also like to agree a review date with you now, so you can see whether I keep my word." [MAS-5]
- **Never say:** "I'll be in this job forever." / "Older agents are out of touch."
- **Role-play line:** "You just joined, right? Will you still be doing this next year?"

> Numbering note: objections 1–21 are first-meeting objections; objections 22–30 below are mostly raised in reviews. Total: 30.

## 4. Review-meeting objections (22–30)

### 22. "I want to compare with other insurers."

- **Really worried about:** Getting a fair deal.
- **Response A:** "That's sensible, and you're welcome to. To be clear, I represent one insurer, so I can't compare across the whole market for you." [Fact pack 5.5]
- **Response B:** "When you compare, look at the same benefits, exclusions, waiting periods and premium terms side by side. I'm happy to explain any terms in our documents."
- **Never say:** "Other insurers are worse." / "I'll compare all the insurers for you." / "We're the cheapest."
- **Role-play line:** "Let me check what other companies offer first."

### 23. "Another adviser told me to switch my policy. Should I?"

- **Really worried about:** Doing the wrong thing; being caught between advisers.
- **Response A (slow down):** "That's a big decision, so it's good you're asking. Switching can mean new waiting periods, losing cover for conditions you've developed since, and surrender costs. Before any decision, ask for a written comparison of what you'd gain and lose." [MS-1] [MAS-2]
- **Response B (stay neutral):** "I won't comment on the other adviser. What I can do is go through what your current policy does for you now, so you have the facts."
- **Never say:** "They're just trying to churn you." / "Switch to me instead." / "Your current policy is rubbish."
- **Role-play line:** "Another agent said I should cancel my old plan and buy a new one."

### 24. "I want to cancel / surrender my policy to free up cash."

- **Really worried about:** A cash-flow problem right now.
- **Response A (understand):** "I'm sorry things are tight. Can you tell me a bit about what's happening? I want to make sure we look at all your options."
- **Response B (facts):** "It's your decision. Before you decide, let's check what you'd lose: the cover, any surrender value compared with premiums paid, and whether you could get similar cover again at your age and health. Ask the insurer what options the policy itself offers. Then you decide."
- **Never say:** "You can't cancel." / "Cancel that one and I'll get you a cheaper new one." (Twisting.) / Pressuring them to keep it for your own persistency numbers.
- **Role-play line:** "I lost my job. I want to cash out my policy."

### 25. "My hospital plan premium went up. I want to drop it or the rider."

- **Really worried about:** Rising costs, especially as they get older.
- **Response A (options, not fear):** "That's a real concern. Let's look at the options together, like a lower ward class or keeping the plan without the rider, and what each means for your out-of-pocket costs."
- **Response B (facts):** "A few facts to weigh: you can only hold one Integrated Shield Plan at a time. MediSave can pay for the private part only up to the Additional Withdrawal Limits ($300 / $600 / $900 a year, by age next birthday). Rider premiums must be paid fully in cash. And MediShield Life stays with you whatever you choose." [Fact pack 2.5] [MOH-5]
- **Never say:** "Never downgrade; you'll regret it." / "With a rider you'll never pay a cent." / "Pay your whole plan with MediSave."
- **Role-play line:** "My hospital plan premium jumped again. Should I just cancel the rider?"

### 26. "I don't need a review. Nothing has changed."

- **Really worried about:** A disguised sales meeting.
- **Response A:** "Totally fine. A review is just to check your cover still matches your life. Has anything changed, like a new job, home, marriage or child?"
- **Response B:** "One thing worth checking even if nothing has changed: nominations. For example, getting married revokes an existing CPF nomination. Would a 10-minute check be useful?" [Fact pack 1.6]
- **Never say:** "Reviews are compulsory." / "Your policy might be invalid if we don't review."
- **Role-play line:** "No need to review lah, everything's the same."

### 27. "My investment-linked policy is down. I want out."

- **Really worried about:** Losing more money.
- **Response A (listen, no promises):** "It's stressful seeing the value fall. What were you originally hoping this policy would do for you, and has that goal changed?"
- **Response B (facts):** "The value can go down as well as up, and I can't predict when or whether it will recover. Before deciding, let's look at the charges, any surrender value, the cover you'd lose, and your time horizon. Then you decide." [Fact pack 5.2]
- **Never say:** "It will definitely bounce back." / "Just hold, it always recovers." / "Switch into this other fund and you'll recover your losses."
- **Role-play line:** "My ILP is losing money. I want to stop it."

### 28. "Why do I need a nomination? I have a will."

- **Really worried about:** Paperwork; thinks the will covers everything.
- **Response A (CPF):** "A will is important. But CPF savings aren't part of your estate and can't be left by will; a CPF nomination decides who receives them. Making one is free." [Fact pack 1.6, 3.3]
- **Response B (insurance):** "For insurance, a trust nomination (spouse or children only) takes precedence over a will. Without an insurance nomination, the insurer may pay up to $150,000 to a 'proper claimant' and the rest goes through the estate. For anything legal, please speak to a lawyer." [Fact pack 3.3]
- **Never say:** "A nomination means you don't need a will." / "Just put your CPF in your will."
- **Role-play line:** "I already did a will. Isn't that enough?"

### 29. "I don't want to talk about death or illness."

- **Really worried about:** Discomfort, superstition, or recent loss.
- **Response A:** "That's completely understandable. We can talk about it as 'planning for what matters to you' and go at your pace."
- **Response B:** "If now isn't the right time, that's fine. We can stop here and pick it up whenever you're ready, or not at all."
- **Never say:** "You have to face reality." / Graphic illness or accident stories. / "Pantang is silly."
- **Role-play line:** "Aiya, don't talk about these things, very sway."

### 30. "Just tell me what to buy. You're the expert."

- **Really worried about:** Overwhelm. They want to hand over the decision.
- **Response A:** "I'm glad you trust me. To recommend anything responsibly, I need to understand your situation first, and you should understand why I'm recommending it." [MAS-2] [MS-1]
- **Response B:** "Let's keep it simple: three questions about your goals, budget and who depends on you. Then I'll explain the options in plain language, including the downsides."
- **Never say:** "Don't worry about the details, just sign here." / "Everyone your age buys this one."
- **Role-play line:** "Aiyah, you decide for me lah."

## 5. When to walk away

Walking away is a professional skill, not a failure. **Stop, or pause and get your coach,** when:

| Signal | What to do |
|---|---|
| The client has said "no" clearly, or "no" twice in different words | Thank them, close warmly, and don't follow up unless they invite it. |
| The client can't afford it without hardship (cutting essentials, borrowing) | Don't recommend. Note the reason. Suggest a review later. |
| No real need emerges from the fact-find | Say so. "Based on what you've told me, I don't see a gap right now." |
| The client doesn't seem to understand (language, cognitive or literacy barriers, distress, recent bereavement) | Pause. Offer a trusted family member or a later meeting. Speak to your coach. [MAS-1] |
| Someone else seems to be pressuring the client, or deciding for them | Stop. Speak to your coach. |
| The client asks you to hide or bend information (health, income, beneficiaries) | Refuse politely. Stop the application. Tell your coach and record it. |
| The meeting turns hostile or unsafe | End the meeting politely and leave. Tell your team leader. |
| The client asks to be removed from contact | Stop. Update your list and the principal's DNC/consent records. [PDPC-1] |
| You're tempted to cut a corner to hit a number | Stop. Talk to your coach. No case is worth it. |

**Exit line:** "Thank you for your time and for being honest with me. If anything changes, you know where to find me."

## 6. Documenting the conversation

Record what happened on the **same day**, in the **principal's approved system and forms only**, never in personal notes apps or chat groups. This protects the client and you. [MAS-2] [MS-1]

**Record after every meeting:**
- [ ] Date, time, place or channel, and who was present (including family members or interpreters)
- [ ] Objections raised, **in the client's own words**
- [ ] What you explained, and which official source or document you showed
- [ ] What the client decided, including "no" or "not now", and why
- [ ] Documents given (for example product summary, illustration, benefits summary), if any
- [ ] Any vulnerability signals and how you responded
- [ ] Agreed next step and date, or "client will contact me"
- [ ] For any recommendation: the needs it addresses, why it is suitable, its disadvantages and costs, and alternatives discussed (the reasonable basis) [MAS-2]
- [ ] For any switch or replacement the client raises: the written comparison of costs, lost benefits and disadvantages, and your coach's review [MAS-2] [MS-1]

**Rules:**
- Facts, not opinions. Write "Client said premium is above budget of $X a month," not "client is cheap."
- Only collect what you need. No unnecessary sensitive data in notes. Follow your principal's data-protection rules.
- Never back-date or change a record after the event. Add a dated correction instead.
- Raise any concern about misconduct (yours or anyone else's) with your supervisor or compliance. MAS Notice FAA-N27 on reporting representative misconduct takes effect on 1 Jan 2027. [Fact pack 5.8]

## 7. Practice plan and scoring

**Weekly huddle (Weeks 5–13):** two cards per rep per huddle, rotating so every rep covers all 30 cards by about Week 13.

**Round format (6 minutes):** The "client" reads the role-play line and plays the real worry. The rep uses LACE and either Response A or B in their own words. The observer scores. Then swap.

| Element | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listened and let the client finish | | | |
| Acknowledged without arguing | | | |
| Asked a question to find the real worry | | | |
| Answered with facts or a source, or exited gracefully | | | |
| No pressure, guarantees, disparagement, names or switching | | | |

**Automatic fail:** any "Never say" line, any figure not in Section 8, or naming a product or insurer. Redo the round.

**Coach note:** reps can also practise hard variants, for example a client who raises Objection 3 then Objection 2, or a supportive spouse who joins halfway (Objection 8).

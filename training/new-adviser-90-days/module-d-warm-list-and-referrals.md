# Module D: Building a Warm List and Asking for Referrals Without Pressure

**When:** Week 2 (warm list), Week 6 (referrals), then reviewed weekly
**Time:** 20-minute talk, 25-minute role-play, 15-minute quiz and review

> People remember how you made them feel. A good referral comes from a client who felt **respected**, not rushed. Source references point to `sources.md`.

---

## Learning goals

By the end of this module, each new representative can:

1. Build a warm list of people they know, sorted by **relationship**, not by "who looks rich".
2. Contact people in a way that respects their personal data and the **Do Not Call** rules. [PDPC-1]
3. Make a first approach that is honest about their new role, with no disguised "catching up" and no bait. [MAS-3]
4. Ask for referrals naturally, and accept "no" warmly.
5. Keep a simple, private and secure record of contacts and consent, following firm policy.

---

## Part 1: The warm list

### Who goes on it
Write down everyone you know by first name. Don't judge yet. Use these prompts:
- Family and relatives
- School, polytechnic, ITE and university friends
- Former colleagues, NS mates, clubs, sports, religious or volunteer groups
- Neighbours, parents from your children's school
- Service providers you know well (hairdresser, tuition teacher, gym trainer)

**Suggested starting goal:** 100 names by the end of Week 2. This is a suggestion, not a quota. Quality of relationship matters more than count.

### How to sort it
| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Close, would happily take your call |
| **B** | Friendly, in touch in the last two years |
| **C** | Acquaintance, would need context |

Sort by relationship strength. **Don't** add notes about people's guessed income or health. Record only what you need, and follow your firm's data policy.

### Personal data and contact rules [PDPC-1]
- Under the PDPA's **Do Not Call** provisions, organisations generally must **not** send marketing messages (calls, texts, fax) to Singapore numbers on the **DNC Registry**, unless they have the person's **clear and unambiguous consent**, or another exemption applies. [PDPC-1]
- An exemption can apply for messages relevant to an **ongoing relationship**, but a series of one-off transactions does **not** create one. [PDPC-1]
- **Practical rule for new reps:** before any marketing call or message to someone who hasn't given you consent, **ask your firm's compliance team** how to check the DNC Registry, and keep a record. Your firm's process is the one to follow.
- Never buy, swap or scrape contact lists.

## Part 2: The honest first approach

Say clearly that you are now a representative. A disguised approach ("just catching up!") damages trust, and MAS has flagged deceptive lead tactics online. [MAS-3, para 1.2(b)]

**Sample message (adapt to your voice; firm approval may be needed for any template):**
> "Hi [Name], hope you're well! I wanted to let you know I've started a new career as a financial adviser representative with [Firm]. I'm not asking you to buy anything. I'm practising helping people review their goals and existing cover. If you're ever open to a 30-minute chat, I'd value it. And if not, no worries at all, I'd still love to catch up."

Why it works: it's honest, it makes no ask to buy, it gives an easy "no", and the friendship comes first.

## Part 3: Asking for referrals without pressure

### When to ask
- **After** you've delivered value: a clear fact-find, a policy review, a helpful explanation. Ask whether or not they bought anything.
- **Never** make advice, a discount or a gift depend on giving names.

### How to ask (the "Permission, Profile, Pass-on" approach)
1. **Permission:** "Can I ask you something? Feel free to say no."
2. **Profile:** "I work best with people like young families who want to get organised. Does anyone come to mind who might find a chat like today's useful?"
3. **Pass-on:** "Would you be comfortable asking them first, and passing my number on if they're keen? I won't contact anyone who hasn't said yes."

Step 3 matters. **The friend asks first**, so the new contact gives consent before you reach out.

## Role-play script (25 minutes)

### Scene 1: The honest call (10 minutes)

**Rep:** "Hi Daniel, it's [Name]. Is now an okay time for two minutes?"

**Daniel:** "Sure, what's up?"

**Rep:** "I wanted to tell you personally that I've started as a financial adviser representative with [Firm]. I'm not calling to sell you anything. I'm building my practice by offering people a no-obligation review of their goals and cover. Would you be open to that sometime, or not right now?"

**Daniel:** "Not really right now. Work's crazy."

**Rep:** "Totally understand, thanks for being honest. Can I check in sometime next year, or would you prefer I don't?"

**Daniel:** "Next year's fine."

**Rep:** "Great. And let's grab that kopi anyway. Nothing to do with work!"

**Observer checks:** Honest about the role? Easy "no" given? Asked permission for follow-up? Friendship protected?

### Scene 2: The referral ask (10 minutes)

**Rep:** "Thanks, Priya. I hope today's review was useful. Can I ask you something? It's completely fine to say no."

**Priya:** "Go ahead."

**Rep:** "I work best with young families getting organised. Does anyone you know come to mind who might find a chat like today's helpful?"

**Priya:** "Maybe my sister. She just had a baby."

**Rep:** "Thank you. Would you be comfortable asking her first? If she's keen, you could pass on my number, or let me know she's okay to be contacted. I won't reach out unless she says yes."

**Priya:** "Sure, I'll ask."

**Rep:** "Really appreciate it. And whatever she decides, thank you for trusting me today."

### Scene 3: "No" (5 minutes)

**Client:** "I'd rather not give names."

**Rep:** "Of course, I completely respect that. Thank you for your time today. You know where to find me if you need anything."

## Quiz (10 questions)

1. Suggested warm-list starting goal by end of Week 2?
2. How should the warm list be sorted?
3. What information should you **not** record about warm-list contacts?
4. Under the PDPA Do Not Call provisions, what generally applies to sending marketing messages to numbers on the DNC Registry?
5. Does a series of one-off transactions create an "ongoing relationship" for the DNC exemption?
6. Why must a first approach say clearly that you are now a representative?
7. When is the best time to ask for a referral?
8. In "Permission, Profile, Pass-on", who contacts the referred person first?
9. True or false: offering a gift in exchange for names is a good way to build referrals.
10. What should you do before making marketing calls to people who haven't given consent?

### Answers

1. **About 100 names.** This is a suggestion, not a quota.
2. By **relationship strength** (A, B, C), not by guessed wealth.
3. Guessed income, guessed health or other personal judgements. Record only what you need, following firm policy.
4. Organisations generally must **not** send them, unless they have clear and unambiguous consent or another exemption applies. [PDPC-1]
5. **No.** [PDPC-1]
6. Honesty builds trust. Disguised approaches damage it, and MAS has flagged deceptive lead solicitation. [MAS-3]
7. **After** delivering value, whether or not the person bought anything. Never as a condition of service.
8. **The existing client** (your friend) asks them first, so consent comes before you reach out.
9. **False.** Don't make advice, gifts or discounts depend on referrals. Firms usually restrict gifts, so check your firm's rules.
10. **Check with your firm's compliance team** on its DNC check process, and keep a record. [PDPC-1]

**Pass mark suggestion:** 8/10, plus a warm list of any size shown to the team leader by Day 14 (names only, no sensitive notes).

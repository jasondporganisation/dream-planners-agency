# Module F: Daily Routine, Time-Blocking and Tracking

**When:** Week 1 (set up the routine), Week 4 (first review), Week 8 (adjust)
**Time:** 20-minute talk, 25-minute role-play, 15-minute quiz and review

> All targets here are **suggestions** to help you build habits. They are not quotas, not promises of income, and not a measure of your worth. Good advisers build steady habits and look after clients well. Your firm's own performance framework and any MAS requirements on how you are assessed come first. [MAS-1] [MAS-6]

---

## Learning goals

By the end of this module, each new representative can:

1. Set up a weekly time-blocked calendar with protected time for learning, prospecting, appointments, admin and rest.
2. Track activity honestly with a simple funnel: **contacts → conversations → fact-finds → recommendation meetings → submissions**.
3. Track **quality** measures as well as activity: complete documents, follow-ups done, client feedback, and no compliance issues. [MAS-1]
4. Run a 15-minute end-of-day review and a 30-minute weekly review.
5. Spot early signs of overload and ask for help.

## Suggested activity ranges (habits, not quotas)

| Phase | Honest new contacts / day | Conversations / week | Fact-finds / week | Recommendation meetings / week |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 (licensing, set-up) | 0 (list-building only) | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Weeks 3–4 | 3–5 | 5–8 | 1–2 (practice and real) | 0–1 |
| Weeks 5–8 | 5–8 | 8–12 | 2–4 | 1–3 |
| Weeks 9–13 | 5–10 | 10–15 | 3–5 | 2–4 |

- "Contact" means an honest, compliant approach (see Module D) after any needed **Do Not Call** check. [PDPC-1]
- **Submissions** are tracked but **never targeted** in this pack. A submission should only follow a proper fact-find, a documented reasonable basis and full disclosure. [MAS-2] [MS-1]
- If your numbers are well below the range, talk to your team leader about what's in the way. Don't just work longer hours.

## The tracker (copy into the firm's approved tool or a simple sheet)

**Daily log**

| Date | Contacts | Conversations | Fact-finds | Recommendation meetings | Submissions | Follow-ups done | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Weekly quality check (tick or cross)**
- [ ] Every fact-find is complete, or refusals are recorded [MAS-2]
- [ ] Every recommendation has documented reasons, **including disadvantages** [MAS-2]
- [ ] Every client received the required documents before deciding [MS-1]
- [ ] Free-look explained correctly every time [LIA-1]
- [ ] Every social media post was firm-approved [MAS-3]
- [ ] No client data in personal chats, photos or notes outside firm systems
- [ ] All follow-ups promised this week were done

**Never record** client NRIC numbers, health details or account numbers in a personal tracker. Use the firm's systems only.

## 20-minute talk outline

### 0:00–0:03 Why routine beats motivation
- Motivation goes up and down. Calendars don't.
- Early in your career, you control the **inputs** (time, practice, honest contacts), not the outcomes.

### 0:03–0:08 Time-blocking
- Walk through the weekly template.
- Live exercise: everyone blocks next week in their calendar now.

### 0:08–0:12 The funnel
- Contacts → conversations → fact-finds → recommendation meetings → submissions.
- Look at your conversion between stages to learn, not to judge. For example, if many conversations lead to few fact-finds, practise your Module B opening.

### 0:12–0:16 Quality first
- The weekly quality check. Why it matters: Fair Dealing Outcomes 2, 3 and 4. [MAS-1]
- A fast submission with missing documents is a problem, not a win.

### 0:16–0:19 Reviews and wellbeing
- End-of-day 15 minutes: What went well? What will I do differently? What's first tomorrow?
- Weekly 30 minutes: Numbers, quality check, one skill to practise next week.
- Watch for signs of overload: skipped meals, no day off, dreading calls. Tell your team leader early. That's a strength, not a weakness.

### 0:19–0:20 Close
- Commitment: share next week's blocked calendar with your buddy by Friday.

## Role-play script (25 minutes)

### Scene 1: The weekly review with a coach (12 minutes)

**Coach:** "Let's look at your week. What are you proudest of?"

**Rep:** "I did my first two real fact-finds."

**Coach:** "Excellent. How did they go against the quality check?"

**Rep:** "One was complete. The other client didn't want to share savings, and I'm not sure I recorded it."

**Coach:** "Good catch. Let's fix that today on the firm's form. Now your numbers: 22 contacts, 6 conversations, 2 fact-finds. What do you notice?"

**Rep:** "Lots of contacts but not many conversations."

**Coach:** "What do you think is behind that?"

**Rep:** "Maybe my message is too long."

**Coach:** "Let's practise a shorter honest opener from Module D. And one more thing: how's your energy?"

**Rep:** "Tired. I did appointments four evenings."

**Coach:** "Let's cap evenings at two next week and protect Sunday. Sustainable beats heroic."

### Scene 2: The over-booked day (8 minutes)

The Rep shows a calendar with five back-to-back appointments and no prep time.

**Task:** The Observer and Rep re-plan the day using the template. Add 30-minute buffers, move one meeting, and keep the end-of-day review.

### Scene 3: Saying "not yet" to a submission (5 minutes)

**Client:** "Just submit it today. I'll send the documents later."

**Rep:** "I appreciate you wanting to move quickly. I need to complete the fact-find and give you the full documents first, so you can be sure it's right. Can we finish it on Thursday?" [MS-1] [MAS-2]

## Quiz (10 questions)

1. Name the five stages of the activity funnel.
2. Are the activity ranges in this module quotas?
3. Why are submissions tracked but not targeted?
4. Name three items on the weekly quality check.
5. What should a 15-minute end-of-day review cover?
6. What's the suggested maximum number of appointment evenings per week?
7. A client wants you to submit before the fact-find and documents are complete. What do you do?
8. Name one piece of client data you must never keep in a personal tracker.
9. What is the minimum suggested time off each week?
10. Your conversations are low compared with contacts. What's a good first step?

### Answers

1. **Contacts → conversations → fact-finds → recommendation meetings → submissions.**
2. **No.** They are suggestions to build habits. Your firm's framework applies.
3. A submission should only follow a proper fact-find, a documented reasonable basis and full disclosure. Targeting submissions creates pressure that can work against fair dealing. [MAS-1] [MAS-2]
4. Any three of: fact-find complete or refusals recorded; reasons documented including disadvantages; documents given before decision; free-look explained; social posts approved; no client data outside firm systems; follow-ups done. [MAS-2] [MS-1] [MAS-3] [LIA-1]
5. What went well, what to do differently, and what's first tomorrow.
6. **Two**, as a suggestion for sustainability.
7. Politely decline. Complete the fact-find and give the full documents first. [MS-1] [MAS-2]
8. Any one of: NRIC numbers, health details, account numbers.
9. **One full day.**
10. Review and practise your opening message (Module D) with your coach. Don't just add more contacts.

**Pass mark suggestion:** 8/10, plus a time-blocked calendar for the next week shared with the team leader.

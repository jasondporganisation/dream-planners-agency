# Module B: Running a Fact-Find and Needs Analysis Conversation

**When:** Week 2 (practise every week until Week 8)
**Time:** 20-minute talk, 25-minute role-play, 15-minute quiz and review

> This module is generic. Use **your principal's own approved fact-find form**. This pack gives the conversation, not the form. Source references such as [MS-1] point to `sources.md`.

---

## Learning goals

By the end of this module, each new representative can:

1. Explain to a client why the fact-find matters and that sharing information is their choice. [MS-1]
2. Run a fact-find covering objectives, risk appetite, income, assets, liabilities, cash flow, existing cover and dependants. [MS-1] [MAS-2]
3. Use open questions, then summarise back so the client can confirm or correct. [MS-1]
4. Identify gaps, or confirm there are none, and record the **reasonable basis** for any recommendation, including its disadvantages for this client. [MAS-2]
5. Handle a client who won't share some information, and record it properly.

---

## The shape of a good fact-find (the "5 C" flow)

| Step | What you do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Context** | Introduce yourself, your firm and what you can advise on. Invite the client to check the MAS register. Explain how the meeting will run. | MoneySense tells clients to expect this and to check the register. [MS-1] [MAS-5] |
| 2. **Concerns** | Ask what prompted the meeting and what matters most. | Objectives come first. [MAS-2] |
| 3. **Current position** | Income, expenses, assets, liabilities, CPF, existing policies and investments, dependants, health (when relevant), risk appetite. | Know your client. [MS-1] [MAS-2] |
| 4. **Check back** | Summarise in their words. Ask "What did I miss?" | Accuracy. The client can check and correct their FNA record. [MS-1] |
| 5. **Commit to next step** | Agree what you'll prepare and when. No product talk yet unless the client asks. | No pressure. Clients never have to decide on the spot. [MS-1] |

## 20-minute talk outline

### 0:00–0:03 Why the fact-find is the advice
- A recommendation without a fact-find has no reasonable basis. FAA-N16 sets out **know your client** and **needs analysis** as core duties, and asks you to document why a product suits the client, **including its disadvantages**. [MAS-2]
- MoneySense tells consumers to give **complete, honest** information and says an adviser may recommend **nothing** if there are no gaps. [MS-1]

### 0:03–0:08 Opening well
- Say who you are and what you can and can't advise on.
- Explain: "I'll ask about your goals, money and family so anything I suggest fits you. You can skip any question, but the less I know, the less precise my advice can be."
- Ask permission to take notes.

### 0:08–0:14 The questions (open first, then specific)
- **Goals:** "If we meet again in five years and things went well, what would have happened?"
- **Family and dependants:** "Who relies on your income today? Who might later?"
- **Cash flow:** "Roughly what comes in and goes out each month? What's left?"
- **Assets and liabilities:** savings, CPF balances (the client can show their CPF statement), property and loans.
- **Existing cover:** "What policies do you already have? May I see the summaries?" Review them fairly. Don't dismiss them.
- **Risk appetite:** "If an investment dropped 20% in a year, what would you do?"
- **Health (only when relevant and with consent):** follow your firm's form exactly.

### 0:14–0:17 Listening and summarising
- Talk about 30% of the time, listen 70%.
- Summarise: "So the three things that matter most are… Did I get that right?"
- Write the client's own words for their goals.

### 0:17–0:19 Gaps, not products
- Show the gap in numbers the client gave you. Use official figures, such as CPF or MediShield Life from Module E, with sources.
- If a client declines to give information, or chooses a product other than what you recommend, **record it on the firm's form**. MoneySense tells consumers that if they pick a different product, its suitability is their responsibility. [MS-1]

### 0:19–0:20 Close
- Agree the next meeting and what you'll bring: a needs summary and options, with the documents listed in Module A.

## Role-play script (25 minutes)

**Set-up:** Pairs plus an Observer with the checklist below. Client profile card: *Mei, 34, married, one child aged 2, salaried, has one existing policy from years ago, no idea what it covers.*

**Rep:** "Thanks for making time, Mei. I'm [Name], a representative of [Firm]. My rep number is [xxx], and you can check it on the MAS register any time. Today I'd like to understand your goals and situation. I won't recommend anything today unless you ask. Is that okay?"

**Mei:** "Sure. My husband said we should 'sort out insurance' now that we have a baby."

**Rep:** "Congratulations on your little one. When you say 'sort out', what would make you feel it's sorted?"

**Mei:** "If something happened to one of us, the other could still pay the flat and childcare."

**Rep:** "That's clear and important. Can I ask a few questions about your monthly budget and the flat loan so we can see what 'still pay' really means in numbers?"

**Mei:** "Okay. Take-home is about [amount]. The loan is about [amount] a month."

**Rep:** "Thank you. And the existing policy? Do you have any documents for it?"

**Mei:** "Somewhere… Do I need them?"

**Rep:** "It helps a lot. I'd rather not suggest anything that overlaps with what you already have. Could you look for the policy summary before we next meet? I'll help you read it."

**Mei:** "I'd rather not tell you our savings."

**Rep:** "That's completely fine. It's your choice. I'll note that you chose not to share it, which means my analysis won't take savings into account. You can share it later if you wish."

**Rep (summary):** "Let me check I've got this right. Your main goal is that if either of you can't work, the flat and childcare are still covered. You have one older policy we'll review. You've chosen not to share savings. Did I miss anything?"

**Mei:** "I'd also like to start saving for her education."

**Rep:** "Noted, that's goal number two. Next time I'll bring a needs summary covering both goals, with options and their pros and cons. You won't need to decide on the day. Does [date] work?"

### Observer checklist
- [ ] Introduced self, firm and rep number, and invited a register check
- [ ] Asked about goals before money
- [ ] Covered dependants, cash flow, liabilities and existing cover
- [ ] Respected the refusal to share, and said it would be recorded
- [ ] Summarised back and asked "what did I miss?"
- [ ] No product named or pushed
- [ ] Clear next step, with no pressure to decide

## Quiz (10 questions)

1. Name the two core FAA-N16 duties that a fact-find serves.
2. What must the documented basis for a recommendation include besides the reasons it fits?
3. List five areas a fact-find should cover.
4. A client refuses to share their savings. What do you do?
5. True or false: if there's no gap, you should still recommend a small product to "start the relationship".
6. Why summarise back to the client?
7. A client chooses a product different from your recommendation. What does MoneySense tell consumers about this?
8. What should you say about your identity at the start of the meeting?
9. Should you ask about health in every fact-find?
10. What is the best next step at the end of a first fact-find meeting?

### Answers

1. **Know your client** and **needs analysis**. [MAS-2]
2. **The disadvantages** of the product for the client's circumstances. [MAS-2]
3. Any five of: objectives, risk appetite, income, assets, liabilities, cash flow, existing insurance and investments, dependants. [MS-1]
4. Respect it. Explain that your analysis will be limited. **Record the refusal** on the firm's form.
5. **False.** MoneySense says an adviser may recommend nothing if no gaps are found. [MS-1]
6. So the client can confirm or correct it. Your record must be accurate, and clients can ask to correct their FNA record. [MS-1]
7. That it is then **their responsibility** to make sure the alternative suits them. Record it. [MS-1]
8. Your name, your firm, the products you can advise on, and that they can verify you on the **MAS register**. [MS-1] [MAS-5]
9. **No.** Only when it's relevant to the need being discussed, with consent, and following your firm's form.
10. Agree a date to present a needs summary and options. No pressure to decide on the spot. [MS-1]

**Pass mark suggestion:** 8/10, plus an observed role-play signed off by the team leader before the first real fact-find.

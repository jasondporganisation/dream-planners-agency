# Module A: MAS and FAA Conduct Basics for Representatives

**When:** Week 1 (repeat key points in Weeks 4 and 9)
**Time:** 20-minute talk, 25-minute role-play, 15-minute quiz and review
**Facilitator:** Team leader or the principal's compliance officer

> Source references such as [MAS-1] point to `sources.md`. Your principal's compliance manual comes first. Where it is stricter than anything here, follow it.

---

## Learning goals

By the end of this module, each new representative can:

1. Name the five Fair Dealing Outcomes and say what each one means for their own behaviour. [MAS-1]
2. Explain in plain words why every recommendation needs a **reasonable basis** built on the client's objectives, financial situation and particular needs. [MAS-2]
3. List the documents a client should receive before deciding, and say why. [MS-1]
4. Explain the free-look period correctly. [MS-1] [LIA-1] [LIA-2]
5. Spot social media posts that would break the MAS digital advertising standards, and fix them. [MAS-3]
6. Know that they must not give advice until their appointment appears on the MAS public register. [MAS-5]

## 20-minute talk outline

### 0:00–0:03 Why this comes first
- Trust is the product. Clients can check who you are. They should never be surprised by what they bought.
- MAS considers whether a firm and its representatives follow the Fair Dealing Guidelines when it decides if they remain **fit and proper**. [MAS-1]
- The message for the room: doing it right is how we build a long career.

### 0:03–0:08 The five Fair Dealing Outcomes [MAS-1]
1. Customers can be confident that fair dealing is central to the firm's culture.
2. Products and services offered are **suitable for their target customer segments**.
3. Customers are served by **competent representatives**.
4. Customers receive **clear, relevant and timely information** that accurately represents what is offered.
5. Complaints are handled in an **independent, effective and prompt** way.

Ask: "Which of these five do you personally control every day?" (Answer: mainly 3 and 4, and how you pass complaints on, which is 5.)

### 0:08–0:12 Needs-based advice: the reasonable basis [MAS-2] [MS-1]
- Before you recommend, you must **know your client**: objectives, risk appetite, income, assets, liabilities, cash flow, existing policies and investments, and dependants. [MS-1]
- You analyse that information and identify what is suitable. You document **why** it is suitable, including its **disadvantages** for this client. [MAS-2]
- If the fact-find shows no gap, it is fine to recommend nothing. [MS-1]
- If a client declines your recommendation and picks something else, record it. MoneySense tells consumers that the alternative is then **their** responsibility. [MS-1]

### 0:12–0:15 Disclosure: what the client should hold before deciding [MS-1] [MS-2]
- For insurance: fact-find (FNA) documentation, a cover page, a product summary, a Product Highlights Sheet (for investment-linked policies) and a policy illustration. [MS-1]
- Illustrations are **projections, not guarantees**. Say so out loud. [MS-1]
- The client never has to buy on the spot. [MS-1]
- **Free-look:** at least **14 days** from receiving the policy document for life policies. If posted or emailed, the 14 days start **7 days** after the posting or emailing date. For investment-linked policies the refund is adjusted for market value, so it can be less than what was paid. [LIA-1] [LIA-2] [MS-1]

### 0:15–0:19 Social media: what not to post [MAS-3]
The MAS Digital Advertising Guidelines took effect on **25 March 2026**. Representatives count as "digital marketers" of their firm. [MAS-3, para 1.7 and 2.1]

Do not:
- Post benefits in one place and leave the risks for a link, the comments or a later slide. **Each post must not mislead when viewed on its own.** [MAS-3, para 5.4]
- Cut disclosures to fit a character limit. Format limits must not cause key risks or caveats to be left out. [MAS-3, para 5.3–5.4]
- Show past returns without a clear statement that **past returns do not indicate future performance**. [MAS-3, Example 2.1(b)]
- Flash a disclaimer too fast to read in a video. [MAS-3, Example 2.1(c)]
- Re-post a client testimonial or a "thank you" screenshot casually. **Re-posting or sharing counts as publishing an advertisement.** [MAS-3, para 5.6]
- Use fake profiles, catfishing or other deceptive ways to get leads. MAS has seen representatives do this and names it as a problem. [MAS-3, para 1.2(b)] Treat "DM me for a free gift" bait the same way: your firm will very likely not approve it.
- Open a business social media account the firm does not know about. Firms may require **prior approval** of accounts and keep a register, and must act against unauthorised accounts. [MAS-3, Example 4.1 and para 5.11]
- Post anything not approved under your firm's process. Expect disciplinary action that gets stricter for repeat breaches. [MAS-3, para 5.12–5.14]

Also never post: client names, photos, policy details or claims stories without written consent and firm approval. Never promise or hint at returns, "guaranteed" outcomes or income.

### 0:19–0:20 Wrap-up
- You may only advise once your appointment shows on the **MAS Financial Institution Representatives Register**. Clients can and should look you up there. [MAS-5] [MS-1]
- If unsure, stop and ask compliance. "Let me check and come back to you" is always a compliant answer.

## Role-play script (25 minutes)

**Set-up:** Three people per group: Representative, Client, Observer. Rotate roles.

### Scene 1: The rushed client (8 minutes)

**Client:** "My colleague bought this plan from you last week. Just sign me up for the same thing. I don't need all the questions."

**Representative:** "Thank you for trusting me. I'd love to help. What suits your colleague may not suit you, though, because your income, family and existing cover are different. Can I take about 20 minutes to understand your situation first? Then anything I suggest will fit *you*."

**Client:** "Fine, but quickly."

**Representative:** "Of course. I'll also give you the product summary and illustration to read. You don't have to decide today. And after you receive your policy you still have a 14-day free-look period to change your mind."

**Observer checks:** Did the rep refuse to copy-paste a recommendation? Did they mention documents, no pressure, and free-look correctly?

### Scene 2: The social media draft (8 minutes)

The Representative shows the Observer this draft post:

> "Retire at 55 with $1M! My clients earned 8% last year. DM me 'RICH' for a free gift. #financialfreedom"

**Task:** Together, list every problem, then rewrite it.

**Model answer, the problems:**
- It hints at a promised outcome ("retire at 55 with $1M").
- It shows past returns with no statement that they don't predict the future. [MAS-3]
- Its "DM me for a free gift" bait is a lead tactic that risks being deceptive. MAS has flagged deceptive lead solicitation. [MAS-3, para 1.2(b)]
- It has no balance or risks, and leaves out who the rep is and which firm. [MAS-3]
- It has not been approved by the firm.

**Model rewrite (still needs firm approval):**
> "Planning for retirement starts with knowing where you stand today. I help people in Singapore review their goals, CPF and existing cover, with no obligation. [Name], Representative of [Licensed FA firm], MAS Rep No. [xxx]."

### Scene 3: The awkward question (9 minutes)

**Client:** "Is this the best plan in the market? Can you guarantee I'll get this amount?"

**Representative:** "Good questions. I can only advise on the products my firm offers, and I'll explain why I think this one fits your needs. That includes what it *doesn't* do well for you. The illustration shows projections, not guarantees. I'll show you which parts are guaranteed and which are not."

**Observer checks:** No "best" claims, no guarantees, disadvantages mentioned, guaranteed and non-guaranteed parts separated.

## Quiz (10 questions)

1. How many Fair Dealing Outcomes are there, and which one is about representatives' competence?
2. True or false: if a fact-find shows no gap, the rep should still recommend something so the meeting isn't wasted.
3. Name three pieces of information a rep must gather to have a reasonable basis for a recommendation.
4. What must a rep's documented reasoning include besides why the product fits?
5. How long is the free-look period for a life policy, and when does it start if the policy is emailed?
6. True or false: under the free-look period, an investment-linked policy always refunds 100% of the premium.
7. From what date do the MAS Guidelines on Standards of Conduct for Digital Advertising Activities apply?
8. A rep posts the benefits on an Instagram carousel and the risks only on their website. Is this acceptable? Why?
9. Does re-sharing a client's thank-you post count as an advertisement?
10. Where can a client check that a rep is properly appointed?

### Answers

1. **Five.** Outcome 3: customers are served by competent representatives. [MAS-1]
2. **False.** MoneySense says that if no gaps are found, the adviser may recommend nothing. [MS-1]
3. Any three of: objectives, risk appetite, income, assets, liabilities, cash flow, existing insurance and investments, dependants. [MS-1] [MAS-2]
4. **The disadvantages** of the product for this client's circumstances. [MAS-2]
5. **At least 14 days** from receiving the policy document. If it's posted or emailed, the 14 days start **7 days after** the posting or emailing date. [LIA-1] [LIA-2]
6. **False.** The refund is adjusted for changes in the market value of the investments, so it may be less. [LIA-2] [MS-1]
7. **25 March 2026.** [MAS-3, para 1.7]
8. **No.** Each post must not mislead when viewed on its own. Putting risks on a separate webpage is the example MAS gives of what is inappropriate. For carousels, disclosures go on every frame or on the first frame. [MAS-3, para 5.4 and Example 2.1(d)]
9. **Yes.** Re-posting or sharing content, including testimonials, is dissemination of an advertisement. [MAS-3, para 5.6]
10. The **MAS Financial Institution Representatives Register**, at eservices.mas.gov.sg/rr. [MAS-5]

**Pass mark suggestion:** 8/10. Anyone below that re-reads this module and re-takes the quiz with the team leader before their first client meeting.

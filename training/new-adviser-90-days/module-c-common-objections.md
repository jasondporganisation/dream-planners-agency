# Module C: The 10 Most Common Objections, with Calm and Compliant Responses

**When:** Week 5 (practise in every team huddle from Week 5 to Week 13)
**Time:** 20-minute talk, 25-minute role-play, 15-minute quiz and review
**Go further:** the [Objection Handling Playbook](../objection-handling-playbook.md) covers 30 objections from first meetings and reviews, when to walk away and how to document the conversation. Print the [one-page cheat sheet](../objection-cheatsheet.md) for huddles.

> An objection is information, not an attack. Our aim is not to "overcome" it. We aim to **understand it, answer honestly, and respect the client's decision.** Source references point to `sources.md`.

---

## Learning goals

By the end of this module, each new representative can:

1. Use the **LACE** method: Listen, Acknowledge, Clarify, Explain (or Exit gracefully).
2. Respond to the ten most common objections without pressure, false urgency, "best in market" claims or guarantees. [MAS-1] [MS-1]
3. Recognise when "no" means no, and close the meeting warmly.
4. Explain free-look, documents and "you don't have to decide today" accurately. [MS-1] [LIA-1] [LIA-2]

---

## What we never say (because it breaks Fair Dealing Outcome 4 or 2) [MAS-1]

- "This offer ends today. You must sign now." Clients are never obliged to buy immediately. [MS-1]
- "This is the best plan in the market."
- "You're guaranteed to get this amount." Illustrations are projections, not guarantees. [MS-1]
- "Everyone your age is buying this."
- "You'll lose out if you don't switch now." Switching can be costly. You must explain the costs and disadvantages. [MS-1] [MAS-2]
- "Don't worry about the fine print."
- Anything about how much you earn from the sale.

## The 10 objections and model responses

| # | Objection | Calm, compliant response |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"I need to think about it."** | "Of course. This is an important decision and you should never feel rushed. What part would you like to think over? I can leave you the product summary and illustration to read. Shall I call you on [day] to answer any questions?" [MS-1] |
| 2 | **"It's too expensive."** | "Thank you for telling me. Let's look at your budget again. We can look at a smaller amount, a different structure, or simply wait. It should be something you can keep up comfortably. What monthly amount would feel right?" |
| 3 | **"I already have enough insurance."** | "That's great to hear. Would it help if we reviewed what you have against the goals you mentioned? If it already covers them, I'll tell you so, and there's nothing to add." [MS-1] |
| 4 | **"I'll just rely on MediShield Life / CPF."** | "Those are important national foundations. Let me show you exactly what they cover, on the official pages, so you can decide whether that feels enough for you." (Use Module E figures, from the live pages.) [MOH-1] [CPF-7] |
| 5 | **"I don't trust financial advisers."** | "That's fair, and I'd rather earn your trust than ask for it. You can check my registration on the MAS register. You'll get every document before deciding, and you can ask me to put my reasons in writing." [MAS-5] [MS-1] |
| 6 | **"My friend or relative is an agent."** | "That's good. Someone you know can help you well. I'm happy to be a second opinion, or to step back. What would be most helpful?" |
| 7 | **"I'm too young or healthy to need this."** | "Being young and healthy is a great position to be in. Shall we look at who relies on you, and what you'd want covered if plans changed? If nothing is needed now, that's a fine answer." |
| 8 | **"I want to buy online by myself."** | "That's a valid choice. MoneySense has a guide to buying direct, and I'm happy to share it. If you'd like help comparing options later, I'm here." [MS-3] |
| 9 | **"Can you guarantee the returns?"** | "No, and I'd be wrong to. The illustration shows which parts are guaranteed and which are not. Projections are not guarantees, and past performance doesn't predict future returns. Let me show you both columns." [MS-1] [MAS-3] |
| 10 | **"What if I change my mind after signing?"** | "You have a free-look period. For life policies it's at least 14 days from when you receive the policy document. If it's posted or emailed, the 14 days start 7 days after it's sent. For investment-linked policies the refund is adjusted for market movements, so it can be less than you paid." [LIA-1] [LIA-2] [MS-1] |

## 20-minute talk outline

### 0:00–0:03 The mindset
- Objections usually mean "I'm not sure yet", "I don't understand" or "this isn't for me".
- Fair dealing means clear information and suitable products. Pushing past a real "no" fails both. [MAS-1]

### 0:03–0:07 The LACE method
- **Listen:** let the client finish. Pause for two seconds.
- **Acknowledge:** "That makes sense."
- **Clarify:** "Can I ask what's behind that?"
- **Explain** the honest facts with a source, **or Exit**: "No problem. Thank you for your time."

### 0:07–0:16 Walk through the 10 objections
- Read each model response aloud. Ask the group what makes it compliant: no pressure, a source, the client in control.

### 0:16–0:19 Red lines
- Go through "What we never say."
- Switching (replacing an existing policy): only after a full comparison of costs, lost benefits and disadvantages, all documented. [MAS-2] [MS-1]

### 0:19–0:20 Close
- The goal is not a "yes". The goal is a client who understands their choice.

## Role-play script (25 minutes)

**Round format:** The Client draws two objection cards. The Rep uses LACE. The Observer scores each element from 0 to 2.

### Sample run: Objection 2 then Objection 1

**Client:** "Honestly, this is more than I wanted to spend."

**Rep (Listen, then Acknowledge):** "Thank you for being upfront. That's really useful."

**Rep (Clarify):** "Is it the monthly amount, or more that you're unsure it's worth it?"

**Client:** "The monthly amount. We just had the baby."

**Rep (Explain):** "That makes complete sense. We can look at a smaller amount, focus on the most important goal first, or revisit in a few months. What feels comfortable?"

**Client:** "I need to think about it."

**Rep:** "Of course. Here's the summary and illustration to read at home. There's no deadline from me. Would you like me to call on Thursday, or would you rather contact me when you're ready?"

**Client:** "I'll contact you."

**Rep (Exit gracefully):** "Perfect. Thank you, Mei. Congratulations again on your little one."

## Quiz (10 questions)

1. What does LACE stand for?
2. A client says "I need to think about it." Give one compliant response.
3. True or false: "This price ends today" is acceptable if it's actually true.
4. A client asks if returns are guaranteed. What must you say?
5. How long is the free-look period for a life policy?
6. Why can an investment-linked policy's free-look refund be less than the premium paid?
7. A client says they already have enough cover. What's the right next step?
8. What must happen before recommending that a client replace an existing policy?
9. A client says, "My cousin is an agent." What is a good response?
10. Which Fair Dealing Outcome is broken by misleading a client about a product's features?

### Answers

1. **Listen, Acknowledge, Clarify, Explain (or Exit).**
2. For example: "Of course. Here are the documents to read, and you don't need to decide today. Shall I call on [day]?" Clients are never obliged to buy immediately. [MS-1]
3. **False** as a closing tactic. Don't use deadlines to pressure a client. They must have time and documents to decide. [MS-1] Even a genuine deadline must be stated factually, without pressure, and following firm rules.
4. Say no. Show the guaranteed and non-guaranteed parts. Illustrations are projections, not guarantees, and past performance doesn't predict the future. [MS-1] [MAS-3]
5. **At least 14 days** from receiving the policy document. If posted or emailed, it starts 7 days after posting or emailing. [LIA-1] [LIA-2]
6. The refund is adjusted for changes in the **market value** of the underlying investments. [LIA-2]
7. Offer a fair review against their goals. If it's enough, say so. [MS-1]
8. A full comparison of costs, benefits lost and disadvantages, documented as part of the reasonable basis. [MAS-2] [MS-1]
9. Respect the relationship. Offer to be a second opinion or to step back.
10. **Outcome 4**: clear, relevant, timely information that accurately represents the product. [MAS-1]

**Pass mark suggestion:** 8/10, plus an average role-play score of 8 or more (out of 10) across two rounds.

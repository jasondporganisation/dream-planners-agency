# Huddles: March 2027

**Facilitators:** Heron (people) and Spectre (sales). **Length:** 30 minutes. See the [format and rules](README.md).

**March focus:** annual reviews, tax-filing season (IRAS filing usually opens in March; check iras.gov.sg for this year's deadline) and a Fair Dealing refresher before the quarter closes.

**Standing rules:** figures come from the [fact pack](../../compliance/sg-fact-pack-2026-11.md) only, with their effective dates. Do not name products or insurers. Make no earnings claims. Do not name clients.

## Week 9: Tue 2 Mar 2027: Annual reviews that actually happen

**Theme:** Client reviews  
**Why this week:** Q1 is the natural window for annual reviews. Booking them early spreads the load across the year.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no names.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. Use the 30-minute review agenda: life changes, fact-find refresh, cover check, nominations, will and LPA check-in, and next steps.
2. Nominations are a review must. Marriage revokes an existing CPF nomination, but divorce does not. CPF savings do not go through a will, and making a nomination is free (fact pack §1.6).
3. Write up the review within 2 working days, including what was discussed and what the client decided not to do.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Review walkthrough (threes).** Client persona: *"Raj, 34, married last year, first child on the way."* The adviser runs the life-changes and nominations section of the review agenda. The observer uses checklist B in the review agenda.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Asked about life changes first
- [ ] Mentioned that marriage revokes the CPF nomination
- [ ] Did not give legal advice on wills; referred out
- [ ] Agreed next steps and a write-up

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many annual reviews will you book this week?*

## Week 10: Tue 9 Mar 2027: Tax-filing season: records, not reliefs advice

**Theme:** Planning concepts  
**Why this week:** Clients are filing taxes and will ask about reliefs.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no names.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. Explain how relief works in general terms: reliefs reduce chargeable income, not the tax bill dollar for dollar. Relief caps and amounts are **not in our fact pack**. Send clients to iras.gov.sg and do not quote numbers.
2. We are not tax advisers. Help clients find their documents and suggest they check IRAS or a tax professional for their own case.
3. Use tax season as a planning prompt: "While you're looking at your year, shall we put your annual review in the diary?"

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Boundary drill (pairs).** The client asks: "How much can I save on tax if I top up CPF?" The adviser practises a reply that explains the concept, declines to quote a figure, points to IRAS or CPF and offers a review. Do 3 rounds, each with a different persona.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Quoted no figure that is not in the fact pack
- [ ] Made clear they are not a tax adviser
- [ ] Named the official source
- [ ] Turned the question into a review invitation

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many review invitations will you send off the back of tax season?*

## Week 11: Tue 16 Mar 2027: Fair Dealing and documentation refresher

**Theme:** Compliance  
**Why this week:** It is the end of the quarter. Good files now prevent problems later.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no names.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. MAS's Fair Dealing outcomes ask for suitable recommendations from competent representatives, clear and timely information, and complaints handled properly. Ask whether each file would show that.
2. Document the reason for every recommendation: the client's need, the options considered and why this option fits. If it isn't written down, it didn't happen.
3. Record what the client declined or chose against your recommendation, and that you explained the consequences.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**File audit drill (pairs).** Each person brings one anonymised file note from this quarter, with all client identifiers removed. Their partner checks it against the documentation checklist in the review agenda and Module A. Fix one gap on the spot.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Need, options and reason are recorded
- [ ] Client declines or deviations are recorded
- [ ] Note was written within 2 working days
- [ ] Contains no identifying data beyond what the approved system needs

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many open file notes will you complete or tidy up before the quarter ends?*

## Week 12: Tue 23 Mar 2027: "Can you guarantee the returns?"

**Theme:** Objection handling and compliance  
**Why this week:** Clients comparing options often anchor on illustration numbers.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no names.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. For participating policies, illustrations show projected values at **4.25%** and **3.00%** a year (LIA caps since 1 Jul 2021). These rates are **illustrative, not guaranteed** (fact pack §5.2).
2. Since 16 Nov 2026, ILP illustrations add a **0% return column** and a lapse statement. Never discuss ILP projections without the 0% scenario (fact pack §5.8).
3. Honesty builds trust: "Some parts are guaranteed and some aren't. Let me show you which is which." Never say "you can't lose money".

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Card drill.** Use playbook card **#15 "Can you guarantee the returns?"** Work in pairs and do 3 rounds. Round 3 is a tough client who pushes: "But what do you *expect* it to give?"

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Separated guaranteed from non-guaranteed parts
- [ ] Mentioned both illustrated rates (and the 0% scenario for ILPs)
- [ ] Did not predict or expect a return
- [ ] Named no insurer or product

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *Who (by count) needs a follow-up to re-explain the guaranteed and non-guaranteed parts of what they hold?*

## Week 13: Tue 30 Mar 2027: Q1 review and Q2 plan

**Theme:** Time management and mindset  
**Why this week:** This is the last huddle of the quarter.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no names.
- Give Q1 totals: appointments, fact-finds, reviews and submissions (counts only). What was your best week, and why?
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. Look at the ratios, not just the totals. Track appointments per fact-find and fact-finds per recommendation. A weak ratio shows you which skill to practise.
2. Keep, start, stop. Name one habit to keep, one to start and one to stop for Q2.
3. Compare yourself with your Q1 self, not with the person next to you.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Peer coaching (pairs, 4 min each way).** Each person shares their Q1 ratios. Their partner asks 3 coaching questions, from the coach checklist, without giving advice. Close by writing the Q2 keep, start and stop on one card.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] The coach asked rather than told
- [ ] The plan is specific and measurable
- [ ] No income or earnings targets were mentioned

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *What are your Q2 keep, start and stop? Read them out.*

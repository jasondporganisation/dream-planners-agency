## Week 18: Tue 4 May 2027: Social media and digital advertising rules

**Theme:** Compliance  
**Why this week:** Posting tends to spike with mid-year content. FSG-03 has been in force since 25 Mar 2026.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no client names. The facilitator records team totals only, never individual numbers by name, and does not rank people.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. MAS treats representatives as "digital marketers". **Each post must stand on its own.** A disclaimer in the bio or a link does not fix a misleading post (FSG-03 para 5.4, fact pack §5.1).
2. Re-sharing content, including client testimonials, counts as advertising (para 5.6). Never re-share client comments that haven't been vetted.
3. Get every post approved through our principal's process before it goes live. Interviews and podcast guest spots need prior approval too (fact pack §5.3).

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Post review drill (pairs).** The facilitator shows 4 made-up draft posts. Pairs mark each one as approve, fix or reject, and name the rule involved. Then rewrite one "fix" post.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Spotted bio-link-only disclaimers
- [ ] Spotted the word "guaranteed" used wrongly
- [ ] Spotted unvetted testimonials
- [ ] Named the FSG-03 or fact pack rule

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *Which of your scheduled posts will you re-check against FSG-03 before they go out? Give a count.*

## Week 19: Tue 11 May 2027: Rejection and explanatory style

**Theme:** Mindset  
**Why this week:** It is mid-quarter, and the no's add up.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no client names. The facilitator records team totals only, never individual numbers by name, and does not rank people.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. How you explain a "no" to yourself matters. "They weren't ready this time" is temporary and specific. "I'm bad at this" is permanent and general, and it drains activity.
2. Separate the person from the outcome. You can do everything right and still hear no.
3. Ask for help early. A struggling week is a coaching conversation, not a secret.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Reframe drill (pairs).** Each person writes 3 real recent setbacks (no client names). Their partner helps rewrite each one as temporary, specific and with a next action. Share one with the room.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Reframes are honest, not falsely positive
- [ ] Each one has a next action
- [ ] Nobody compares themselves with others

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *What is one setback you'll act on this week, and one person you'll ask for help?*

## Week 20: Tue 18 May 2027: Claims support as a service moment

**Theme:** Client reviews and service  
**Why this week:** How you behave during a claim is the strongest review a client will ever give you.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no client names. The facilitator records team totals only, never individual numbers by name, and does not rank people.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. Be present fast. Acknowledge within the same day and explain the process and documents needed. See the claims support guide.
2. Don't promise outcomes. The insurer assesses each claim, and your job is to help the client submit a complete, accurate one.
3. Follow up on schedule even when there is no news. Silence is what clients remember.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Claims call role-play (threes).** Client persona: *"A spouse calls to say their husband is in hospital."* The adviser handles the first call with empathy, explains the next steps and promises nothing about payout.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Showed empathy first
- [ ] Set out clear document and next-step guidance
- [ ] Made no promise of payout
- [ ] Agreed a follow-up time

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *Which open claims or service requests (count) will you update clients on this week?*

## Week 21: Tue 25 May 2027: Referrals through centres of influence

**Theme:** Referrals  
**Why this week:** Some clients are natural connectors: HR leads, community organisers, small business owners.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no client names. The facilitator records team totals only, never individual numbers by name, and does not rank people.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. A centre of influence is someone others trust for advice. Serve them well first, then share what you do and who you help.
2. Offer value, not commission. Free education talks (approved content only) or a checklist they can share. Never offer payment for introductions without checking our principal's rules.
3. Disclosure matters. If anyone introduces you for payment, MAS expects them to disclose it (FSG-03, fact pack §5.1).

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Centre of influence conversation (pairs).** Persona: *"A community centre volunteer who runs parent talks."* The adviser proposes a no-pitch money-basics session using approved material. Their partner pushes back on time and trust concerns.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Proposal is educational, not a sales event
- [ ] Mentions approval of materials
- [ ] No inducement offered
- [ ] Clear next step

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *Which 1–2 potential centres of influence (by role, not name) will you approach?*

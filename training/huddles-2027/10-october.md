## Week 40: Tue 5 Oct 2027: Year-end CPF top-ups: the concept

**Theme:** Planning concepts  
**Why this week:** Clients start asking about year-end top-ups in Q4.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no client names. The facilitator records team totals only, never individual numbers by name, and does not rank people.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. A top-up builds retirement savings for yourself or family members. Tax relief may apply, but **relief caps and cut-off dates are not in our fact pack**. Send clients to cpf.gov.sg and iras.gov.sg for this year's rules.
2. Link top-ups to the retirement sums. For members turning 55 in 2027 the sums are BRS **$114,100**, FRS **$228,200** and ERS **$456,400** (fact pack §1.3). These are benchmarks, not personal needs.
3. Top-ups are generally hard to undo, and CPF money is locked in until it is withdrawn under CPF's rules. Make sure the emergency buffer and cashflow come first.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Explain-and-refer drill (pairs).** Client persona: *"Grace, 47, asks: should I top up my mum's CPF before year-end to save tax?"* The adviser explains the concept, quotes no relief figure, points to CPF and IRAS, and checks Grace's cashflow first.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Quoted no relief cap or deadline that isn't in the fact pack
- [ ] Called the retirement sums benchmarks
- [ ] Raised liquidity and cashflow first
- [ ] Named the official sources

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many clients will you invite to a year-end review that includes a neutral top-up check?*

## Week 41: Tue 12 Oct 2027: Year-end planning invitations

**Theme:** Prospecting  
**Why this week:** Year-end is a natural reason to talk, while people still have time to act.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no client names. The facilitator records team totals only, never individual numbers by name, and does not rank people.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. Lead with the calendar: "Before the year closes, would a 30-minute check on nominations, cover and goals be useful?"
2. Segment your list: recent life events, no review in 12 months, clients turning 55 or 65, and new parents. Each segment gets a tailored message.
3. Never use "deadline" pressure for products. Calendar prompts are about the client's own admin, such as nominations and records.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Segment and script (pairs).** Each person writes one invitation for each of 2 segments. Their partner rates each for relevance and pressure, and you rewrite the weaker one.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Message is tailored to the segment
- [ ] No product or price
- [ ] No false urgency
- [ ] Clear, optional ask

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many year-end invitations will you send, and to which segments?*

## Week 42: Tue 19 Oct 2027: The sandwich generation: parents and children

**Theme:** Fact-finding  
**Why this week:** Year-end family gatherings bring up caregiving worries.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no client names. The facilitator records team totals only, never individual numbers by name, and does not rank people.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. Ask about care, not just money: who would look after an ageing parent, and how would that affect the client's work and income?
2. Ageing is real. Life expectancy at 65 is **21.6 more years** for residents (2025, SingStat; fact pack §4), and that's an average, not a prediction. Plan for long horizons.
3. Prompt legacy and capacity planning. Without an LPA, a family needs a court-appointed Deputy (fact pack §3.1). We prompt and refer.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Three-generation fact-find (threes).** Client persona: *"Kumar, 44, two children, father recently had a fall."* The adviser maps the care and money responsibilities across three generations in 8 minutes.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Asked about care time and work impact
- [ ] Quoted life expectancy as an average
- [ ] Mentioned the LPA concept and referred out
- [ ] No product talk

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many fact-finds will include a parents and caregiving section this week?*

## Week 43: Tue 26 Oct 2027: "Send me the details first" and "I don't have time"

**Theme:** Objection handling  
**Why this week:** Q4 calendars are full, and these stalls go up.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no client names. The facilitator records team totals only, never individual numbers by name, and does not rank people.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. "Send details" usually means "I'm not sure this is relevant". Ask one question to make it relevant, then offer a short, specific slot.
2. "No time" is about priorities. Offer 20 minutes, a video call or a lunch slot, and respect a real no.
3. If you do send material, send only approved, product-neutral content and agree when you'll follow up.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Card drill.** Use playbook cards **#10 "Can you send me the details first?"** and **#11 "I don't have time."** Do 3 rounds in pairs, with a strict 45-second limit for each response.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Asked a relevance question
- [ ] Offered a short, specific slot
- [ ] Sent approved material only
- [ ] Respected a real no

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many "send details" stalls will you convert into a specific next step or a respectful close?*

# Huddles: January 2027

**Facilitators:** Heron (people) and Spectre (sales). **Length:** 30 minutes. See the [format and rules](README.md).

**January focus:** start the year clean. Cover what changed on 1 Jan 2027, set up a weekly routine and build fact-finding skill. The fact pack was compiled on 3 Nov 2026 and is due for re-checking on 1 Jan 2027. It expires after 31 Jan 2027 unless it is re-verified. Week 1 is the moment to confirm that it has been re-checked.

**Standing rules:** figures come from the [fact pack](../../compliance/sg-fact-pack-2026-11.md) only, with their effective dates. Do not name products or insurers. Make no earnings claims. Do not name clients.

## Week 1: Tue 5 Jan 2027: What changed on 1 Jan 2027

**Theme:** Compliance and planning concepts  
**Why this week:** Several figures changed on New Year's Day. Anything quoted from memory this week is probably out of date.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no names.
- Share one win from the December holidays, or a plan for this quarter.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. CPF contribution rates rose on 1 Jan 2027. The total rate for workers above 55 to 60 went from 34% to **35.5%** (employer 16.5%, employee 19%). For workers above 60 to 65 it went from 25% to **26%** (13% / 13%). The Ordinary Wage ceiling stays at **$8,000 a month** (fact pack §1.1, §1.2).
2. The retirement sums for members turning 55 in 2027 are BRS **$114,100**, FRS **$228,200** and ERS **$456,400** (fact pack §1.3). They are CPF benchmarks, not personal retirement needs. Never say "you need $228,200 to retire".
3. MAS Notice FAA-N27 (Reporting of Misconduct of Representatives) is in force from 1 Jan 2027. The new SDIC Annex P-B applies, and the wording for representatives is unchanged (fact pack §5.8). Conduct is a record that follows you between firms.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Quote-it-right drill (pairs).** Partner A reads a "never say" line from the fact pack (§1.2, §1.3, §1.4). Partner B rewrites it into the "safe way to say it" form, with source and date, in one sentence. Swap after 3 lines. Then each pair writes one client-friendly sentence about the CPF rate change for someone aged 57.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] The figure matches the fact pack exactly
- [ ] An effective date is stated
- [ ] The source is named (CPF Board or MAS)
- [ ] Nothing is called guaranteed

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many clients aged above 55 will you send a neutral, fact-pack-worded note about the CPF rate change this week?*

## Week 2: Tue 12 Jan 2027: Your weekly operating rhythm

**Theme:** Time management  
**Why this week:** Habits set in the first fortnight of the year tend to stick.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no names.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. Block your time before you fill it. Prospecting, appointments, admin and learning each get a fixed slot in the calendar. See Module F's daily routine.
2. Track activity, not outcomes. Appointments booked, fact-finds completed and reviews held are inside your control. Submissions follow from them.
3. Hold a 20-minute weekly review every Friday or Saturday. Ask what happened, what is next week's top three, and who is waiting to hear from you.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Calendar build (individual, then pairs).** Each person blocks next week in their own calendar: at least two prospecting blocks, one admin block and one learning block. Their partner checks the plan against Module F and challenges one block that looks unrealistic.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Prospecting blocks are on the calendar, not just on a list
- [ ] An admin and documentation block exists
- [ ] There is at least one evening or weekend boundary

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *Which recurring blocks will you protect this week, and what will you say no to?*

## Week 3: Tue 19 Jan 2027: Fact-finding: open questions first

**Theme:** Fact-finding  
**Why this week:** January is when people set goals. Clients are open to talking about plans.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no names.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. Spend the first 70% of the meeting on the person, not the paperwork. Ask open questions: "What would you like your money to do for you in the next five years?"
2. Cover the whole picture before any solution: income, expenses, CPF, existing cover, dependants, goals, risk attitude and health. A partial fact-find gives a partial recommendation. See Module B.
3. If a client declines to share information, record that they declined and explain the limits it places on your advice. Do not fill in the gaps with guesses.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Persona role-play (threes: adviser, client, observer).** Client persona: *"Mei, 29, accountant, renting, saving for a wedding, says she already has insurance through work."* The adviser runs 8 minutes of discovery only, with no product talk. The observer counts open versus closed questions.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Asked at least 5 open questions before any closed one
- [ ] Asked about goals before cover
- [ ] Summarised back what they heard
- [ ] Did not mention any product

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many full fact-finds will you complete this week, and with which kinds of client? Do not use names.*

## Week 4: Tue 26 Jan 2027: Pre-CNY reconnect, not pitch

**Theme:** Prospecting  
**Why this week:** CNY is a natural reason to get back in touch. Use it to show care, not to pitch.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no names.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. Send warm-list greetings with no ask attached. One personal line beats a forwarded e-card. Use the templates in client-care messages.
2. Prospecting is about permission. "Would you be open to a coffee after CNY to catch up on plans for the year?" is an invitation the person can decline.
3. Respect consent and PDPA. Only contact people who gave you their number, and do not add people to broadcast lists without permission.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Message clinic (pairs).** Each person drafts 3 CNY messages: one for a client, one for a warm-list friend and one for a lapsed contact. Partners rate each message: does it feel like care or like a sales pitch? Rewrite any that feel like a pitch.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] No product or price is mentioned
- [ ] The message is personalised
- [ ] Any invitation is optional and easy to decline

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many personal CNY greetings will you send, and how many after-CNY coffees will you invite people to?*

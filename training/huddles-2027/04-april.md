## Week 14: Tue 6 Apr 2027: MediShield Life basics, and why we don't quote 2027 deductibles

**Theme:** Planning concepts and compliance  
**Why this week:** The second phase of the inpatient deductible increase took effect on 1 Apr 2027. The new amounts are not in our fact pack.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no names.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. MediShield Life is basic, lifelong cover for large hospital bills, sized for Class B2/C wards in public hospitals. It covers all citizens and PRs, including pre-existing conditions (fact pack §2.1).
2. The current claim limits are up to **$200,000 per policy year** with **no lifetime limit**, subject to per-treatment limits (fact pack §2.4, benefits from 1 Jun 2026).
3. The 2027 deductible amounts are **UNVERIFIED**. Do not quote any deductible as current until the fact pack is refreshed. Say: "Deductibles changed on 1 April. Let's check the latest on the MOH site together."

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Myth-buster drill (pairs).** The client says: (1) "MediShield Life covers my whole bill", (2) "MediShield Life is useless", (3) "I have a pre-existing condition, so I'm not covered." The adviser answers each in 30 seconds using only fact pack wording.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Used fact pack wording
- [ ] Quoted no 2027 deductible
- [ ] Named no insurer or product
- [ ] Kept a neutral tone, with no fear-selling

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many clients will you invite to a hospital-cover check as part of their review?*

## Week 15: Tue 13 Apr 2027: Prospecting: a daily 30-minute block

**Theme:** Prospecting  
**Why this week:** Pipelines often thin out after the Q1 review rush.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no names.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. Consistency beats intensity. Thirty minutes every working day does more than one panicked Saturday.
2. Keep a warm-list ladder: reconnect, catch up, then a permission-based invitation. Give every name a next action and a date (Module D).
3. Treat rejection as data, not a verdict. Track the reasons people say no so you can see patterns.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Live list drill (individual, 6 min, then pairs).** Each person picks 10 warm-list names (initials only on paper) and writes the next action and date for each. Their partner challenges any "next action" that is vague.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Every name has an action and a date
- [ ] Actions are permission-based
- [ ] No cold-messaging of strangers

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many prospecting blocks will you hold, and how many conversations do you plan from them?*

## Week 16: Tue 20 Apr 2027: Fact-finding: dependants and income needs

**Theme:** Fact-finding and planning concepts  
**Why this week:** Most cover gaps come from not asking who depends on the client's income.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no names.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. Map who depends on the client: children, a spouse, ageing parents, siblings. Ask about money and about care time.
2. Use the concept of income replacement: how many years would the family need support, and for what? Work it out from the client's own numbers, not a rule of thumb.
3. Count existing resources before any gap: savings, CPF, employer benefits and existing cover. The gap is what remains.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Needs-mapping role-play (threes).** Client persona: *"Siti, 41, single income, two children aged 8 and 12, mother living with them."* The adviser maps dependants and existing resources on paper in 8 minutes and recommends nothing.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Asked about care time as well as money
- [ ] Counted existing resources
- [ ] Used no rule-of-thumb multiples
- [ ] Made no recommendation in the drill

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many fact-finds this week will include a completed dependants map?*

## Week 17: Tue 27 Apr 2027: "It's too expensive" and "CPF / MediShield Life is enough"

**Theme:** Objection handling  
**Why this week:** Cost and "the government covers it" are the two biggest reasons people decline.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no names.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. "Too expensive" is usually about priorities. Go back to the client's own goals and budget. A smaller plan that fits beats a big plan that lapses.
2. Respect the national schemes. They are the foundation. Explain what they are designed to cover and let the client judge any gap.
3. Never pressure someone into a premium they can't sustain. Suitability includes affordability.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Card drill.** Use playbook cards **#3 "It's too expensive"** and **#9 "CPF / MediShield Life is enough."** Do 3 rounds each in pairs. The observer times the adviser's talking share, which should stay under 50%.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Went back to the client's stated goals
- [ ] Spoke positively about the national schemes
- [ ] Checked affordability
- [ ] Talked for less than half the time

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many past "too expensive" conversations will you revisit with a budget-first approach?*

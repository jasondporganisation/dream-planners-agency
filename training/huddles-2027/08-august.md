## Week 31: Tue 3 Aug 2027: Reviews after life events

**Theme:** Client reviews  
**Why this week:** Life events (marriage, a baby, a new job, a new home) are when plans go out of date.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no client names. The facilitator records team totals only, never individual numbers by name, and does not rank people.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. Set event triggers in the cadence: ask about changes at every touchpoint, not only at the annual review.
2. Marriage revokes an existing CPF nomination and a will (fact pack §1.6, §3.2). That alone justifies a review.
3. A review is not a sales meeting. Sometimes the right outcome is "no change needed". Record it.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Life-event triage (pairs).** The facilitator reads 5 short scenarios: new baby, divorce, job loss, home purchase, parent diagnosed. For each one, the adviser names the first 2 questions they would ask and the first document to check.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Showed empathy first
- [ ] Checked nominations where relevant
- [ ] Accepted "no change needed" as a valid outcome
- [ ] Kept to concepts, with no product talk

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many clients with recent life events will you contact for a review?*

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

**Observer checks:**

- [ ] Story focuses on the client, not on money earned
- [ ] Listener reflects back the value

## Week 33: Tue 17 Aug 2027: Cashflow and emergency savings concepts

**Theme:** Planning concepts  
**Why this week:** Protection plans fail when cashflow fails. Get the basics right first.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no client names. The facilitator records team totals only, never individual numbers by name, and does not rank people.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. Work out the emergency buffer from the client's *own* monthly expenses and job stability, not from a generic rule. Any rule of thumb is a starting conversation, not a figure to quote.
2. Pay yourself first: automate savings on payday, then spend what remains.
3. Only recommend premiums the client can sustain through a bad year. Ask what would happen if income stopped for a few months.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Cashflow conversation (threes).** Client persona: *"Jun, 27, first job, spends most of his pay, wants to start investing."* The adviser runs a cashflow-first conversation and recommends nothing.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Used the client's own numbers
- [ ] No generic figure quoted as fact
- [ ] Asked about affordability in a bad year
- [ ] No product named

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many fact-finds will include a complete cashflow section this week?*

## Week 34: Tue 24 Aug 2027: From review to referral

**Theme:** Referrals  
**Why this week:** A good review is the most natural moment to ask for a referral.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no client names. The facilitator records team totals only, never individual numbers by name, and does not rank people.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. Ask at the end of a review that went well: "Was today useful? Is there one person who'd value the same check?"
2. PDPA and DNC apply. The client asks their friend first, and you only contact someone who has agreed. Check the DNC register before sending marketing messages (client-care referral-ask).
3. Follow up on the thank-you: a handwritten note or message, whatever the outcome. Never share details about the referred person.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**End-of-review ask (pairs, 3 rounds).** Round 1: the client says yes. Round 2: they're unsure. Round 3: they ask "what's in it for me?" The adviser responds without offering any inducement.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Asked only after a positive review
- [ ] Followed the consent-first process
- [ ] No inducement
- [ ] Thanked the client, whatever the answer

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *How many review-end referral asks will you make this week?*

## Week 35: Tue 31 Aug 2027: Energy, boundaries and sleep

**Theme:** Time management  
**Why this week:** Late Q3 fatigue is common. Protect your energy before Q4.

### 0–5 min · Wins and numbers

- Each person reports last week's appointments, fact-finds, cases submitted and reviews done. Use counts only, with no client names. The facilitator records team totals only, never individual numbers by name, and does not rank people.
- Share one win from last week. A learning or a client thank-you counts as a win.
- Read back last week's commitments: done, part done or not done. Do not shame anyone. Ask what got in the way.

### 5–15 min · Skill topic

1. Manage energy, not just time. Put demanding tasks like prospecting and fact-finds in your peak hours, and admin in the low ones.
2. Set response-time boundaries. Clients appreciate a clear "I reply within X working hours" more than midnight replies.
3. Sleep is a performance habit. Tired advisers make compliance mistakes.

### 15–25 min · Role-play or drill

**Energy audit (individual 4 min, pairs 6 min).** Each person marks last week's calendar blocks as high, medium or low energy. Their partner suggests one swap that puts a key task into a high-energy slot.

**Observer checks:**
- [ ] Identified peak hours
- [ ] Made one concrete swap
- [ ] Set one boundary, such as a reply window

### 25–30 min · Commitments

Each person states one commitment and the facilitator writes it down. Prompt: *What is your one energy swap and one boundary for this week?*

## 1. Who joins and why

### The pool is shrinking, and fastest among the young
MAS figures for **life and composite insurance agents** ([MAS parliamentary reply, 2 Apr 2024](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-replies/2024/pq-on-insurance-agents-in-labour-force)):

| Year | 21–30 | 31–40 | 41–50 | 51–60 | 60+ | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 4,735 | 4,499 | 2,472 | 2,127 | 1,372 | 15,205 |
| 2022 | 4,101 | 4,443 | 2,471 | 2,102 | 1,429 | 14,546 |
| 2023 | 3,773 | 4,207 | 2,428 | 2,048 | 1,478 | 13,934 |

- The **21–30 band fell 20%** in two years (4,735 to 3,773). It peaked at 6,031 in 2018 ([MAS reply, 5 Apr 2021](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/parliamentary-replies/2021/reply-to-parliamentary-question-on-statistics-on-insurance-agents)). Only the 60+ band grew.
- "There is an even distribution of male and female agents." "About 90% of the agents are Singapore citizens." MAS doesn't separate full-time from part-time agents and doesn't collect education data (same 2024 reply).
- LIA counts of reps holding exclusive tied-agency contracts: **13,771** at end-2023 ([LIA](https://www.lia.org.sg/media/4141/20240215_lia-4q2023-results_media-release.pdf)), **12,767** at end-2024 ([LIA](https://lia.org.sg/media/4458/20250213_lia-4q2024-results_media-release.pdf)), **12,197** at mid-2025 ([LIA](https://www.lia.org.sg/media/4643/20250813_lia-1h2025-results_media-release.pdf)), and **12,389** at end-2025 ([LIA](https://www.lia.org.sg/media/4789/20260211_lia-4q2025-results_media-release.pdf)). That is about a 10% fall over two years, with a small recovery in late 2025. The 2026 LIA releases don't give this count.

### Business is moving to FA firms
By weighted new-business premium in FY2025, FA representatives brought in **35.7%**, bank reps **32.9%** and tied reps **28.2%** ([LIA 4Q2025](https://www.lia.org.sg/media/4789/20260211_lia-4q2025-results_media-release.pdf)). In 1H 2026 the shares were 37.3% FA and 25.8% tied ([LIA 1H2026](https://www.lia.org.sg/media/4950/20260812_lia-1h2026-results_media-release.pdf)). Recruits now have more choice of platform than before, and FA firms compete hard for them.

### Career switchers vs fresh graduates
- **No industry-wide split has been published.** The big insurers target both groups. Prudential's Management Associate Programme targets "fresh university or polytechnic graduates and young professionals" and features a 30-year-old career switcher from finance operations ([Prudential, Mar 2024](https://www.prudential.com.sg/about/newsroom/press-release/2024/prudential-ramps-up-hiring)). Income recruited "fresh graduates and mid-career job seekers" with "little to no financial advisory experience" ([Income, Jan 2021](https://www.income.com.sg/about-us/corporate-information/press-releases/ntuc-income-opens-up-1,200-job-opportunities-with)).
- **Mid-career supply in 2027:** in finance and insurance, retrenchments rose "from 510 in Q4 2025 to 560 in Q1 2026" while the resignation rate "fell to 0.6%" ([Insurance Business, 2026](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/asia/news/breaking-news/singapore-insurance-sector-posts-recordlow-resignation-rate-amid-restructuring-wave-579479.aspx)). Few people are leaving jobs voluntarily, so likely switchers are mostly the retrenched and people who feel stuck. **(Inference, not a measured figure.)**

### Why people join
No Singapore survey of motives was found. Recruiters' own copy leans on flexibility, being your own boss and income without a cap, e.g. "more control over your time and income" ([job ad](https://jobleads.com/sg/job/independent-financial-advisor-representative-mid-career-switch-opportunity--singapore--e162e938caeaadd146c3c5a30dfaa3548)). Ray Alliance's JD takes the opposite line ("not suitable for individuals looking for quick commissions"), which suggests some firms see a market for recruits who are put off by hype. **The reasons people join are untested assumptions. careerdna.sg can measure them directly** (see recommendations).

## 2. What new representatives earn in years 1–2, and how agencies present it

### Hard data is thin
- **No published median first-year income** for new Singapore reps was found.
- **MOM Occupational Wages, June 2022:** "insurance sales agent/broker (including independent financial planner)" in the insurance industry had a median gross of **S$5,050 a month** (basic S$4,062) ([data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_43a37d383fe7b33368f1a3465467571d/view)). **Caveat:** the survey covers *employees* of firms with 25 or more staff, and basic wage excludes commissions. Commission-only tied agents are self-employed and mostly not counted. **Don't use this as a new-rep benchmark.**
- **Self-reported or unsourced figures:** Payscale has 3 responses (2018) for a Prudential Financial Consultant ([Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/research/SG/Job=Financial_Consultant/Salary/5a6a1141/Prudential)), which is too few to use. A careers blog gives S$24k–54k a year in years 1–2 with no source ([SkillUp](https://www.skillup.sg/blog/financial-planner-salary-singapore)). **(Estimate, low confidence.)**

### How agencies present it: allowances, not earnings

|---|---|---|

| Income SMA (2021) | "starting monthly base remuneration of S$2,900", "18-month earn-as-you-learn programme" | [Income](https://www.income.com.sg/about-us/corporate-information/press-releases/ntuc-income-opens-up-1,200-job-opportunities-with) |
| AIA Entrepreneur Power Scheme (job ad, undated) | "monthly allowance of up to 24 months upon meeting the required validation" | [job ad](https://sg.epicareer.com/job/24785990-financial-services-professional) |

The pattern: an "up to" allowance figure, conditional on hitting production targets, over 18–36 months. ### What the market's offers have in common
- **Exam sponsorship plus a small upfront allowance** (about $700–$2,700 at Income) is the floor.
- **Large guaranteed or performance-linked monthly allowances** come from the big insurers, run over 2–3 years, and are tied to targets.
- **Named mentoring and a first-year roadmap** are standard claims. A small team can make these concrete in a way big agencies can't.

**Channel quality matters more than volume.** LIMRA (US) found "only 15% of financial professionals in agency systems that recruit mainly inexperienced individuals" stayed, and "the majority of those left within their first two years" ([InsuranceNewsNet, 1 Mar 2023](https://insurancenewsnet.com/innarticle/5-key-factors-that-help-retain-new-financial-professionals)). That is US data, not Singapore.

### Internships: the regulatory line
Unlicensed interns must not give financial advice. MAS's requirements "aim to ensure that such services are only provided by fit and proper and adequately qualified persons", and it has banned unappointed individuals for doing so: 3–5-year bans in the 2023 sub-agency case ([MAS, 2023](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/enforcement/enforcement-actions/2023/mas-bans-four-former-representatives-and-one-other-individual-for-unlawful-sub-agency-activities)). Interns can shadow, research and run events. They should not quote, recommend or close. Universities also expect written key employment terms, and student-pass holders are limited to 16 hours a week during term ([NUS guidelines](https://fass.nus.edu.sg/cnm/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2020/06/internship-guidelines-for-employers.pdf)).

### Recruitment practices regulators have penalised
- **Supervision caps:** On 19 Sep 2025 MAS fined Singlife FA **$93,750** for "failing to implement effective policies relating to recruitment of representatives". Managers "recruit[ed] more Tier 1 representatives than they were allowed to supervise" and "parked" the excess under others ([MAS, 19 Sep 2025](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/enforcement/enforcement-actions/2025/mas-issues-composition-against-singlife-fa-pte-ltd-and-takes-reg-action-against-8-individuals)). **Size your intake to what you can supervise.**
- **Upfront incentives:** MAS warned that mass-recruitment incentives "increase the risk of FA representatives engaging in aggressive sales tactics" and proposed spreading sign-on incentives "over a minimum period of 6 years" ([MAS, 2018](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2018/promoting-responsible-recruitment-in-the-financial-advisory-industry)).

## 6. Retention benchmarks

**Singapore: no published 90-day, 13-month or 1-year agent retention figure** was found from MAS, LIA or any insurer. Prudential's own view is that reps who "persist for at least two years are more likely to stay" ([Prudential, 2024](https://www.prudential.com.sg/about/newsroom/press-release/2024/prudential-ramps-up-hiring)).

**Regional and global benchmarks (not Singapore):**
| Market | Figure | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| South Korea, 1H 2021 | 13-month agent retention averaged **41.5%** at 20 life insurers and **57.6%** at non-life insurers | [Asia Economy, citing FSS](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2021120806572644664) | Verified |
| US (LIMRA, 2020 data) | "only 15%" of new reps in agencies recruiting mainly inexperienced people stayed; "the majority of those left within their first two years" | [InsuranceNewsNet, 2023](https://insurancenewsnet.com/innarticle/5-key-factors-that-help-retain-new-financial-professionals) | Verified (secondary) |
| Canada (LIMRA, 2011) | 4-year retention 30% overall: 45% for experienced recruits, 29% for inexperienced | [Insurance Portal](https://insurance-portal.ca/article/retention-rates-frozen/) | Search snippet only |
| Hong Kong | 62% of new licences still active; >50% chance of quitting before the first 3-year licence ends | [HK Insurance Authority](https://www.ia.org.hk/en/legislative_framework/Conduct_in_Focus_Issue_10_03.html) | Unverified (page blocked) |

No reliable 90-day benchmark exists; "30% leave in 90 days" figures online have no source. **Planning estimate for Dream Planners:** assume about 40–60% of contracted recruits are still active at 13 months, which is the Korean range. Set your own baseline from your first cohort. **(Estimate.)**

**Regulatory angle:** MAS's [Dec 2025 thematic review of recruitment and onboarding](https://www.mas.gov.sg/publications/monographs-or-information-paper/2025/thematic-review-of-financial-institutions-recruitment-and-onboarding-training-of-representatives) expects "structured recruitment policies and processes, approved by senior management" and competency assessment before reps advise ([Linklaters summary](https://financialregulation.linklaters.com/post/102m25m/singapore-mas-sets-out-expectations-for-recruitment-and-onboarding-of-financial)). Retention problems that come from weak onboarding are now a supervisory issue, not just a business one.

# An Honest Career Preview: Your First Year as a Financial Representative

*Dream Planners · for candidates considering the 2027 intake · DRAFT pending Brand Guardian and principal compliance approval*

**Read this before you decide.** We'd rather you say no now than find out in month four that the job wasn't what you expected.

### What the job is
You help people protect their families, plan for health costs and save for retirement. You find out what they need, recommend only what fits (sometimes nothing), explain the downsides, and keep records. It's part advice, part admin, part constant learning.

### Before you can advise anyone
1. Be **at least 21** at appointment and hold **A Levels (3 H2 + 2 H1), an IB Diploma, a polytechnic diploma, a degree or equivalent** (MAS rule). [S5]
2. Pass the CMFAS exams: **RES5, M9, M9A**, plus **HI** for health insurance. [S11] RES5 is 150 questions in 3 hours, and Part II needs **80%**. [S12]
3. Pass fit and proper checks and be appointed; your name then appears on the **MAS register**. [S16][S6]
4. After that: at least **30 hours of structured CPD every year**. [S11]

Until step 3, you can study and practise, but you **cannot advise anyone**, not even family.

### What the first year really looks like
| When | What usually happens |
|---|---|
| Months 1–2 | Study, exams (and maybe a resit), training, role-play. Little or no client work. |
| Months 2–3 | First client meetings with your mentor present. Lots of "not now". |
| Months 4–12 | Building a client base, mostly through people you know and their referrals. Some months are quiet. |

- **Income is commission-based and variable.** It's not guaranteed and it can be uneven month to month. We don't publish earnings examples, because any single figure would mislead you.
- **Not everyone stays.** No Singapore figure is published. Overseas regulator data suggests a large share of new agents leave within about a year (e.g. ~41.5% 13-month retention at South Korean life insurers in 2021, not Singapore data). [S24]
- **Rejection is normal**, and so are admin and compliance checks.

**Reimbursement:** whether exam fees are reimbursed, and on what conditions (including any repayment if you leave early), depends on the principal's current written scheme. **You'll get those terms in writing before you commit.** Don't rely on anything said verbally. [S10]

### What Dream Planners gives you
- A **named mentor** and weekly one-to-one coaching
- A **structured 90-day plan** linked to your exams and our training pack (conduct rules, fact-find, CPF and national schemes, objections, referrals, routine)
- **Role-play** before you meet real clients, and your mentor in the room for your first meetings
- Check-ins at **Day 7, 30, 60 and 90** about skills, habits, quality and wellbeing, **never income targets**, with someone without recruitment targets involved [S9]
- A small intake, sized to what we can properly supervise [S8]
- Permission to decide it's not for you, without pressure

### This is probably not for you right now if…
You need a fixed monthly salary in the next 12 months · you want fast money · you dislike rules and paperwork · someone is pressuring you to join.

### Questions to ask us (and any other agency)
1. Can I see the written terms for exam reimbursement, any allowance and any clawback?
2. Who is my mentor, and how many new people do they supervise?
3. What happens if I fail RES5 twice?
4. How do you check the quality of my advice?
5. What did people who left in their first year say?

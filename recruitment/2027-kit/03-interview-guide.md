# Structured Interview Guide (12 Questions)

**For:** interview panels for the 2027 intakes
**Format:** 60 minutes, two interviewers. **At least one interviewer must not have sales or recruitment targets** (LIA Good Recruitment Practices). [S9]
**Record:** one scorecard per interviewer (`04-candidate-scorecard.md`), scored independently before discussing.
**Status:** DRAFT for Heron and compliance review.

## Rules for interviewers
1. Ask all 12 questions, in order, to every candidate. Same wording. Follow-up probes are listed; don't invent new topics.
2. **Don't ask** about age (beyond confirming the candidate will meet the MAS age-21 requirement by the planned appointment date) [S5], marital status, pregnancy or family plans, religion, race, nationality beyond legal work eligibility, health, or how many people the candidate "knows". [S4][S18]
3. **Don't** ask for a list of names of friends or family ("warm list") at interview, and don't count contacts as a selection criterion.
4. **Don't** quote or imply any income. If asked, give the approved answer: "Income is commission-based and variable. We'll take you through the principal's written terms."
5. Fit and proper (honesty, financial soundness, competence) is checked through the principal's formal declaration and checks, **not** by probing personal finances at interview. [S16]
6. Score against the anchors below, not against "gut feel". Write one line of evidence for every score.

## Scoring scale (applies to every question)

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | No relevant evidence, or answer raises a concern |
| 2 | Weak: vague or generic, little personal example |
| 3 | Adequate: a relevant example with some detail |
| 4 | Strong: specific example, clear actions and outcome, some reflection |
| 5 | Excellent: several specific examples, strong reflection, clearly applies to this role |

Each question below gives what a **1**, **3** and **5** look like. 2 and 4 sit in between.

## The 12 questions

### Section A: Motivation and realistic expectations

**Q1. Why financial planning, and why now?**
- Probe: What else are you considering? What would make you decide this isn't for you?
- 1: "Money", "be my own boss", or can't say. 3: Interest in helping people with money plus some understanding of the role. 5: Clear, client-centred reason linked to own experience; has weighed alternatives.
- 🟢 Mentions a real experience with financial decisions (own or family's). 🔴 Focuses on income speed, "passive income", or recruiting others.

**Q2. What do you think the first 12 months in this role will look like?**
- Probe: How will you handle months with little or no commission?
- 1: Expects quick success. 3: Knows there's study and building time. 5: Has read the honest career preview, describes exams, supervised practice, variable income and a plan for runway.
- 🟢 Has a realistic financial runway plan (no need to disclose amounts). 🔴 "I'll make it work somehow" with no plan, or says they need a fixed monthly income immediately.

**Q3. Tell us about a time you committed to learning something difficult over several weeks.**
- Probe: How did you plan it? What happened when it got hard?
- 1: No example. 3: Example with a basic plan. 5: Structured plan, adapted when stuck, finished.
- Links to RES5 (Part II needs 80%). [S12]

### Section B: Client focus and integrity

**Q4. Describe a time you told someone something they didn't want to hear because it was the right thing to do.**
- 1: No example, or avoided it. 3: Example, handled reasonably. 5: Handled with care, explained reasons, kept the relationship.
- 🟢 Puts the other person's interest first. 🔴 Story ends with "I just went along with it" or bending a rule.

**Q5. Scenario: a friend says, "Just sell me whatever you need to hit your target this month." What do you do?**
- 1: Accepts. 3: Declines, vaguely. 5: Declines, explains needs-based advice, offers to do a proper fact-find later (after appointment) or says no product may be right.
- 🔴 Any answer that accepts selling unneeded products. This is a compliance red flag.

**Q6. Tell us about a time you made a mistake at work or study that affected someone else.**
- 1: Can't name one, or blames others. 3: Names it and fixed it. 5: Owns it, fixed it, changed a process so it didn't recur, told the right people promptly.
- 🟢 Owns mistakes and escalates early. 🔴 Hides or minimises.

**Q7. Scenario: before you're appointed, an uncle asks which policy he should buy. What do you say?**
- 1: Gives a recommendation. 3: Says they can't advise yet. 5: Explains they can't advise until appointed and on the MAS register, and suggests he speak to an appointed representative. [S6][S7]
- 🔴 Any advice or product comparison before appointment.

### Section C: Resilience and self-management

**Q8. Tell us about a time you faced repeated rejection or setbacks. What kept you going?**
- 1: Gave up quickly, no reflection. 3: Persisted with some strategy. 5: Persisted, reflected, changed approach, kept wellbeing in check.
- 🟢 Healthy coping, asks for help. 🔴 Describes burnout patterns as normal, or anger at the people who said no.

**Q9. How do you plan a week when nobody is telling you what to do?**
- Probe: Show us how you'd use a calendar. (Links to training Module F.)
- 1: No method. 3: Some routine. 5: Clear system, time-blocks, tracks and reviews.

**Q10. What would you do if, three months in, you were finding the work much harder than expected?**
- 1: Quit without talking to anyone, or "push harder" only. 3: Talk to mentor. 5: Talk to mentor early, review what's working, considers whether the role still fits, without shame.
- 🟢 Open to deciding it's not a fit. 🔴 "I'd never give up" with sunk-cost tone (risk of pressure selling).

### Section D: Communication and learning

**Q11. Explain something complicated you know well to us as if we're new to it. (2 minutes)**
- Any topic, not finance-specific.
- 1: Unclear, jargon-heavy. 3: Clear enough. 5: Clear, checks understanding, uses a simple example, invites questions.

**Q12. What questions do you have for us?**
- 1: None. 3: Practical questions (training schedule, mentor). 5: Probing questions about supervision, compliance, support, how the team handles mistakes, written terms.
- 🟢 Asks to see written terms, the clawback conditions, or how quality is checked. 🔴 Only asks about income ranges or how fast they can recruit others.

## Red and green flags summary

| 🟢 Green flags | 🔴 Red flags |
|---|---|
| Client-first reasons for joining | Income-first or "get rich" motives |
| Realistic about variable income; has a runway plan | Needs fixed income now, no plan |
| Owns mistakes, escalates early | Blames others, hides problems |
| Clearly refuses to advise before appointment | Willing to "help friends" before appointment |
| Refuses to sell unneeded products | Would sell to hit a target |
| Asks about supervision, training, written terms | Only asks about income or recruiting others |
| Comfortable deciding the role isn't for them | Pressured by family or others to join |
| Steady, structured study habits | No plan for exams |

**Any 🔴 on Q5 or Q7 = do not proceed** without a second interview led by the person without recruitment targets, and a note to the principal's compliance team if appropriate.

## Decision guide (out of 60)

| Total | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| 48–60 and no 🔴 on Q5/Q7 | Proceed to offer discussion (written terms, honest career preview signed as read) |
| 36–47 | Second conversation; agree specific gaps to test |
| Below 36, or any score of 1 on Q4–Q7 | Do not proceed this intake; thank the candidate and offer feedback |

Final decisions also depend on: the principal's fit and proper checks [S16], MAS minimums [S5], and **supervision capacity** (don't exceed what the team can supervise) [S8].

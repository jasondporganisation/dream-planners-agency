# Career Fair / Campus Talk: 20 Minutes

**Title on the poster:** "Is a career in financial planning right for you? An honest 20 minutes"
**Audience:** final-year students, recent graduates, faculty society members (mixed ages; many under 21)
**Speakers:** one presenter (an appointed representative or team leader) plus, if possible, a second person without recruitment targets for Q&A [S9]
**Materials:** 8 slides, printed one-page honest career preview (`06-honest-career-preview.md`), QR code to book a career conversation (consent notice on the booking form)
**Status:** DRAFT. Slides and handout need Brand Guardian approval. Booth and talk must also follow the university's employer rules.

## Ground rules for the speaker
- No income figures, no "my best month", no lifestyle photos (cars, holidays). No "unlimited income". [S1][S3]
- Don't collect names or contacts by hand. Interested students use the QR booking form, which has the PDPA consent notice.
- Don't give financial advice or comment on anyone's policies, even if asked. Say: "I can't advise here, but I can show you how the career works."
- Don't ask anyone's age. When eligibility comes up, state the rule: 21 at appointment, per MAS. [S5]

## Slides and speaker notes

### Slide 1: Opening (2 min)
**On slide:** "An honest 20 minutes about financial planning careers." Team name, principal logo per approved rules.

**Speaker notes:**
- "Thanks for coming. I'm [first name], a representative with Dream Planners. I'm not going to tell you how much you'll earn, because honestly it varies hugely and I can't predict it for you. What I can do is tell you what the job is, what it costs to start, and what the first year is really like."
- "If by the end you think it's not for you, that's a good outcome too."
- Quick show of hands: "Who has a parent or relative who's had a call from an insurance agent?" (Light, builds rapport; don't ask for details.)

### Slide 2: What the job is (3 min)
**On slide:** Understand → Analyse → Recommend → Review. Protection · Health · Savings · Retirement.

**Speaker notes:**
- Walk through one anonymised, made-up example: "A couple in their thirties with a new baby. We look at what happens to the family if one income stops, how hospital bills are covered, and what CPF already does for them. Sometimes the right answer is 'you're already covered, don't buy anything'."
- Stress the fact-find and needs-based advice. Mention that national schemes (CPF, MediShield Life, CareShield Life) are part of the job, linked to Module E of our training pack.
- The job is also: admin, compliance records, following up, and constantly learning.

### Slide 3: The licensing path (3 min)
**On slide:**
1. Meet MAS minimums: 21+ at appointment; A Level / IB / poly diploma / degree [S5]
2. Pass CMFAS: RES5, M9, M9A (+ HI for health insurance) at the Singapore College of Insurance [S11][S12–S15]
3. Fit and proper checks, then appointment by the principal; name appears on the MAS register [S16][S6]
4. Then, and only then, advise clients. 30 hours of CPD every year after that [S11]

**Speaker notes:**
- "Before step 4, nobody advises anyone, not even family. MAS has banned people for advising without being appointed." [S7]
- "RES5 is the big one: 150 questions, 3 hours, and Part II needs 80% to pass. Plan real study time." [S12]
- "If you're under 21 you can still intern with us in a non-advising role, and start studying, but you can't be appointed until you're 21."

### Slide 4: A realistic first year (3 min)
**On slide:** Months 1–2: study and exams · Months 2–3: role-play, first supervised meetings · Months 4–12: building a client base, mostly through people you know and referrals

**Speaker notes:**
- "Most of your early weeks are learning. Then you start meeting people with your mentor in the room."
- "Rejection is normal. Many people you approach will say no or not now, and that's fine."
- "Some people leave in the first year. We don't have a Singapore figure for how many; one overseas regulator's data shows a large share of new agents don't stay past 13 months." Optional: "One Korean regulator figure put 13-month retention at life insurers at about 41.5% in 2021. That's not Singapore, but it shows the pattern." [S24]
- "That's why we'd rather you decide carefully now."

### Slide 5: Costs and how income works (2.5 min)
**On slide:** CMFAS exam fees (SCI, non-member, weekday, first attempts): RES5 S$218.00 · M9 S$130.80 · M9A S$130.80 · HI S$109.00 · Total S$588.60 [S12–S15]. Resits cost the same again. Income: commission-based, variable, not guaranteed.

**Speaker notes:**
- "Whether these fees are reimbursed, and on what conditions, depends on the principal's current scheme. We'll show you the written terms before you commit." (Do not state any reimbursement or allowance amount unless the principal has confirmed it in writing and Brand Guardian approved the wording.)
- Other costs to think about: transport to meetings, phone, time without a fixed salary, and possibly your own professional development.
- "Income comes from commission when clients buy something suitable. Some months are slow. We don't publish earnings examples because they'd mislead you."
- If asked "how much do people earn?": "It genuinely varies, and I'm not allowed to make promises. In a career conversation we can walk through the principal's written terms with you."

### Slide 6: Who does well, who doesn't (2.5 min)
**On slide:** Does well: curious, disciplined, likes people, OK with rejection, honest when it costs a sale. Struggles: wants a fixed salary now, wants quick money, dislikes admin and rules.

**Speaker notes:**
- "If you need a steady monthly salary in the next year, this may not be the right time, and that's OK."
- "The people who last usually care about getting it right for the client more than closing."

### Slide 7: Ways in (1.5 min)
**On slide:** Graduate track (21+ at appointment) · Non-advising internship (shadow, research, events, case study) · Free 20-minute career conversation

**Speaker notes:**
- Internship: "You'll shadow (with client consent), research and help run events. You won't advise or sell." Written terms, and student-pass holders are limited to 16 hours a week in term. [S23]
- Mention the 90-day onboarding plan and the named mentor.

### Slide 8: Q&A and next step (2.5 min)
**On slide:** QR to book a free career conversation · "Take the one-page career preview home"

**Speaker notes:**
- Prepared answers:
  - *"Is it MLM?"* "No. Representatives are appointed by a licensed insurer and must be on the MAS register before advising. Nobody earns anything just for bringing in a recruit; a representative's income comes from advising clients. If you'd like to know how team leaders are paid, we'll explain it from the principal's written terms." (Do not deny that leaders may earn on their team's business if the principal's scheme provides for it; confirm the wording with the principal before use.)
  - *"Do I have to sell to my friends?"* "Many people start with people they know, but you never have to pressure anyone, and 'no' is a fine answer."
  - *"Can I do this part-time?"* "Ask us in the career conversation; it depends on the principal's rules."
- Close: "Thanks. If it's not for you, I hope you now know a bit more about how financial planning works."
